\noindent\textbf{
Tandem mass spectra are incomplete observations of the gas-phase chemistry that generates them, yet molecular structure elucidation is usually learned as a direct spectrum-to-structure mapping. Here we present ORBIT-MS, a World--Generator--Verifier framework that separates molecular proposal from executable fragmentation evidence. A spectrum-conditioned Generator constructs molecular hypotheses; for each candidate and observed fragment peak, a Verifier proposes coherent one- or two-electron redistribution events, while a spectrum-blind gas-phase World scores the same event traces from molecular structure and acquisition context alone. A deterministic bond--electron executor applies each event and materializes explicit charged products, allowing inverse-generation confidence to be combined with importance-corrected forward fragmentation evidence. Under molecule-disjoint development, the standard no-ground-truth-formula Generator reaches \result{final no-formula Exact@1/10 and structural-similarity result}; the spectrum-blind World reaches \result{final cosine/Jensen--Shannon and retrieval Hit@K result}; and matched WGV reranking changes \result{Exact@1/10 WGV gain on a frozen candidate pool}. This decomposition makes proposal failure, forward-fragmentation failure and evidence-ranking failure separately measurable rather than collapsing them into one score. We use the resulting evidence hierarchy to assign \result{dark spectrum count} previously unannotated spectra to the strongest supported structural statement, yielding \result{recurrent entity/family count} recurrent molecular entities or families with \result{headline structural/biological result}. ORBIT-MS therefore treats a molecular prediction as a chemical hypothesis that must survive executable gas-phase evidence, providing a route from uncertain tandem spectra to auditable structural and biological inference.
}

\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is the principal technology for surveying small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Yet most tandem mass spectra generated by untargeted studies remain without a confident structural assignment.\cite{daSilva2015,Alseekh2021} Recent advances in spectral retrieval, molecular networking, formula and fingerprint prediction, learned spectrum models and de novo structure generation have substantially improved the hypotheses that can be proposed for a spectrum.\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Wang2021CFMID,Goldman2023,Bohde2025,Qiang2026DeepMet} The dominant abstraction, however, remains a mapping between an observed spectrum and an answer: a score, a candidate rank, a fingerprint, or a molecular structure.

A tandem mass spectrum is not the reaction process itself. It is a partial observation of an ensemble of gas-phase events that depend on molecular structure, ionization state, collision conditions, competing pathways and instrument response. A fragment absent from an acquired spectrum is therefore not automatically chemically impossible, just as a peak present in the spectrum does not by itself specify the unique trajectory that produced it. Treating the spectrum as the complete target obscures this latent reaction process and makes forward prediction, mechanistic explanation and inverse structure inference separate problems that need not agree with one another.

We take a different view. For a molecule \(M\) under experimental condition \(c\), we define a latent molecular fragmentation world \(\mathcal{W}(M,c)\): a probabilistic, multi-branch reaction network over ionization microstates, electron-flow events, charged and neutral products, hydrogen transfer, branch competition and termination. The observed spectrum \(S_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is generated through an observation process acting on this world,
\[
M,c \rightarrow \mathcal{W}(M,c) \rightarrow S_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
This formulation separates chemical possibility from experimental visibility and makes the latent reaction world, rather than a vector of spectral bins, the shared object of inference.

ORBIT-MS implements this idea as a World--Generator--Verifier (WGV) system with an explicit chemical executor. The Generator \(G\) proposes molecular hypotheses from the observed spectrum by iterative, spectrum-conditioned graph construction. For a candidate molecule and an observed fragment peak, the Verifier \(V\) proposes a coherent electron-redistribution event conditioned on the measured spectrum, whereas the spectrum-blind World \(W\) scores the same event using only the molecular state and acquisition context. A deterministic bond--electron executor applies the proposed event and materializes explicit charged products, allowing candidate structures to be ranked by combining inverse-generation confidence with forward fragmentation evidence.

The three learned components are optimized under different supervision while sharing the same executable chemistry. \(G\) is trained primarily from paired spectrum--precursor structures compiled into factorized graph-construction actions. \(V\) is optimized online with a group-relative policy-gradient objective whose rewards are computed only after exact event execution and comparison with a selected observed peak. \(W\) is trained by replaying executor-supported \(V\) traces without access to the measured fragment spectrum. Hard masks and the executor, rather than learned rewards, enforce electron availability, shell and bond-capacity constraints, molecular inventory and graph-construction validity. Canonical molecular generation uses independent ancestral samples rather than beam search, particle filtering or resampling.

This shared World also changes the form of structural evidence. A candidate can progress from mass or formula compatibility to chemical validity, World reachability, executable path support, candidate-specific discriminative evidence and bidirectional spectrum--structure consistency. Because stronger evidence can be evaluated against known structures and calibrated prospectively, the output need not collapse every spectrum to an unjustified rank-1 identity. Instead, the system reports the strongest bounded structural statement supported by the available evidence.

We use this capability to address the dark metabolome. Public repositories contain enormous numbers of unannotated spectra,\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Bushuiev2026Dreams} but their chemistry is usually analysed as anonymous features or spectral clusters. Once dark spectra can be moved to explicit evidence levels and bounded molecular structures, they can instead be counted as recurrent chemical entities, organized into structural families, related to characterized metabolism, assigned perturbation-dependent biological context and compared as molecular objects across cohorts. The paper therefore proceeds from a fragmentation-world paradigm, through graded structural evidence, to large-scale analysis of dark chemistry and its biology.

\section*{ORBIT-MS couples molecular generation to executable fragmentation evidence}

ORBIT-MS separates three questions that are usually collapsed into one spectrum-to-structure score: which molecular structures should be proposed, which fragmentation events can explain a measured peak, and whether those events are intrinsically plausible for the candidate molecule. The Generator \(G\) addresses the first question by iterative spectrum-conditioned molecular graph construction. The Verifier \(V\) addresses the second by conditioning on a candidate, the observed spectrum and a selected peak. The spectrum-blind World \(W\) addresses the third by scoring the identical coherent-event trace without access to measured fragment peaks. All proposed events are passed through the same deterministic bond--electron executor before they can contribute evidence \figureonetodo.

The production fragmentation language is deliberately narrower than a free-form reaction generator. A coherent event is sampled autoregressively as a sequence of one- or two-electron transfers between atom or bond sites; local masks exclude immediate electron, shell and bond-capacity violations, and the executor must materialize the resulting state into explicit products. On the complete TRAIN-derived SEARCH\_DEV development split, the trained spectrum-conditioned verifier increased \result{final V strict peak-support endpoint} relative to its frozen initialization while maintaining \result{V materialization rate}; the spectrum-blind World trained from supported traces changed \result{final W forward-support endpoint}. These component metrics are used only to diagnose the fragmentation model; project-level performance is evaluated by standard spectrum simulation, retrieval and de novo generation.

Forward spectrum prediction provides the direct test of \(W\). Given only molecular structure and acquisition context, the World samples coherent events, executes them and aggregates charged products into a predicted fragment-mass distribution. On the current complete 8,401-spectrum SEARCH\_DEV development evaluation, the frozen World obtains cosine similarity 0.09935 and Jensen--Shannon similarity 0.09199 at the current sampling budget, with a median cosine of zero. The failure distribution is informative: 66.84\% of spectra have zero cosine, the World predicts 2.27 mass peaks per case on average compared with 38.83 observed peaks, and a substantial fraction of predicted intensity lies in low-\(m/z\) products absent from these spectra. We therefore treat these values as a measured forward-model baseline rather than evidence that fragmentation simulation is solved; the standard simulation metric, rather than internal event-support rates, remains the optimization target.

The molecular Generator is evaluated separately because a verifier cannot recover a structure that was never proposed. In the standard no-ground-truth-formula SEARCH\_DEV setting, recovered full-population predictions give Exact@1 of 131/8,401 (1.559\%), Exact@10 of 370/8,401 (4.404\%), and proposal Recall@100 of 464/8,401 (5.523\%). The correct formula is nevertheless present in the mass/adduct-derived proposal list for 8,140/8,401 cases (96.89\%), showing that formula availability alone does not explain the low structural recall. The gap is largest for complex molecules: among 2,110 targets with at least 30 heavy atoms, the correct formula is available for 1,930 cases but the correct connectivity appears among 100 generated samples in only five. These results identify precise molecular connectivity generation, rather than formula proposal or candidate-list emptiness, as the dominant current inverse-model bottleneck.

\section*{Mechanistic World evidence provides a separate test of molecular hypotheses}

For a completed candidate \(M\), WGV evaluates observed peaks without allowing the spectrum-conditioned verifier to become the chemistry authority. For each target peak, \(V\) proposes coherent events from \(q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c)\); the exact executor determines the charged products; and \(W\) replays the identical trace to obtain \(p_W(a\mid M,c)\) without observing the measured fragment spectrum. Candidate evidence therefore combines forward plausibility, correction for the observation-conditioned proposal and agreement between executed product masses and the target peak. Event evidence is aggregated over independently sampled traces and then across observed peaks using intensity-aware weights.

This decomposition is essential for interpreting end-to-end performance. Generator proposal Recall@K measures whether the correct connectivity is present at all, whereas matched WGV reranking measures whether fragmentation evidence moves the correct candidate within an unchanged generated pool. The primary comparison therefore freezes one \(G\) candidate set and evaluates \(G\)-only and \(G+\lambda E(M,S)\) rankings on exactly the same molecules; the zero-World setting must reproduce the original \(G\) ordering case by case. The complete matched no-formula WGV result remains \result{pending matched WGV Exact@1/10 result}, and the official mass-pool retrieval result remains \result{pending complete Hit@1/5/20 and MCES@1 result}; partial or smoke evaluations are not treated as scientific outcomes \figuretwotodo.

The observation-conditioned role of \(V\) is also tested directly. In a frozen 256-case spectrum-shuffle control, the precursor state, queried peak and random seed were held fixed while the remainder of the spectrum was replaced by a same-adduct peer spectrum after neutral-loss alignment. At a 32-target budget, matched spectra produced at least one strict-hit event for 43/256 molecules (16.80\%) and shuffled spectra for 40/256 (15.63\%); the paired difference of 1.17 percentage points had a bootstrap 95\% interval of \([-0.39,3.13]\) points. Replay log probabilities shift with the measured spectrum, but strict-hit separation is therefore not yet established at the declared development gate. This negative control localizes a current limitation to spectrum-conditioned evidence use rather than event materialization, which remains near 99\%.

Together, the current development results separate three bottlenecks that a single de novo accuracy number would obscure: \(G\) has limited exact-connectivity proposal recall despite broad formula support; \(W\) produces sparse and sometimes low-mass-biased forward spectra; and \(V\) uses spectral context at the probability level but has not yet shown strong strict-hit separation under the shuffle intervention. Subsequent model promotion is therefore tied to matched standard-task improvements rather than to component-only diagnostics.

\section*{Dark molecular families reveal biology hidden from feature-level metabolomics}

\section*{Discussion}

Mass-spectral structure elucidation is usually framed as a direct mapping problem: given an observed spectrum, predict the correct molecular structure. ORBIT-MS instead separates proposal from executable evidence. The Generator proposes candidate structures, the spectrum-conditioned Verifier searches for coherent electron-redistribution events that explain observed peaks, and the spectrum-blind World asks whether the same events are plausible from the candidate and acquisition context alone. A deterministic bond--electron executor sits between learned inference and chemical products. This architecture does not require the Generator, Verifier and World to be one neural model; it requires them to communicate through a common executable chemical state and event language.

The value of the fragmentation World is therefore not that every sampled electron path becomes a proven gas-phase mechanism. Local masks and exact execution enforce bookkeeping and materializability, whereas event propensity and mechanistic relevance remain learned and empirically evaluated. Multiple latent events may explain the same observed fragment, and a verifier conditioned on the observation can always propose evidence more easily than a free forward model. The \(p_W/q_V\) correction and exact product comparison are designed to separate these roles: \(V\) is an inference proposal, \(W\) is a spectrum-blind prior over executable events, and the executor determines what those events actually produce.

The current development results also show why this decomposition is useful even before end-to-end performance is mature. Formula availability is already high in the no-formula candidate lists, yet exact-connectivity proposal recall remains low, especially for large molecules. Conversely, free World simulation is chemically executable but spectrally sparse and biased toward low-mass products at the present sampling and training regime. Spectrum-conditioned \(V\) changes event probabilities, but the matched-versus-shuffled strict-hit control is not yet decisive. These are different failure modes and should not be hidden behind a single structure-ranking score.

This distinction changes how uncertainty can be reported. A candidate may be chemically valid yet absent from the Generator pool; it may be proposed but unsupported by the forward World; or it may survive because several latent fragmentation events remain observationally indistinguishable. ORBIT-MS therefore reports proposal coverage, executable fragmentation evidence and final ranking separately. The intended repository-scale evidence hierarchy builds on this decomposition: dark spectra are assigned the strongest bounded structural statement supported by the available evidence rather than being forced to a unique rank-1 identity.

If the standard WGV benchmarks improve after the remaining forward-World and generator bottlenecks are addressed, the framework provides a direct bridge from method to dark-metabolome science. Chemically related observations can then be collapsed into recurrent structural entities or bounded families, compared across independent repositories and associated with perturbation-dependent biological context without conflating model confidence with experimental identification. Authentic standards remain the highest evidence tier for the structures tested experimentally.

Important limitations remain. The bond--electron executor defines an executable approximation to supported gas-phase chemistry, not a complete quantum-mechanical mechanism model. Product hypotheses discovered by \(V\) are latent explanations rather than experimental fragment-structure labels. The measured spectrum is incomplete, and an unobserved generated fragment cannot generally be treated as chemically false. Current development metrics are obtained on a molecule-group-disjoint partition of the official training fold; official validation and test results remain sealed until the model and evaluation protocol are frozen. Repository-scale entity counts, perturbation dependence and family-level biological associations will introduce additional uncertainty that must be quantified separately.

The framework therefore changes the role of mass-spectral AI in a specific way: a generated molecule is treated as a hypothesis that must survive an executable forward chemical test. Whether this principle ultimately improves the standard de novo, retrieval and spectrum-simulation tasks is an empirical question, and the paper separates those benchmark outcomes from the internal diagnostics used to develop the World.

\subsection*{Molecular and spectral representations}
ORBIT-MS separates the representation used for molecular construction from the representation used for gas-phase electron redistribution. An experimental spectrum is represented as a variable-length set of peaks \(S=\{(m_i,I_i)\}\) together with precursor \(m/z\), adduct, ionization polarity and available acquisition metadata. Spectrum encoders operate on peak nodes and pairwise relations derived from normalized \(m/z\), intensity, pairwise mass differences and, when available, ordered parent--child MS\(^{n}\) transitions. For spectrum-blind World inference, acquisition context contains only precursor and acquisition variables and never measured fragment-peak features.

Gas-phase fragmentation is represented by a \emph{bond--electron} (BE) state. A BE state stores atom identities and atomic numbers together with a symmetric integer matrix whose diagonal entries are non-bonding valence electrons localized on an atom and whose off-diagonal entries are electrons shared between atom pairs. A conventional single, double or triple bond therefore contains two, four or six shared electrons, while odd occupancies allow radical intermediates without introducing a separate graph-edit language. Charged precursor roots are compiled from the candidate structure and adduct and are retained only when they materialize to the observed precursor mass and polarity.

\subsection*{Exact bond--electron chemical executor}
Learned policies never directly declare a fragment structure. Instead, they propose electron-redistribution events that are applied by a deterministic chemical executor. An electron site is either an atom or an occupied bond, and an elementary arrow transfers one or two electrons from a source site to a target atom or bond. Local masks exclude moves that lack source electrons or immediately exceed supported shell or bond-electron capacities, but they do not encode a finite catalogue of named fragmentation reactions.

For a parent BE state \(x\) and a proposed coherent event \(a\), the executor applies all electron transfers to obtain a new BE state,
\[
x' = T_{\mathrm{exec}}(x,a).
\]
It then checks exact electron bookkeeping and BE symmetry and attempts to materialize the resulting state as explicit chemical products. Successful materialization returns charged and neutral components with product SMILES, elemental formula, formal charge, exact \(m/z\) and the corresponding parent-atom indices. Failed bookkeeping or materialization remains a failed event rather than being repaired by local search. Mechanistic class labels, where recorded, are audit metadata and do not define event legality.

\subsection*{Coherent electron-event parameterization}
The canonical PR85 fragmentation policy generates one complete coherent electron event per Monte Carlo draw. An event is an autoregressive sequence of one- or two-electron arrows. For each arrow the policy selects an occupied source site, chooses the electron count, predicts whether the target is an atom or bond, selects the target atom and, for a bond target, its partner atom, and then predicts whether to continue the same coherent event or stop. The partially updated BE matrix is carried through the sequence, so electrons transferred into a site may be used as a later source within the same concerted event. A finite maximum number of arrows is used only as an engineering compute guard.

The canonical event path uses independent complete samples. It does not perform local top-\(k\) action selection, beam search, sequential Monte Carlo, effective-sample-size resampling, survivor duplication or fallback enumeration of reaction templates. The same event factorization is used by the spectrum-conditioned verifier and the spectrum-blind World; their conditioning information and learned parameters differ.

\subsection*{Peak-to-product posterior}
To provide a soft product-level hypothesis for a selected observed fragment peak \(p=(m_p,I_p)\), ORBIT-MS uses a peak-to-product posterior \(P2\). Given a candidate BE state, acquisition context and the measured spectrum, the posterior encodes the candidate and spectrum once and conditions a target-peak query on the candidate representation, the global spectrum representation, the embedding of the queried peak and numerical target-\(m/z\) features. It predicts a retention logit for every precursor atom and a charge distribution over \(\{-2,-1,+1,+2\}\). The retention probabilities define a soft precursor-atom set, and together with the charge distribution yield an expected product \(m/z\).

The product posterior is trained with weak composition and observed-charge supervision over the full training population and with stronger atom-set and charge supervision only when a TRAIN event is executed successfully and its product strictly supports the queried peak. These executor-supported products are treated as latent mechanistic hypotheses rather than experimentally observed fragment-structure labels. During verifier training the product posterior is frozen; its atom-retention probabilities, predicted fragment boundary, retention uncertainty and target-mass residual are converted into continuous intent features and injected into the event representation.

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned verifier}
The Verifier \(V\) is a peak-anchored posterior event policy,
\[
q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c),
\]
that proposes coherent electron events capable of explaining a selected observed peak. It combines a guided BE/spectrum encoding of the candidate with the product intent supplied by \(P2\). The measured spectrum affects the verifier through the spectrum encoder and target-peak query; it does not alter the executor or the local chemistry masks.

For each selected peak, \(V\) draws multiple complete events independently and executes each draw exactly once. A draw that cannot be materialized receives a negative reward. Materialized products receive a smooth reward determined by the distance between a same-polarity unit-charge product \(m/z\) and the target peak, with an additional intensity-weighted bonus for a strict mass match. Training uses a group-relative policy-gradient loss within the set of draws for the same target, increasing the probability of events with higher relative reward without treating any single latent mechanism as observed ground truth. Strictly supported products and their exact event traces are stored as TRAIN-only latent-event receipts for subsequent product-posterior refresh and World training.

\subsection*{Spectrum-blind gas-phase fragmentation World}
The free World \(W\) parameterizes
\[
p_W(a\mid M,c)
\]
with the same coherent-event factorization as \(V\) but without access to the measured fragment spectrum or a target peak. Its input is the candidate BE state and acquisition context only. During inference, independent World draws are executed by the same deterministic chemical executor to obtain charged products and a spectrum-blind distribution over fragment masses.

The current production World is trained by posterior-to-World trace replay. Exact-supported event traces discovered by \(V\) are grouped by training spectrum and target peak and replayed under \(W\); the loss is the negative log probability of the supported trace under the spectrum-blind policy, balanced across supported peaks. Cases without a replayable TRAIN trace do not contribute a trace-likelihood gradient. The canonical production objective does not use importance weights. This separation makes \(V\) an observation-conditioned proposal mechanism and \(W\) a spectrum-blind forward model over the same executable event language.

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned molecular generator}
The Generator \(G\) constructs precursor structures directly from the measured spectrum. Its spectrum encoder represents the MS/MS spectrum as a peak-relation graph with peak-node features and pairwise mass/intensity relations. The current molecular construction state is encoded by a graph neural network over atom identity, formal charge, radical state, local degree/valence and current bonds. A separate molecular-goal representation records the remaining elemental inventory or formula hypothesis. Graph--spectrum cross-attention injects the spectral representation into the current molecular graph, followed by message passing conditioned on the construction state, molecular goal and pooled spectrum context.

The construction policy is factorized rather than enumerating the full graph-edit action space. It predicts the action kind, element, formal charge, radical state, attachment site or atom pair and bond order, with hard masks enforcing remaining formula inventory, charge-aware valence, connectivity and legal emission. The primary paired-supervision stage compiles each known precursor structure into legal graph-construction teacher actions and minimizes their negative log likelihood. Optional posterior-distillation and World-reward stages are evaluated separately and are not part of the canonical paired generator unless explicitly stated.

For the standard no-formula de novo task, formula hypotheses are inferred from precursor mass, adduct and TRAIN-derived formula priors using only benchmark-permitted inputs. Ground-truth formula is reserved for the secondary formula-provided setting. At inference time the canonical generator produces independent ancestral molecular samples; it does not use beam search, top-\(k\) graph expansion, particle filtering or resampling. Invalid or unfinished trajectories remain failures in the denominator.

\subsection*{Integrated WGV evidence and candidate ranking}
For a completed molecular candidate \(M\) and an observed target peak \(p\), \(V\) is used as an inference proposal over coherent events while \(W\) scores the same event traces without seeing the measured fragment spectrum. For each sampled event
\[
a_k\sim q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c),
\]
the exact executor returns explicit charged products. The World replays the identical trace to obtain \(\log p_W(a_k\mid M,c)\), and the observation term is computed from the distance between the closest same-polarity unit-charge product and the target peak. With a fixed Gaussian mass kernel and a finite evidence floor, the event-level log evidence is
\[
\ell_k =
\log p_W(a_k\mid M,c)
-\log q_V(a_k\mid M,S,p,c)
+\log p_{\mathrm{obs}}(p\mid M,a_k),
\]
with an additional precursor-root log weight when multiple charged precursor roots are evaluated.

Event samples for one peak are combined with a log-mean-exp estimator,
\[
E_p(M,S)=\operatorname{logmeanexp}_k \ell_k.
\]
Peak-level evidence is then aggregated using square-root intensity weights,
\[
E(M,S)=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{\sqrt{I_p}}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{I_j}}\,
E_p(M,S),
\]
where \(\mathcal P\) is the selected set of eligible observed fragment peaks. This produces a candidate-specific World evidence score while correcting for the fact that \(V\) was allowed to condition on the observation. For matched molecular candidate pools, integrated WGV ranking combines the frozen Generator score with the World evidence,
\[
\operatorname{Score}(M)=
\operatorname{GScore}(M)+\lambda E(M,S),
\]
and the zero-World setting is required to reproduce the frozen Generator ordering exactly.

\subsection*{Spectrum simulation, molecular retrieval and benchmark protocol}
The forward World is also evaluated independently of the inverse generator. For spectrum simulation, molecular structure and benchmark-permitted acquisition context are supplied to \(W\), independent coherent events are sampled, and their executed charged products are aggregated into a predicted fragment-mass distribution. The measured spectrum is used only by the frozen evaluation metrics. For molecular retrieval, each candidate in the official MassSpecGym candidate universe is passed through the same spectrum-blind World to construct a World-generated fragment-mass fingerprint, which is then matched to the observed spectrum. Fixed bond-cut, neutral-loss or rearrangement rules are retained only as baselines or auxiliary diagnostics and are not the canonical retrieval backend.

Adaptive development uses only a molecule-group-disjoint partition of the official MassSpecGym training fold. The current split contains 175,548 ADAPTIVE\_TRAIN spectra from 20,430 molecule groups, 8,401 SEARCH\_DEV spectra from 1,135 molecule groups and 9,782 promotion-calibration spectra from 1,136 molecule groups; official validation and test folds remain sealed during adaptive development. The standard no-ground-truth-formula setting is primary wherever MassSpecGym defines one. De novo generation is evaluated with connectivity-level InChIKey-14 Exact@1/10 together with structural-similarity metrics and a fixed raw generation budget; retrieval uses the official mass-based candidate pool with Hit@1/5/20 and MCES, with the formula-filtered pool reported separately as a bonus setting; and spectrum simulation uses the official sparse cosine and Jensen--Shannon similarity implementations. Every evaluation spectrum and every failed generation remains in the denominator.
