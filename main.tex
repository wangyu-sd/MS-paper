\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is one of the principal technologies for discovering and characterizing small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Modern LC--MS/MS experiments can survey thousands of molecular features in a complex sample, but converting fragmentation spectra into chemical structures remains a central bottleneck. This problem persists even as metabolomics expands from single-cell measurements to population-scale studies: a recent Nature Perspective identified metabolite identification as a shared challenge across these otherwise distinct regimes and as a constraint on the construction of future metabolic atlases and foundation models.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Spectral-library matching provides strong evidence when an authentic reference has already been measured, yet libraries cover only a small fraction of the chemical diversity encountered in untargeted experiments.\cite{daSilva2015,Wang2016GNPS} Most measured spectra therefore lie somewhere between partial structural information and unresolved chemical identity.

Computational approaches have progressively improved the molecular hypotheses available for these spectra. Fragmentation trees, molecular fingerprints and formula-aware models enabled increasingly powerful database retrieval and chemical-class inference;\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Hoffmann2022,Xing2023,Goldman2023} forward models learned to reproduce spectra and local fragmentation behaviour from molecular structure;\cite{Wang2021CFMID,Young2024,Nowatzky2025} and de novo generators increasingly construct complete, chemically plausible molecular structures directly from spectra.\cite{Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} Most recently, DeepMet showed that chemical language models can anticipate previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites and combine a learned molecular prior with predicted MS/MS spectra to prioritize candidate structures in complex biological samples.\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet} At the same time, representation learning on millions of unlabelled spectra has expanded the scale at which chemical similarity can be organized.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams} Together, these advances have substantially improved the quality and breadth of the molecular hypotheses that can be brought to an unknown spectrum.

A better hypothesis, however, is not necessarily an easier hypothesis to verify. Contemporary structure-discovery workflows have already exposed this distinction: many computational strategies can associate structures with mass-spectrometric observations, yet determining which of several compatible structures is actually correct remains difficult.\cite{Gentry2024} An implausible decoy may be excluded by many obvious spectral inconsistencies, whereas a same-formula isomer sharing most local substructures may reproduce the dominant features of the same spectrum. This distinction separates three questions that are often treated as interchangeable. \emph{Spectral reproduction} asks whether a molecule can generate a spectrum resembling the observation. \emph{Chemical support} asks whether an observed fragment mass is structurally supported and, at higher authority, whether that support can be grounded in an auditable fragmentation path. \emph{Structural discrimination} asks whether that observation supports one molecular hypothesis more strongly than the relevant alternatives. A plausible molecular structure is therefore not an elucidated molecular structure: structure generation proposes hypotheses, whereas structure elucidation requires evidence that can falsify their competitors.

Here we develop ORBIT-MS around this distinction. We first define discriminative fragmentation evidence by separating fragment-mass support, competitor-relative specificity and atom-traceable chemical authority. We then show why this distinction becomes increasingly important as molecular hypotheses improve: close alternatives share more of the observable fragmentation support, causing fixed evidence systems to lose discriminative margin. ORBIT-MS uses experimentally grounded counterexamples to revise the executable evidence model under frozen controls and reports a unique structure only when the available evidence justifies that resolution. We finally apply this principle at metabolomics scale, converting previously dark spectra into an evidence-bounded molecular resource and asking whether the resulting structural organization exposes biological patterns that were inaccessible while the same features remained chemically unresolved.

\section*{Discriminative fragmentation evidence resolves molecular alternatives}

\section*{Better molecular hypotheses expose an evidence bottleneck}

Having established how structural evidence is defined, we next asked when such evidence becomes limiting. Contrary to the expectation that better molecular generators should monotonically simplify structure elucidation, structural discrimination became harder once incorrect candidates approached the true molecule. Across \result{METEOR generations and independent peer-reviewed external candidate sources}, the nearest incorrect candidates became progressively more similar to the ground truth, with increasing frequencies of same-formula, shared-scaffold and local-connectivity alternatives. Spectra that readily excluded early decoys therefore entered a different regime in which many of the dominant fragment masses were shared by the serious alternatives.

Metabolite structure elucidation consequently shifts from being primarily generation-limited to being increasingly evidence-limited.

\section*{Experimental spectra distinguish evidence from chemical plausibility}

A chemically plausible explanation is not necessarily experimental evidence. Structure-derived fragmentation rules can produce convincing mechanistic possibilities even when the measured spectrum is absent, and a structurally supported fragment mass may be shared by every candidate under consideration. We therefore asked how much of the apparent molecular explanation came from chemical prior and how much depended on the identity of the experimental spectrum.

\section*{Discussion}

Our results recast a central difficulty in tandem-mass-spectrometry metabolite structure elucidation. The field has made rapid progress in proposing molecular hypotheses---from formula and fingerprint inference to retrieval, spectrum simulation and complete molecular generation.\cite{Duhrkop2019,Hoffmann2022,Goldman2023,Xing2023,Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX,Qiang2026DeepMet} Recent Nature studies expose both sides of the resulting problem: chemical language models can now generate and prioritize previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites, while structure-discovery workflows continue to emphasize that computationally compatible structures require an independent path to establish which identity is correct.\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet,Gentry2024} ORBIT-MS addresses the adjudication step directly. It treats fragment-mass support, competitor-relative specificity and chemical authority as distinct layers and asks which observations actually change the relative support of plausible molecular hypotheses.

This distinction becomes increasingly important as hypothesis generation improves. An implausible alternative can be rejected by many spectral inconsistencies, whereas close same-formula candidates share much of the same fragment-mass support. Stronger generation therefore does not eliminate the elucidation problem; it changes its limiting step from \emph{can a plausible structure be proposed?} to \emph{what observation distinguishes it from other plausible structures?} The resulting evidence bottleneck is not resolved by maximizing explained intensity or theoretical fragment coverage. A forward model can reproduce much of a spectrum without establishing chemical identity; a candidate can support many observed masses that are also supported by every serious alternative; and an intense peak can carry little structural information if it is non-discriminative. Conversely, a weak peak can be decisive when its mass is candidate-specific, with atom-traceable chemistry supplying additional authority for how that support is interpreted.

The same framing makes adaptation scientifically interpretable. Fragmentation chemistry is incomplete, so absence of a high-authority explanation cannot automatically be treated as evidence that a molecule is wrong, and blindly adding fragmentation possibilities can increase non-specific matching. ORBIT-MS instead uses hard molecular alternatives as counterexamples that expose missing informative support, overgenerated shared support or insufficient chemical authority. Bounded revisions are retained only when they improve held-out discrimination while preserving frozen anchors. This converts reciprocal model development from an unconstrained self-improvement loop into a falsifiable revision process for an executable evidence model.

At metabolomics scale, the consequence is a different type of resource. A dark-metabolome map should not be a catalogue of forced predicted SMILES. It should preserve the resolution justified by each experiment: exact structure where discriminative evidence and orthogonal validation support it, bounded candidate sets where ambiguity remains, and lower-resolution molecular families where the spectrum cannot decide. Organizing dark features in this way makes structural uncertainty explicit while still allowing recurrent molecular families and transformation series to be analysed across biological samples. \result{Insert final resource-scale and biological-discovery synthesis.} Representative standard-based validation then anchors the biological interpretation without implying that every resource entry has the same level of structural certainty.

More broadly, metabolite identification remains a shared challenge even as the field scales towards cellular and population-level atlases and foundation models.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Increasing the number, diversity or plausibility of molecular hypotheses does not increase the information contained in a fixed experimental observation. Scientific elucidation additionally requires knowing which observations justify a proposal, which alternatives remain compatible with the data, and where the current evidence stops being sufficient. This principle creates a natural route from passive prediction to sequential hypothesis testing: unresolved alternatives define the additional fragment or measurement that would be most informative, and targeted MSn or collision-energy acquisition can then test that distinction directly. Reliable metabolomics therefore requires not only plausible molecular hypotheses, but explicit experimental evidence for why one hypothesis is supported over its alternatives and a scalable representation of the uncertainty that remains.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary separation}

The study is organized around three frozen objects at each evaluation point: a candidate-generation model, a candidate-specific evidence model and the experimental spectrum. The hypothesis model maps a spectrum and precursor constraints to a candidate set. The evidence model maps the same spectrum together with a candidate set to candidate-specific fragmentation evidence and ranking. The experimental spectrum is never described as ``evolving'' unless a new measurement is physically acquired.

All population-level analyses use molecule groups, rather than individual spectra, as the primary independent unit whenever repeated spectra correspond to the same molecular structure. Development, calibration and test partitions are molecule-group disjoint. Candidate pools used for causal comparisons are frozen before evaluation, and official test data are not used to authorize model or evidence-program changes.

A chemical mechanism is considered auditable only to the level represented by the deterministic trace. Fragment-mass compatibility and reaction-level authority are therefore recorded separately: a mass match can support a candidate at the fragment-mass layer, but it is not labelled as a verified reaction without an atom-mapped event. A chemistry-backend proposal is treated as possible under that backend, not as an empirical reaction probability. Unsupported peaks are not candidate contradictions unless the relevant fragment-generation recall has independently passed a preregistered reliability criterion.

\subsection*{Benchmark spectra, structure standardization and leakage control}

Primary supervised benchmarking uses MassSpecGym and \datatodo{additional peer-reviewed reference datasets selected for the final study}.\cite{MassSpecGym2024} We retain benchmark-provided train, validation and test roles where available. Molecular structures are standardized using \methodtodo{final canonicalization protocol}; repeated spectra associated with the same standardized two-dimensional connectivity are grouped before inference.

Structure-only and chemical-language pretraining corpora are audited against all frozen validation/test molecules. \subsection*{Candidate-specific fragmentation evidence}

The evidence model is explicitly layered. A lower-level fragment-mass representation records which observed masses are supported by a candidate under the frozen fragmentation model. Candidate specificity compares that support against the current competing structures. Atom-traceable trajectories provide a higher-authority chemical grounding for selected mass assignments; they are not required to treat every mass-compatible observation as a fully verified mechanism.

The pLSE reaction engine represents CID as a bounded reaction-state network. An ion state records the molecular graph, analytical adduct, charge carrier, formal-charge sites, proton or sodium state where represented, and radical state where represented. Candidate reaction centres are compiled without access to the target spectrum. Elementary operators emit atom-mapped products and co-products and record bond changes, atom/component lineage, formal-charge changes, hydrogen or hydride transfer where represented, product charge carrier, theoretical masses and source provenance.

For candidate \(M_j\) and peak \(p\), the eligible trajectory set \(\mathcal T(M_j,p)\) contains only trajectories whose terminal ions satisfy the frozen mass tolerance and trace-validity requirements. The production implementation records fragment-mass compatibility separately from the following high-authority trajectory score. Candidate-intrinsic high-authority support is
\[
s_{jp}=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal T(M_j,p)}Q(\tau,p),
\]
with \(Q\) defined without ground-truth identity. Candidate-relative peak specificity is
\[
d_{jp}=s_{jp}-\max_{k\neq j}s_{kp}.
\]
The target manuscript score is
\[
D(M_j\mid S,\mathcal C)=\sum_p w_p\phi(d_{jp})-\lambda\Omega(M_j),
\]
where \(\Omega(M_j)\) controls accidental matching opportunity and \(\phi\) retains only support justified by the frozen evidence policy. \methodtodo{Replace with the exact frozen production equation and explicitly report how fragment-mass compatibility, candidate specificity and trajectory authority are combined, including the assignment policy and all fixed coefficients or parameter-free terms.}

Observed peaks are assigned to theoretical fragments using \methodtodo{final global one-to-one assignment objective}. Mass tolerances are preregistered by dataset/instrument class. Safety-cap truncation and unavailable reaction families are recorded separately from chemical impossibility.

\subsection*{METEOR hypothesis model}

METEOR is the spectrum-conditioned molecular hypothesis model. The final implementation uses \result{final backbone, model size and checkpoint lineage}, with a chemistry-specific vocabulary, continuous token-mass state and peak-level spectrum conditioning. Experimental peaks are represented using absolute \(m/z\), precursor-relative \(m/z\), intensity and acquisition metadata where observed. \methodtodo{Freeze final direct-prefix/cross-attention interface and checkpoint identities.}

\subsection*{Chemical-World and Spectral-Reality controls}

Chemical-World and Spectral-Reality share the same fragment-reaction graph schema but differ in experimental authority. Chemical-World contains structure-derived fragmentation possibilities only. Spectral-Reality retains the same proposal graph and links it to observed peaks through a deterministic mass gate. A peak can remain compatible with multiple fragments; the training record therefore does not force a unique fragment identity when the spectrum does not support one.

Spectrum dependence is evaluated with correct-spectrum, no-spectrum and shuffled-spectrum conditions on identical candidate pools. Shuffled donors must come from different molecule groups and match the recipient for adduct, neutral mass within \result{window}, and \methodtodo{final acquisition/peak-richness matching}. Spectral and identity lift are computed after equal-weight molecule-group aggregation.

Structured reasoning-specific controls use the parent/pilot \(\times\) normal/no-reasoning/shuffled-reasoning six-arm design. This analysis is supporting evidence and is reported in Extended Data rather than used as the principal chemical claim.

\subsection*{Reciprocal adaptation and factorial endpoint evaluation}

A reciprocal ORBIT run begins from immutable hypothesis and evidence snapshots. Each round produces a deterministic observation, selects one target, authorizes at most one bounded intervention and settles the change through paired evaluation. Evidence updates must improve the live candidate-frontier endpoint while preserving frozen anchors, non-target checks and a success bank. Hypothesis updates require a held-out molecular endpoint and correct-versus-shuffled spectrum dependence. Training loss, reward and pLSE score alone cannot authorize a METEOR promotion.

Evidence-program revisions are linked to a frozen counterexample class. Missing-support failures identify observed, discriminative masses not reached by the active fragment-generation basis; overgeneration failures identify broad transformations that produce shared or accidental support across alternatives; authority failures identify assignments for which mass compatibility exists but the available trace is insufficient for a stronger mechanistic claim. Each proposed revision is evaluated on the target failure class, frozen anchors, non-target checks and held-out molecule groups before promotion.

The principal causal comparison is performed after final snapshots freeze. We evaluate \((H_0,E_0)\), \((H_T,E_0)\), \((H_0,E_T)\) and \((H_T,E_T)\) on identical held-out cases and calculate the preregistered interaction effect. \result{Add fixed-side and ungated-alternation controls and replicate runs in the final experiment.} The complete cross-generation matrix is computed post hoc from frozen snapshots and is not used to tune intermediate rounds.
