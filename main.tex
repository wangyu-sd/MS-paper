\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is one of the principal technologies for discovering and characterizing small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Modern LC--MS/MS experiments can survey thousands of molecular features in a complex sample, but converting fragmentation spectra into chemical structures remains a central bottleneck. This problem persists even as metabolomics expands from single-cell measurements to population-scale studies: a recent Nature Perspective identified metabolite identification as a shared challenge across these regimes and as a constraint on future metabolic atlases and foundation models.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Spectral-library matching provides strong evidence when an authentic reference has already been measured, yet libraries cover only a small fraction of the chemical diversity encountered in untargeted experiments.\cite{daSilva2015,Wang2016GNPS} Most measured spectra therefore lie between partial structural information and resolved chemical identity.

The biological cost of this ambiguity extends beyond missing compound names. Chemically anonymous peaks cannot be reliably grouped into structural families, placed into transformation series or assigned to host, microbial or dietary metabolism. Recent metabolomics studies illustrate what becomes possible once structures are sufficiently constrained: newly resolved mammalian metabolites can be traced across tissues and to dietary, microbial and endogenous origins, while structurally defined metabolite families can reveal disease associations and biological activity.\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet,Gentry2024} The dark metabolome is therefore not only an annotation deficit; it is a hidden layer of biochemical organization whose tissue distribution, metabolic origin and functional relationships remain difficult to interpret while the underlying features are structurally unresolved.

Computational approaches have substantially improved the molecular hypotheses that can be brought to these spectra. Fragmentation trees, molecular fingerprints and formula-aware models enabled increasingly powerful retrieval and chemical-class inference;\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Hoffmann2022,Xing2023,Goldman2023} forward models learned to reproduce spectra and local fragmentation behaviour from molecular structure;\cite{Wang2021CFMID,Young2024,Nowatzky2025} and de novo generators increasingly construct complete, chemically plausible structures directly from spectra.\cite{Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} DeepMet recently showed that chemical language models can anticipate previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites and combine a learned molecular prior with predicted MS/MS to prioritize candidate structures in complex biological samples.\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet} At the same time, representation learning on millions of unlabelled spectra has expanded the scale at which chemical similarity can be organized.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams} These advances improve hypothesis generation, but they do not guarantee that the experiment contains evidence capable of distinguishing the resulting alternatives. Better hypotheses can therefore make the remaining elucidation problem harder rather than easier.

We separate the structural support available from a molecular candidate from the experimental observations that make that support discriminative. For a molecule $M$ and executable fragmentation program $G$, we define a fragment-mass fingerprint $F_G(M)$ as the set of exact fragment masses supported by the program, together with provenance and authority when available. This representation deliberately avoids assuming that every supported mass is observed, intense or generated by a uniquely known gas-phase mechanism. A measured MS/MS spectrum samples this larger structure-derived support space under acquisition-dependent observability. The same observed mass may be supported by many candidates and therefore carry little identity information; another, possibly weak, peak can be decisive if its mass is supported only by one serious alternative. FMF space is therefore the scientific representation on which candidate-relative evidence can be defined and audited.

A fixed fragmentation program and a fixed hypothesis model are both incomplete. We therefore formulate metabolite structure elucidation as a \emph{long-horizon scientific inference problem}: the system must persist as molecular alternatives change, recognize when its current evidence or reasoning becomes insufficient, identify the scientific program responsible for that failure, test a bounded revision and determine whether the revision caused recovery before carrying it into the next challenge. ORBIT-MS separates two mutable programs while keeping scientific authority outside the mutable search space. On the evidence side, pLSE/ERSF controls the generation, search and interpretation of FMF support. On the hypothesis side, METEOR retains frozen molecular pretraining while its spectrum-conditioning, typed reasoning, post-training and decoding programs can be revised. Improved METEOR candidates create harder local alternatives that stress FMF discrimination; repaired evidence can in turn expose unsupported mechanistic shortcuts or poorly grounded METEOR reasoning. Durable challenge sets record the sequence challenge $\rightarrow$ degradation $\rightarrow$ targeted repair $\rightarrow$ recovery $\rightarrow$ transfer $\rightarrow$ new challenge. LLM workers may propose typed edits, but deterministic chemistry, frozen evaluator/data receipts, fresh evaluation, replay/ablation and protected-data boundaries decide whether any edit receives reusable scientific credit. We use this framework to ask six linked questions: what representation carries molecular identity; why improved hypotheses create harder evidence challenges; what experimentally grounds that evidence; whether a long-horizon agent can learn through challenge and recovery; whether such evidence seeking can map the dark metabolome at scale; and whether the resulting structural organization exposes previously inaccessible biology.

\section*{Experimental spectra ground discriminative FMF evidence}

A structure-derived FMF is a chemical possibility space, not experimental evidence by itself. The fragmentation program can generate masses that are never observed under a given acquisition condition, and a supported mass may be shared by every candidate. We therefore asked what evidence can legitimately ground the agent's structural decisions.

For each candidate set, we constructed matched structure-only and spectrum-conditioned views of the same FMF/reaction prior. The structure-only view retained supported fragment masses and chemical provenance but no fabricated peak presence, intensity or empirical reactivity. The spectrum-conditioned view linked the same prior to measured MS/MS through the frozen mass gate. The effect was strongest for candidate sets sharing formula and scaffold, indicating that useful support was tied to the identity of the experiment rather than to chemical plausibility alone. Thus, the agent's verifier is not another language model: discriminative evidence must remain bound to the measured spectrum.

We further separated mass support from reaction-level authority. In the current CID certificate, a positive complete legal trajectory is sufficient positive reaction evidence for a mass assignment; failure to obtain a positive certificate is treated as negative evidence only when the relevant alternative trajectory search is complete. Unsupported adducts are reported as \texttt{NOT\_EVALUATED}, and search truncation is reported separately from chemical failure. This asymmetry prevents missing or computationally unsearched chemistry from being converted into false candidate contradiction and establishes the evidentiary boundary within which later agent-driven revisions are judged.

\section*{A long-horizon scientific agent learns through molecular challenge and recovery}

The first three analyses define the representation, the changing challenge frontier and the experimental grounding required for reliable structure inference. We next asked whether a scientific agent could persist across these changes and improve the responsible scientific program when a new challenge exposed its limitation. We represented recurrent difficulty as a durable \emph{ChallengeSet}. Each challenge records the spectrum, candidate alternatives, structural-similarity class, creation round, the model that created the challenge, the current FMF/pLSE evidence gap, the METEOR reasoning gap and the evidence required for resolution. Challenge sources include METEOR-generated hard decoys, same-formula and same-scaffold alternatives, pLSE counterexamples, ERSF disagreement and unresolved experimental spectra.

The term \emph{long-horizon scientific agent} refers here to persistence across changing scientific states, not merely long context or long runtime. ORBIT maintains challenge lineage across rounds, detects a measurable degradation or ambiguity increase, attributes the failure to a typed mutable surface, executes or trains a candidate repair, performs fresh evaluation, assigns causal credit through replay and ablation, tests transfer and carries only supported lessons into the next challenge. The scientific target of mutation can therefore change over time even though scientific legality and evaluation authority remain fixed.

The interaction between hypothesis and evidence is deliberately asymmetric. Improvements in METEOR candidate fidelity create locally harder alternatives and can reduce the discrimination of a previously adequate FMF/pLSE program. Failure attribution then determines whether the dominant deficit lies in missing or overgenerated fragment support, search/evidence logic, or the hypothesis/reasoning program. On the evidence side, the mutable program can be represented as $P=(G_{\rm chem},S_{\rm search},E_{\rm evidence},I_{\rm impl})$. On the hypothesis side, METEOR is represented as $M=(P_{\rm frozen},C_{\rm spectrum},R_{\rm cot},T_{\rm post},D_{\rm decode})$: molecular pretraining remains immutable, whereas spectrum conditioning, typed reasoning, post-training and decoding/evidence-request programs can be revised.

METEOR reasoning is treated as a typed scientific program rather than free-form prose. The durable reasoning state contains observed evidence, candidate hypotheses, mechanistic constraints, discriminative predictions, unresolved ambiguities, counterfactual predictions, a next-evidence request and a final candidate distribution. A reasoning program does not receive credit for being longer or more fluent; it is evaluated by final molecular discrimination, binding to executable FMF/pLSE evidence, counterfactual correctness, calibration/abstention, same-formula discrimination, efficiency and unsupported-claim rate. When repaired evidence exposes a METEOR statement that cannot be grounded or a false mechanistic shortcut, the reasoning/post-training program itself can become the next mutation target.

Each authorized mutation is bound to a typed scientific contract specifying the parent program, failure evidence, mutable surface, exact edit, scientific hypothesis, predicted gain and regression, falsification test and compute budget. LLM workers may propose the edit, but they cannot choose the protected evaluator, widen their capability boundary or promote themselves. Candidate programs undergo fresh discovery evaluation under immutable chemistry and evaluator/data receipts. Replay tests whether the exact edit reproduces the predicted gain; ablation tests whether removing that edit removes the gain; transfer tests whether the repair survives unseen challenge families. Only replay/ablation-supported lessons from mutation-eligible data can guide later mutations, while held-out, external and prospective evidence remains outside adaptive memory.

\section*{Discussion}

Our results recast tandem-mass-spectrometry metabolite elucidation as a long-horizon scientific inference problem operating over a structure-derived mass-support representation. The field has made rapid progress in molecular hypothesis generation, from formula and fingerprint inference to retrieval, spectrum simulation and complete molecular generation.\cite{Duhrkop2019,Hoffmann2022,Goldman2023,Xing2023,Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX,Qiang2026DeepMet} FMFs address a complementary question: what fragment masses can a structure support, and which of those masses actually distinguish it from the alternatives relevant to the measured spectrum? Sparse FMF combinations can carry substantial molecular identity information, yet the value of any one mass is conditional on the candidate set and on whether the experiment observes it.

This distinction explains why stronger molecular generation need not make elucidation easier. Close same-formula candidates share much of the same fragment-mass support, causing global coverage and spectrum similarity to lose margin as the candidate frontier improves. The resulting evidence challenge is not solved by maximizing the size of the fragmentation lattice or explained intensity. Broad support can increase accidental matching; conversely, a weak but candidate-specific mass can carry disproportionate structural information. Atom-traceable CID trajectories therefore serve as a higher-authority grounding layer for selected evidence rather than as a requirement that every observed peak receive a uniquely known mechanism.

The long-horizon contribution follows from the fact that both the hypothesis and evidence programs are executable and fallible. Better METEOR candidates do not merely improve a generator benchmark; they can stress the current FMF evidence boundary and expose a new scientific failure. Conversely, repaired FMF/pLSE evidence can expose unsupported METEOR reasoning and false mechanistic shortcuts. ORBIT-MS therefore treats challenge--recovery--new-challenge dynamics, rather than monotonic score increase or trajectory length, as the object of scientific learning. A long-horizon scientific agent is useful only if it recognizes which scientific program failed, proposes a bounded change, survives an external verifier, localizes the gain through replay/ablation, transfers to new challenges and retains abstention when the evidence remains insufficient. LLMs are therefore mutation operators rather than scientific authorities.

At metabolomics scale, the same principle leads to a different kind of resource. A dark-metabolome map should not be a catalogue of forced predicted SMILES. Evidence-seeking campaigns can stop at exact structure, bounded candidate sets, molecular families or explicit insufficient-evidence states, while retaining the additional evidence that would be needed to progress. The biological value of such a resource lies in recovering relationships that anonymous peaks conceal. Structurally related features can be tested as families rather than isolated variables, revealing tissue compartmentalization, coordinated responses and repeated molecular transformations even when only a subset of family members reaches exact structural resolution. This preserves uncertainty while making the dark metabolome analyzable as a biochemical system rather than a collection of unexplained signals.

Biological interpretation requires stronger evidence than co-occurrence. Tissue restriction alone does not prove metabolic origin, and a repeated mass difference does not prove an enzymatic reaction. We therefore separate three levels of biological inference: descriptive organization of structurally related features across samples; replicated association of a molecular family or transformation series with a biological context; and causal or source attribution supported by dedicated perturbation, isotope tracing or functional experiments. This hierarchy parallels the structural evidence hierarchy and prevents improved annotation from being converted automatically into overconfident biological mechanism.

Recent metabolomics studies demonstrate the potential payoff of closing this structure-to-biology gap. Newly resolved mammalian metabolites have been traced to diet, microbiota and host metabolism, while structurally defined metabolite families have revealed disease associations and receptor or immune activity.\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet,Gentry2024} ORBIT-MS addresses the complementary bottleneck that precedes those analyses: determining which structural hypotheses are actually supported, improving the scientific programs that fail on harder alternatives, and carrying the remaining uncertainty into downstream biology. \result{Insert final representation, challenge--recovery, dark-resource, biological-programme and validation synthesis.}

More broadly, metabolite identification remains a shared challenge as metabolomics moves toward cellular and population-scale atlases and foundation models.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Increasing the number or plausibility of molecular hypotheses does not increase the information contained in a fixed spectrum. Reliable elucidation therefore requires a scalable structural representation, an experimentally grounded verifier, a mechanism for revising the responsible scientific program when new challenges expose its limitations, and a way to stop when the experiment remains insufficient. FMF space provides the representation; experimental spectra and auditable trajectories provide the grounding; the long-horizon scientific agent supplies verified challenge-driven learning. Coupled to evidence-seeking dark-spectrum campaigns and focused biological validation, these components create a route from passive spectrum-to-structure prediction toward auditable mapping of the dark metabolome and experimentally testable discovery of previously hidden metabolic programmes.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary separation}

The study separates three authority-bearing objects at every evaluation point: molecular hypotheses, the executable FMF/evidence program and the experimental spectrum. Development, calibration and test partitions are molecule-group disjoint. Candidate pools used for causal comparison are frozen before evaluation, and protected held-out, external or prospective evidence is never used to generate adaptive program mutations.

A mass match, a reaction certificate and a molecular identification are distinct claims. Fragment-mass compatibility can support a candidate at the FMF layer; a reaction-level claim additionally requires a complete atom-traceable trajectory; a unique structural identification requires candidate-relative evidence and the frozen resolution policy. Unsupported adducts, unavailable reaction families and search truncation are reported separately from candidate contradiction.

\subsection*{Benchmark spectra, structure standardization and leakage control}

Primary benchmarking uses MassSpecGym and \datatodo{additional peer-reviewed datasets selected for the final study}.\cite{MassSpecGym2024} Benchmark-provided train, validation and test roles are retained where available. Repeated spectra associated with the same standardized two-dimensional connectivity are grouped before population-level inference. Structures are standardized using \methodtodo{final canonicalization protocol}.

All structure-only, chemical-language and post-training corpora are audited against frozen validation/test molecules. The final report includes exact-structure overlap, formula overlap, nearest-neighbour Morgan similarity and MCES distance for each pretraining source. Candidate-generation and evidence-evaluation artifacts are versioned separately so external transfer cannot be contaminated by pLSE/FMF-guided sampling.

\subsection*{Fragment-mass fingerprints}

For molecular candidate $M$ and executable fragmentation program $G$, the FMF is
\[
F_G(M)=\{(m_i, a_i, \rho_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_M},
\]
where $m_i$ is the exact fragment mass supported by the program, $a_i$ denotes the evidence/source authority available for that mass and $\rho_i$ retains provenance such as generating operator, depth and trace identity where available. Duplicate masses are collapsed according to \methodtodo{final binning/provenance retention rule}. The FMF is a structure-derived support space and does not imply that every mass is experimentally observable or that one unique gas-phase structure/mechanism is known.

The initial FMF layers are generated from \methodtodo{final ERSF/CID structural enumeration and reaction basis}. Ablations include depth-1 structural fragments, depth-limited fingerprints, neutral-loss inclusion/exclusion, full-lattice support and high-authority trajectory-certified subsets. The synthetic resolving-power experiment samples $K$ masses from a molecule's FMF and retrieves against a frozen molecular pool; this experiment measures intrinsic representational identity and is reported separately from measured-spectrum evaluation.

For a measured peak $p$ and candidate $M_j$, low-level FMF support $u_{jp}$ records whether an eligible FMF mass falls within the frozen tolerance. Candidate-relative specificity is computed against the same fixed candidate pool, conceptually as
\[
d_{jp}=u_{jp}-\max_{k\neq j}u_{kp},
\]
with the production score using \methodtodo{final weighted/assignment-aware definition}. Candidate opportunity/overgeneration is controlled using \methodtodo{final normalization or penalty}. Global explained intensity and FMF size are diagnostics rather than primary identification endpoints.

\subsection*{High-authority CID evidence}

The pLSE/CID engine represents fragmentation as a bounded reaction-state network. Ion states record molecular graph, analytical adduct, charge carrier, formal-charge state, proton/sodium and radical state where represented, and root-atom lineage. Elementary events record bond changes, atom/component lineage, formal-charge changes, hydrogen/hydride transfer where represented, product charge carrier, theoretical masses and provenance.

The current CID certificate uses direct complete trajectories as reaction-level evidence under the frozen 0.02-Da mass contract. A mass-reached peak receives positive reaction authority when at least one complete legal certifying trajectory is found. Exhaustive enumeration of unrelated alternative trajectories is not required for positive evidence. In contrast, absence of a certificate contributes negative evidence only when the relevant alternative trajectory search is complete; trajectory-budget truncation is reported as search truncation. Endpoint reachability remains diagnostic rather than reaction-level evidence, and unsupported adducts are \texttt{NOT\_EVALUATED} rather than scientific failures.

\subsection*{Candidate populations and difficulty matching}

Candidate populations are standardized and deduplicated at two-dimensional connectivity level. Provenance records generator/checkpoint or database source, formula, exact mass, generation rank and original identifier. Candidate difficulty is quantified independently of FMF/evidence scores by MCES distance, Morgan similarity, same-formula frequency, scaffold relation and exact-mass difference.

For Figure 2, difficulty strata are matched for candidate-set size and precursor constraints under \methodtodo{final matching protocol}. The evidence-challenge analysis is repeated across independent candidate sources and frozen evidence systems; no evidence system is retrained within this analysis.

\subsection*{Fragmentation comparators and ablations}

Figure 1 compares FMF evidence with \result{final peer-reviewed comparator set}, including exact-mass matching, opportunity-normalized matching, forward-spectrum similarity and CFM-ID/SIRIUS-derived or other executable fragmentation-aware scores where technically appropriate. Comparators receive identical spectra and candidate pools. Internal ablations progressively test candidate-isolated FMF support, candidate specificity, FMF depth/source restriction, reaction-centre eligibility, atom/charge accounting, hydrogen-transfer constraints and trajectory authority.

Primary endpoints are \result{frozen molecule-group discrimination metric}, GT rank, Top-$k$, pairwise GT win, GT-best-decoy margin and false-strong alternative rate, with strict same-formula and near-isomer strata. Efficiency endpoints include wall time, expanded states/edges, FMF size and certificate budget.

\subsection*{METEOR hypothesis and typed reasoning program}

METEOR is the spectrum-conditioned molecular hypothesis model. The final implementation uses \result{final backbone, model size and checkpoint lineage}, a chemistry-specific vocabulary, continuous token-mass state and peak-level spectrum conditioning. Experimental peaks are represented using absolute $m/z$, precursor-relative $m/z$, intensity and observed acquisition metadata. Molecular pretraining remains outside the evolvable search space.

The mutable post-pretraining program is represented conceptually as
\[
M=(P_{\rm frozen},C_{\rm spectrum},R_{\rm cot},T_{\rm post},D_{\rm decode}),
\]
where $P_{\rm frozen}$ is the frozen molecular pretrained state, $C_{\rm spectrum}$ is spectrum conditioning, $R_{\rm cot}$ is the typed reasoning program, $T_{\rm post}$ is the authorized post-training program and $D_{\rm decode}$ is decoding/evidence-request policy. The durable reasoning program contains \emph{ObservedEvidence}, \emph{CandidateHypotheses}, \emph{MechanisticConstraints}, \emph{DiscriminativePredictions}, \emph{UnresolvedAmbiguities}, \emph{CounterfactualPredictions}, \emph{NextEvidenceRequest} and \emph{FinalCandidateDistribution}. \methodtodo{Freeze the exact executable representation and spectrum-conditioning interface.}

CoT quality is evaluated by final molecular discrimination, evidence-grounded step fraction, executable mechanistic support, counterfactual prediction accuracy, calibration/abstention, same-formula/same-scaffold discrimination, reasoning cost and unsupported-claim rate. Longer or more fluent reasoning does not receive scientific credit by itself. Where a reasoning statement claims that a fragment or mechanism supports a candidate, evaluation attempts to bind that statement to durable FMF/pLSE/ERSF evidence; otherwise it is explicitly marked ungrounded. Generated non-ground-truth molecules may act as hard negatives or policy actions but do not become positive labels solely because they receive high similarity or evidence scores.

\subsection*{Correct-, absent- and shuffled-spectrum controls}

Structure-only and spectrum-conditioned views share the same FMF/reaction proposal basis but differ in experimental authority. The structure-only view contains chemical possibilities without fabricated peak-presence, intensity or empirical-reactivity labels. The spectrum-conditioned view links the same prior to observed peaks through the deterministic mass gate. Shuffled donors come from different molecule groups and are matched for \result{final adduct, neutral-mass, acquisition and peak-richness constraints}. Spectral and identity lift are aggregated with equal molecule-group weighting.

\subsection*{Long-horizon scientific agent and co-evolution harness}

The term \emph{long-horizon scientific agent} denotes the durable campaign controller that maintains scientific state across multiple challenge, failure-attribution, mutation, training/evaluation, replay/ablation, transfer and evidence-seeking episodes. It is not defined by context length, wall-clock duration or the number of workers. The highest adaptive unit is a ScientificCampaign rather than an individual LLM turn. A campaign contains versioned runs/episodes, immutable job and artifact references, current pLSE/METEOR archives, challenge-set references, resource usage and restart checkpoints. Runtime durability is an implementation requirement rather than a scientific endpoint: browser disconnect or controller restart may not change candidate identity, scientific evidence or evaluation authority. \methodtodo{Freeze the final H0--H7 campaign implementation and version identifiers.}

The evidence-side program is represented conceptually as $P=(G_{\rm chem},S_{\rm search},E_{\rm evidence},I_{\rm impl})$, spanning FMF/ERSF reaction generation, CID search, evidence/scoring and selected implementation surfaces. The hypothesis-side program is the post-pretraining METEOR program defined above. Mutable targets are chosen from measured failure attribution rather than round-robin scheduling; not every subsystem is mutated every round. Fast changes may alter search allocation, decoding or reasoning budget, medium-timescale changes may alter pLSE programs or METEOR post-training, and slow changes may introduce new chemistry operators or causal replay.

Each campaign maintains a ChallengeSet. Challenge cases record spectrum and candidate references, structural-similarity class, creation round, creating target, current failure type, pLSE/FMF evidence gap, METEOR reasoning gap, resolution status and evidence used for recovery. Challenge sources include METEOR-generated hard decoys, same-formula/same-scaffold alternatives, pLSE counterexamples, ERSF disagreement and unresolved spectra. A round records challenge introduction, measured degradation or ambiguity increase, selected mutation target, repair attempt, fresh evaluation, recovery status and any new challenge generated after recovery.

Each proposed mutation is represented by a ScientificMutation contract containing parent identifiers, typed surface, failure-evidence references, a scientific hypothesis, causal claim, exact edit, predicted gains and regressions, falsification test, required evidence and compute budget. Sandboxed workers receive only the assigned mutable surface and capability ceiling; evaluator, protected-data and promotion surfaces are forbidden. Candidate programs are freshly evaluated under immutable dataset and evaluator receipts. Training loss, optimized reward, model prose or mutation-worker confidence are never promotion evidence.

\subsection*{Causal replay, scientific memory and independent repair}

Archive gains are initially treated as associations. Causal replay evaluates the parent and exact edited program under the same dataset receipt and evaluator version, with an optional ablated program that removes the edit. A predicted gain receives replay support only when all preregistered target metrics exceed their minimum gains and the edit remains within its compute budget. Causal localization additionally requires the ablation to remove the gain. Transfer support requires the frozen edit to improve a prespecified unseen challenge family, candidate source or external domain without adaptive feedback. Outcomes are recorded as \texttt{ASSOCIATED}, \texttt{REPLAY\_SUPPORTED}, \texttt{ABLATION\_SUPPORTED} or \texttt{TRANSFER\_SUPPORTED}.

Replay-supported outcomes can be compressed into ScientificLessons retaining the target, typed surface, statement, scope, failure signature, raw evidence references, exact-edit provenance and predicted trade-offs. A lesson is reusable for future mutation only when its causal status is replay- or ablation-supported and all source stages belong to mutation-eligible screen/discovery data. Evidence from held-out, external or prospective stages can support or refute a lesson but cannot increase adaptive mutation probability within the same protocol phase.

Independent review is bound to an immutable candidate/evaluation snapshot. The candidate producer cannot review its own proposal. If repair is requested, the reviewer cannot be the repair actor; the repair creates a new child candidate, inherits no previous evaluation and requires fresh deterministic evaluation. Protocol v2 remains the final promotion authority.

\subsection*{Challenge--recovery evaluation}

Figure 4 evaluates the long-horizon scientific agent using \methodtodo{frozen campaign count, round limit, population sizes, training/mutation budgets and stopping rules}. For each challenge we report candidate fidelity, candidate distinguishability, ambiguity-set size, challenge introduction round, degradation magnitude, selected repair target, recovery status, rounds-to-recovery and total recovery cost. Campaign-level endpoints include challenge introduction rate, recovery rate, cross-generation transfer, cross-family transfer and reintroduced-failure rate.

Primary causal comparisons include both sides fixed, pLSE/FMF-only evolution, METEOR-only evolution, full long-horizon co-evolution, random/no-agent mutation, no replay, no reusable scientific memory and ungated/weakened-verifier controls. Molecular endpoints include GT rank, GT-best-decoy margin, same-formula pair accuracy, ambiguity-set size and false-strong alternatives; evidence endpoints include candidate-specific FMF fraction, certified coverage and unsupported-claim rate; efficiency endpoints include wall time, expanded states, LLM tokens, training tokens/GPU hours and external evidence cost. Improvements on protected/external data are evaluated only after the relevant program freezes and never flow back into mutation allocation.

\subsection*{Risk-controlled structural resolution}

Candidate generation and evidence-based discrimination are reported separately. Generator recall is the fraction of molecule groups in which the ground truth is present in the candidate set. Conditional discrimination is evaluated only when the ground truth is present. End-to-end unique resolution combines both components.

A disjoint calibration split constructs structural prediction sets $\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\alpha}(S)$ using \methodtodo{final split-conformal or other risk-control method}. We report empirical set coverage, candidate-set size, unique-resolution rate, conditional accuracy and false-resolution rate. A unique two-dimensional structure is emitted only when the calibrated set contains one candidate. Stereochemical resolution is not claimed from MS/MS alone unless independently supported.

\subsection*{Dark-spectrum campaigns and resource construction}

For each quality-controlled dark spectrum, a DarkSpectrumCampaign stores the acquisition context, metabolomics/family context, initial candidate universe, current belief state, evidence budget and terminal status. The adaptive loop is candidate proposal $\rightarrow$ executable FMF/pLSE evidence $\rightarrow$ posterior/ambiguity $\rightarrow$ next discriminating computation/evidence $\rightarrow$ belief update $\rightarrow$ frozen stopping rule. The first implementation may restrict the adaptive action space to computation; prospective CE/MS3/adduct/CCS/standard actions are included only when actually available and predeclared.

Valid terminal states are \texttt{RESOLVED\_SUPPORTED}, \texttt{NARROWED\_HYPOTHESES}, \texttt{UNRESOLVED\_INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE}, \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE} and \texttt{BUDGET\_EXHAUSTED}. A campaign may terminate unresolved without inventing an identity. If a genuine dark/prospective case exposes an algorithmic failure, that failure can create a separate mutation-eligible discovery campaign using only legally reusable evidence; protected/prospective evidence from the source case is not copied into adaptive mutation.

Every campaign contributes a resource entry at the highest resolution supported by the frozen policy: unresolved/precursor level, formula/class, structural family, bounded candidate set, high-confidence putative two-dimensional structure or orthogonally confirmed identity. Resource records retain spectrum provenance, precursor information, candidate set, FMF-supported and discriminative masses, chemical authority, calibrated set size, unresolved alternatives, remaining discriminating evidence requirement, recurrence and validation status. Evidence levels are never collapsed into one binary annotation label.

Chemical-space and molecular-family analyses use \methodtodo{final structure/FMF/network representation and similarity-threshold protocol} independently of biological metadata. Family definitions are frozen before association testing. Recurrent features must satisfy \methodtodo{final detection/reproducibility criteria} across biological samples or cohorts.

\subsection*{Biological organization of the dark-metabolome resource}

Biological analyses begin only after molecular-family definitions, evidence levels and resource entries are frozen. The first layer is descriptive organization: for each evidence-qualified feature or molecular family we quantify \methodtodo{tissue-specificity/entropy score, prevalence, abundance, within-family co-variation and recurrence}. Family-level abundance is aggregated using \methodtodo{final robust family-level summary} without using phenotype labels to redefine family membership. Association testing then evaluates predefined tissue, condition, genotype, treatment or phenotype metadata using \methodtodo{final statistical model, covariates and multiplicity correction}.

Candidate structural-transformation series are constructed from frozen molecular relationships using \methodtodo{exact-mass differences, candidate structural edits and/or FMF relationships}. A repeated mass difference or graph edit is treated as a candidate transformation relationship, not an enzymatic reaction. Reaction or pathway language is used only when supported by independent biochemical knowledge, isotopic evidence, perturbation or direct experimental validation. Metadata-label permutation, evidence-level sensitivity analyses and exclusion of low-confidence members provide negative controls for family-level biological claims.

Discovery and replication samples are separated where the cohort design permits; otherwise exploratory and confirmatory analyses are explicitly distinguished. A family or metabolite programme is elevated to the principal biological result only when it reproduces under \methodtodo{final replication criterion} and remains stable to annotation-confidence/evidence-level sensitivity analyses.

\subsection*{Orthogonal structure validation and biological source attribution}

Anchor metabolites for Figure 6 are selected by a frozen discovery criterion before orthogonal validation. Structure-level confirmation uses \methodtodo{authentic standard acquisition/synthesis}, matched LC--MS/MS, retention time/coelution and targeted MSn or spike-in when needed to distinguish remaining isomers. Unvalidated family members retain the lower structural level justified by the resource.

Biological source or mechanism is tested only when the cohort contains the necessary intervention. Tissue distribution alone does not assign metabolic origin. Where available, diet perturbation tests dietary contribution; antibiotic or gnotobiotic perturbation tests microbiome dependence; stable-isotope tracing tests precursor incorporation; genotype or pathway perturbation tests host-biochemical dependence. A focused functional assay is included only when it directly tests the resource-derived biological hypothesis and when the relevant anchor structure has sufficient orthogonal support. Negative or inconclusive source-attribution experiments leave the corresponding origin unresolved rather than being replaced by post hoc mechanistic interpretation.
