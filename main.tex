\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Untargeted tandem mass spectrometry detects a vast repertoire of molecular signals, yet most experimental spectra remain without a confident structural assignment. This metabolomic dark matter is difficult to elucidate because chemically distinct molecules can explain overlapping fragments, while fixed inference models cannot learn from the structural hypotheses they repeatedly fail to generate or distinguish. Here we introduce ORBIT-MS, an evidence-guided, agent-directed recursive self-improvement (RSI) system that couples a spectrum-conditioned molecular Generator, a structure-conditioned fragmentation World and an experimental-spectrum Verifier. A Scientific Improver Agent diagnoses failed inferences, selects targeted chemical-knowledge, generator or verifier updates, and retains the observed consequences as experience for subsequent decisions. In a source-audited three-study resource, 613,331 of 835,953 eligible biological MS/MS queries lacked an accepted reference-spectrum match. Existing known-formula calibration experiments show that fragment knowledge substantially improves molecular candidate reach and that W/V evidence improves ranking within the same candidate pool. We investigate \result{the population distribution of supported structural resolution among reference-unmatched spectra}, \result{new molecular structures and resolution tiers recovered across completed autonomous RSI generations}, and \result{chemically supported relationships connecting unresolved spectra to characterized molecular families}. Independent spectral evidence and, where available, orthogonal chemistry are used to assess the resulting assignments. ORBIT-MS aims to progressively extend the interpretable chemical space of the metabolomic dark matter rather than to assign an unsupported unique structure to every observation.

\vspace{1em}

Untargeted metabolomics provides a broad view of the small-molecule signals present in biological and environmental samples, but structural interpretation remains a central bottleneck.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024,Alexandrov2026Perspective} Large experimental MS/MS archives contain measurements that are not assigned confidently by existing spectral libraries, and the number of measured spectra greatly exceeds the number of independently characterized molecular structures. Nevertheless, unannotated spectra are not synonymous with undiscovered molecules: different ion species, acquisition conditions and repeated experiments can produce many records for one precursor. Understanding the metabolomic dark matter therefore requires both source-aware measurement accounting and a defensible way to associate fragment evidence with molecular structures.

Established annotation approaches exploit exact-mass constraints, molecular formulas, reference spectra, predicted fingerprints, molecular-network relationships and computational fragmentation.\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Goldman2023,MassSpecGym2024} Spectral foundation models and large-scale experimental data improve representation learning, while molecular generative approaches increase the diversity of plausible structures that can be proposed.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams,Acikalin2026GEMS,Wang2026MetGenX} Yet molecular inference is inherently underdetermined. Distinct connectivities can have identical elemental compositions and overlapping fragment ions; neither a high spectral similarity score nor a plausible generated SMILES alone establishes a particular structure. Conversely, reducing the set of possible structures without removing the true molecular graph requires distinguishing genuinely structure-specific information from fragment evidence shared by many candidates.

We address this difficulty through evidence-guided molecular structure elucidation and agent-directed recursive learning. In ORBIT-MS, a spectrum-conditioned Generator \(G\) proposes candidate molecular graphs, including constructions that reuse fragment and motif knowledge. A structure-conditioned fragmentation World \(W\), which does not observe the measured target fragment peaks during forward inference, supplies fragment hypotheses from each proposed precursor. A Verifier \(V\) evaluates their agreement with measured MS/MS and helps distinguish competing molecular candidates. Crucially, the resulting failures have different chemical meanings: the generator may never reach the true graph, an adequate candidate may be misranked, or the present knowledge resource may lack transferable structural evidence. We use these distinctions to motivate an agentic recursive self-improvement (RSI) loop in which a Scientific Improver proposes an intervention, existing chemical training tools update the shared system and its outcome becomes evidence for the next decision. The agent does not replace the chemical models, and its language-model parameters need not be retrained: recursive improvement occurs in the WGV system and in the experience available to subsequent agent decisions.

We investigate this proposition at three levels. First, we construct an experimentally grounded universe of reference-unmatched spectra and quantify the structural resolution that can be supported. Second, using known-identity benchmarks and source-linked spectral examples, we measure how fragment-informed generation and discrimination determine which molecular graphs are recoverable, and ask whether agent-guided recursive updates unlock additional structures previously beyond the system's reach. Third, we organize supported hypotheses into chemically interpretable families and test them with independent spectral or orthogonal evidence. The paper's outcome is therefore the expansion and independent support of recoverable molecular knowledge, rather than a generic agent-performance leaderboard or an unsubstantiated biological pathway assignment. Figure~\ref{fig:orbit_method} makes the central causal questions explicit: a generator must first reach a candidate graph; evidence must then distinguish it from alternatives; and an agentic learning process can use the observed failure to choose which chemical capability should improve next.

\section*{Structurally resolving the reference-unmatched metabolome}

The ORBIT-MS inference and agent-directed learning workflow is outlined in Fig.~\ref{fig:orbit_method}. We first established which experimental tandem spectra are eligible for structural investigation, before inferring any new molecular identities (Extended Data Fig.~1). The source-audited collections UC, IBD200 and VEO-IBD contained 348 expected acquisition files, of which 272 were verified and 76 were unavailable. Among 835,953 QC-eligible biological MS/MS queries, 13,884 received an unambiguous accepted reference identity, 14,289 an ambiguous accepted identity, 613,331 no accepted reference match and 194,449 were unassessable under the frozen matching protocol. These are spectra or spectral queries, not independent chemical compounds. A broader six-repository source inventory is available, but independently verified acquisition-level and global MS/MS annotation totals remain separate from this three-study analysis.

The large reference-unmatched fraction motivates a structure-first characterization, but spectral similarity alone cannot supply the missing molecular graphs. In the accessible three-study data, 2,255 communities of reference-unmatched spectra recurred in at least two study collections; 98 appeared in all three. This recurrence identifies a reproducible experimental signal organisation, not 2,255 new metabolites or structurally established molecular families. A degree-preserving conditional topology comparison did not show enrichment of the specific three-study community configuration (empirical upper-tail probability 0.91). The communities therefore serve as an auditable discovery substrate, rather than as chemically identified outcomes (Extended Data Fig.~2).

To measure what can be learned chemically from these observations, we organize repeated ion measurements and compatible acquisitions into traceable spectral entities while retaining their original study and scan identifiers. A frozen ORBIT-MS model then generates candidate structures for representative experimental spectra. Each entity receives a bounded structural statement reflecting the available fragment evidence: a putative unique 2D connectivity, a limited set of competing isomers, a shared structural core, a formula or class assignment, or insufficient structural support. These categories do not assert authentic-standard confirmation. The primary resource result will report \result{the complete number of eligible independent spectral entities, the number entering each structural-resolution tier and the calibrated truth containment of the corresponding tier}; the structure-resolution output of the inference system is illustrated schematically in Fig.~\ref{fig:orbit_method}d, while source-linked resolution statistics will be reported in Extended Data Fig.~3.

We next seek relations between structurally supported entities that persist across independent studies. The intended chemical-family landscape records \result{the count of supportable molecular families, the independent-study recurrence of each family and the graph-level distance from characterized metabolites}, including families for which a unique connectivity cannot be established (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}a). This analysis identifies the quantity of structural knowledge recovered, rather than counting every generated candidate as an annotation. Source-linked real spectra and molecular drawings for \result{representative family hypotheses with verified origin and corresponding evidence} will illustrate the evidence behind individual predictions (Fig.~\ref{fig:independent_support}e).

\section*{Fragment evidence enables molecular construction and discrimination}

We examined the structural information supplied by the different components of ORBIT-MS using experiments in which molecular identity was known independently of the inference procedure (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}). Generator \(G\) constructs legal molecular graphs using atom-, ring- and motif-level operations. A fragmentation World \(W\) supplies structure-linked fragment candidates for each molecular hypothesis, whereas Verifier \(V\) evaluates the evidence those fragments receive from the experimental spectrum. This separation matters because creating a candidate molecular structure and selecting the correct structure among generated alternatives are different inferential operations.

The MMA fragment and motif resource allows \(G\) to reuse chemically meaningful substructures during graph construction. In a same-checkpoint comparison on 200 held-out calibration molecules, performed with the reference molecular formula provided, disabling MMA yielded 1.50\% Exact@1, 4.50\% Exact@10 and 6.50\% candidate reach. Enabling MMA raised these values to 6.00\%, 14.50\% and 19.00\%, respectively. The corresponding teacher-forced top-action accuracy increased from 69.1\% to 76.0\%. A canonical hybrid generator further combined atom-normalized search and motif-reserved expansion, reaching 12.50\% Exact@1, 21.50\% Exact@10 and 22.50\% reach in the reported 200-molecule calibration progression. These experiments measure known-formula structural construction and cannot be substituted for target-free de novo identification rates.

Fragment-aware operations also altered the search process itself. A ground-truth-conditioned feasibility analysis reduced the mean molecular-construction trajectory from 32.4 to approximately 24.8 decisions by using canonical atom order and multi-atom ring/motif operations. The motif action was applicable to 87\% of the analyzed molecules and attached a mean of 4.5 heavy atoms per occurrence. Because that analysis used a known target trajectory, its contribution is the demonstration of a feasible and shorter structural action representation, not an unknown-molecule identification score.

The complementarity of hypothesis generation and verification is especially clear when the candidate pool is held fixed. On the held-out calibration evaluation, \(G\)-only ranking gave 7.00\% Exact@1 at 16.00\% reach; incorporating \(W/V\) evidence improved Exact@1 to 8.50\% without changing reach. With MMA-seeded starts, candidate reach increased to 19.00\% but \(G\)-only Exact@1 fell to 5.50\%; the same candidates reranked with \(W/V\) attained 10.00\% Exact@1. Thus expanding the possible structures can make final discrimination more difficult, while experimental fragment evidence can recover some of the lost ranking performance (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}c).

The observation that additional fragments need not provide additional discrimination is equally important. An earlier near-isomer challenge yielded 81.20\% correct pairwise discrimination using World fragments alone, but 80.17\% after simple union with MMA motif evidence. The independent utility of a fragment therefore depends on how it distinguishes the competing precursors, not only on whether its mass is present in the measured spectrum. Figure~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}d will pair \result{source-verified experimental MS/MS and actual competing molecular graphs} with their shared and distinguishing fragment hypotheses. Example recoveries of known benchmark molecules, including complex macrolides and cyclic natural products, will be treated as demonstrations of structure elucidation rather than discoveries of new metabolites.

The principal full-cohort benchmark will report \result{frozen target-free Exact@1/10, raw candidate reach, formula recall, time and molecule-group uncertainty on the complete 17,556-spectrum MassSpecGym TEST population}. Historical external development reports include Exact@1 of 16.73\% on an 813-spectrum NPLIB1/CANOPUS evaluation and 4.64\% on a 237-spectrum CASMI 2022 evaluation; these must be presented with their exact model, candidate supply and evaluation context. A separate homology-assisted, known-formula Macro-Core completion setting reached 49.00\% Exact@1 and 90.00\% reach on 200 calibration molecules, demonstrating the value of a supplied partial core rather than native empty-graph de novo performance. Collectively, these experiments establish the component capabilities needed for subsequent dark-spectral inference while preserving the distinctions among molecular proposal, conditional fragment prediction, fixed-pool ranking and complete identification (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}e).

\section*{Agent-guided recursive self-improvement expands the recoverable chemical space}

Molecular structure elucidation is not limited by one fixed type of error. A plausible precursor formula may admit no correct graph among the generator's proposals; a correct graph may be reached but ranked below competing isomers; or the molecular construction and verification machinery may lack fragment motifs or transferable evidence. These failures define distinct opportunities for learning. We investigate whether the system can identify which deficiency currently limits molecular interpretation and choose a corresponding intervention, so that the next generation resolves chemical structures that the previous generation could not (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}).

The existing WGV system already provides useful mechanisms for learning from such experience. World-simulated spectra of non-evaluation structures produce structure--spectrum supervision and contribute fragment and motif knowledge to MMA. Generator rollouts reveal incomplete construction paths, difficult candidate alternatives and incorrect isomers that may improve subsequent generator or verifier training. Over the historical development sequence, the MMA resource increased from approximately 4.23 million TRAIN-derived entries to 12.98 million entries after HMDB-based simulation, and a later multi-source version contained approximately 31.11 million entries. Successive development configurations revealed bottlenecks in long atom-level trajectories, knowledge coverage, complex ring closure and discrimination among candidate structures. These are observed engineering and knowledge-growth results; the number of entries or generations is not itself a measure of newly elucidated chemical structures.

We introduce a Scientific Improver Agent to turn this collection of learning capabilities into a bounded, iterative scientific workflow (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}a). Given a fresh TRAIN-derived diagnostic packet, the agent distinguishes missing true candidates from reachable but misranked structures, examines available fragment and structural failure evidence, and forms an explicit hypothesis about the current bottleneck. It chooses one action from an existing intervention repertoire: \emph{expand chemical knowledge} using supported structure--fragment resources; \emph{improve the molecular Generator} using valid structure-supervised experiences; \emph{improve the Verifier} using real competing structures and hard isomer negatives; or \emph{collect additional evidence} when the diagnosis is insufficient. These are scientific choices about what the current elucidation system needs to learn, not free-form modifications to its source code.

After the selected intervention is carried out by the existing WGV training tools, the updated candidate system is evaluated against the same predeclared molecular cases and candidate budgets. The resulting structural gains, regressions and unsuccessful interventions are written to an episode ledger alongside the agent's hypothesis and reflection. Later decisions receive a bounded summary of this history, allowing the agent to adapt its choices in light of prior experimental outcomes. Thus RSI has two connected feedback paths: the chemical capability of MMA/\(G\)/\(V\) can change through training, while the agent's subsequent decisions can change through persistent experience without retraining the agent language model itself. In initial controlled RSI experiments, \(W\) is kept fixed to isolate these learning effects.

The decisive measurement is \result{the number and fraction of previously unresolved known-structure molecules correctly recovered across successive autonomous WGV updates, partitioned into new true-structure reach and corrected final ranking}. Recovery losses and structural-class changes will be reported as well. On reference-unmatched experimental spectra, the corresponding chemical endpoint is \result{the number of independently identified spectral entities that acquire additional, externally defensible structural resolution: formula or class to shared scaffold, scaffold to bounded isomer alternatives, and bounded alternatives to a putative unique 2D connectivity}. A higher self-reported model confidence alone is not counted as a new elucidation unless it survives the frozen calibration and supporting spectral-evidence criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}b).

To test the specific contribution of scientific decision-making, complete autonomous trajectories will be compared against a frozen WGV system, a fixed update schedule and a scripted failure-rule controller initialized from the same checkpoints. The proposed analysis reports \result{matched-budget agent-selected versus fixed and scripted intervention gains in newly recovered molecules, transfer to independent structures and cumulative recovery per measured resource cost}, together with a no-memory control for whether past experimental outcomes improve later decisions. Real source-linked case histories will show the observed failure, the selected scientific hypothesis, the generated learning experience, the updated model and the newly supported molecular interpretation (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}c--e). PR95's earlier human/scripted generations establish the system's historical development, while completed autonomous episodes from PR96 must supply the measured RSI results; the two are not conflated.

\section*{Previously unresolved spectra reveal molecular-family relationships}

Recovering individual candidate connectivities is only one step toward understanding the chemistry represented by unannotated spectra. Molecular families may be represented by related spectra that were never linked to specific chemical scaffolds, or by candidate structures whose relationships to characterized metabolites were not previously established in a frozen reference snapshot. We therefore ask whether ORBIT-MS provides evidence for interpretable structural relations across the dark-spectral record, without requiring claims about biological pathways (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}).

Candidate structures with sufficient graph-level support are grouped using explicitly defined common cores and molecular differences rather than solely by spectral embeddings or precursor mass differences. Graph edges record supported atom inventory and connectivity differences: for example, a changed substitution position, an oxygen-containing group, an altered chain length or a ring-connection difference. Such labels describe chemical graph differences; they are not direct measurements of enzymatic reactions, gas-phase fragmentation routes or metabolic transformations. Where experimental evidence does not distinguish positional isomers, the family is represented by a bounded structural set or shared scaffold rather than an invented unique precursor.

The intended central analysis is \result{a source-linked chemical relationship network containing a declared number of supported reference-unmatched families and their closest characterized structural neighbours}. Family-level statistics will quantify \result{the distribution of graph-edit distances to characterized metabolites and the supported recurrence of structural difference types across independent studies and chemical classes} (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}a--d). Because the generator can be initialized from known structural knowledge, comparison to formula-, size-, scaffold- and proposal-prior-matched candidates is necessary to distinguish recovered molecular information from the default preference of a chemical model for familiar structures.

We will further distinguish a previously unmatched experimental spectrum from an unrecognized chemical relationship and from a previously unreported chemical structure. Frozen reference libraries and date-stamped structural/literature searches define the first two observational claims; the latter requires stronger independent chemical evidence. The principal representative cases will be \result{actual structure-supported families selected from the resource using prespecified spectral evidence, independent recurrence and recognition status}, illustrated with real MS/MS, molecular graphs and the exact differences that connect their members (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}e).

The value of this analysis is chemical rather than phenotypic: it tests whether fragmented and previously unassigned measurements can be organized into relationships among molecular structures, providing a navigable resource for targeted identification and subsequent metabolomics research.

\section*{Independent spectral evidence supports molecular hypotheses from the dark metabolome}

Predicted precursor structures and fragment assignments remain hypotheses until tested against evidence that was not used to construct or select them. We therefore examine representative candidates through independent spectral observations and, where available, orthogonal chemistry (Fig.~\ref{fig:independent_support}). This analysis asks how far the assignment can be supported, not whether every candidate can be declared uniquely identified.

The first level of corroboration is reproducibility beyond the originating spectrum. Source-linked spectra from independently deposited studies are compared under compatible precursor ion, adduct, polarity and collision-energy conditions. Where multiple collision energies are available, a candidate must account for changes in measured fragment evidence without conflating repeated recordings from the same acquisition with independent replication. Actual experimental MSn ancestor–product relations provide a stronger conditional constraint when they exist, but they cannot be replaced by a predicted fragmentation tree and relabelled as an observed reaction pathway.

The second level is discrimination against concrete chemical alternatives. For each prioritized case, we preserve a small set of plausible competing connectivities and evaluate which experimentally observed peaks and neutral-loss relationships support or fail to distinguish them. We aim to report \result{the number of preselected molecular-family anchors corroborated by independent spectra, the number remaining bounded to isomer/core resolution and the number contradicted or unassessable} (Fig.~\ref{fig:independent_support}a--d). Real molecular drawings, mirror spectra and candidate-specific evidence will be linked to the originating public source identifiers.

The third level, where technically and materially accessible, is orthogonal confirmation using authentic-standard MS/MS, retention/coelution or independent source-verified chemical reference material. Such confirmation is reserved for a smaller prespecified set of candidates; publication of the computational structure resource does not depend on asserting that all entries have been measured as authentic standards. We will distinguish \result{reference-supported, externally corroborated, standard-confirmed and unresolved cases} without mixing these distinct forms of evidence.

An integrated case or family-level example should finally connect the original reference-unmatched experimental spectrum, the proposed structure or bounded core, relevant fragmentation evidence, the explicit structural differences from known metabolites and independently measured corroboration (Fig.~\ref{fig:independent_support}e). This chain is the central chemical output of ORBIT-MS: a previously unassigned experimental observation becomes a testable structural hypothesis with a measurable and explicitly limited degree of support.

\section*{Discussion}

Much of the experimental tandem-spectral record remains inaccessible to structure-level interpretation. The difficulty is not solely a shortage of spectral reference matches: experimental fragment masses are incomplete and many candidate molecular graphs are consistent with the same observations. ORBIT-MS approaches this problem by linking the construction of candidate structures to chemically represented, experimentally evaluated fragment evidence. The objective is to recover more information about the molecular structures behind unannotated spectra, while preserving the ambiguity that the spectra cannot resolve.

The current development results establish a practical complementarity between fragment-informed generation and spectral verification. MMA evidence can substantially improve the reach of chemically constrained molecular construction; World/Verifier evidence can improve the ranking of structures already generated. Yet more candidate coverage or more fragment matches need not improve final structural identification. Agent-guided RSI is designed to use this changing balance as a scientific feedback signal: distinguish a proposal deficit from an evidence-discrimination deficit, select the relevant existing learning operation, measure the resulting chemical capability and preserve that experience for future decisions. The two central forms of recursion are therefore updating WGV's reusable chemical capability and updating the agent's decision context through observed experimental outcomes. Neither is equivalent to merely repeating an LLM prompt or adding more predicted molecules.

The discovery component of the study focuses on interpretable chemical relationships rather than speculative biological mechanisms. Independently repeated dark spectra provide an empirical substrate, but recurrence alone does not reveal a precursor graph. Likewise, structural similarity between proposed candidates does not establish novelty or biosynthesis. The critical scientific outputs are therefore the full-denominator distribution of justified structural resolution, genuinely supported molecular-family relations and selected cases that survive external spectral or orthogonal chemical checks. These results can provide an organized chemical resource for downstream biological studies without depending on unperformed cohort interventions or functional assays.

Several uncertainties are intrinsic to this task. A target-free precursor formula may be ambiguous, a correct connectivity may be absent from the generator's finite candidate space, predicted fragments may not distinguish isomers, and publicly deposited spectra may lack sufficient metadata or independent standards. We retain these outcomes in the reported population rather than forcing a single structural label. Future measurements such as additional collision energies, MSn, ion mobility or authentic standards can be prioritized for the structural distinctions that remain unresolved.

The broader implication is an evidence-centered, self-improving form of computational chemical discovery: unresolved measurements motivate molecular hypotheses, competing hypotheses expose gaps in fragment knowledge or structural discrimination, an agent selects a testable learning intervention, and the next model generation re-examines the chemical space using the accumulated experience. The impact of this loop will be measured by newly recoverable structures, more informative molecular-family relationships and independent spectral corroboration in metabolomic dark matter, rather than by the complexity of the agent harness or the number of iterations alone.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary units}
ORBIT-MS is assessed as a tandem-spectrum structure-elucidation system and a tool for studying chemically unresolved public MS/MS. Experimental spectral queries, unique precursor connectivity groups, distinct spectral entities and molecular-family relationships are separate counting units. Molecular predictions use available measured precursor and fragment information; a predicted structure is not an experimental molecular identity. The main outcomes are structure-level candidate recovery on known-identity evaluation sets, calibrated structural-resolution assignment on reference-unmatched spectra, supported chemical-family relations and independent experimental corroboration. \methodtodo{Freeze a complete figure-to-source table, final model checkpoint, public data snapshot, spectral entity definition and confirmatory outcome thresholds.}

\subsection*{Experimental spectra, source accounting and reference annotation}
Each query contains measured precursor \(m/z\), product-ion \(m/z\) and intensity, observed ion/adduct information when available and acquisition context supported by its original source. The three-study source audit uses UC, IBD200 and VEO-IBD collections and records expected files, verified and unavailable acquisitions, eligible biological MS/MS and mutually exclusive accepted, ambiguous, unmatched and unassessable reference statuses. Acquisition aliases, conversions, repeated spectra, collision energies, technical repeats and cross-repository redepositions are tracked without treating all records as independent molecules or biological samples. A separate ongoing pan-repository inventory spans GNPS/MassIVE, MetaboLights, Metabolomics Workbench, NORMAN/DSFP, MetaboBank and MB-POST; its public index counts are not pooled with verified physical MS2 counts.

Reference matching is defined by the date-stamped GNPS/MassBank/MoNA or other source-verified experimental spectral snapshot, precursor tolerance, fragment matching rule, adduct restrictions and score thresholds. A reference-unmatched status means that no candidate passed this particular reference protocol, not that the structure is absent from all chemical databases. The source identifiers and annotation dispositions remain attached to all inferred structures. \methodtodo{Freeze release-wide spectrum/reference matching source data, complete missing-file dispositions and the exact number of deduplicated representative dark-spectral entities.}

\subsection*{Molecular identities and benchmark partitions}
Connectivity identity is evaluated using the first 14 characters of a canonical InChIKey. This identifies 2D graph connectivity under the chosen standardization and does not demonstrate stereochemical identity. Known-structure benchmarks retain fixed molecule-group partitions and the complete declared eligible denominator; confidence intervals resample molecular groups rather than treating repeated spectra as independent structures. Historical MassSpecGym TRAIN-derived development partitions contain 175,548 fitting spectra, 9,782 calibration spectra and 8,401 repeatedly inspected search-development spectra out of 193,731 eligible TRAIN spectra. These results remain distinguished from official MassSpecGym TEST evaluation. Target-free inference does not read target molecular formulas, target structures or target fingerprints; analyses providing a reference formula, supplied candidate set or partial Macro-Core are reported as separate conditional tasks.

\subsection*{Direct precursor-composition hypotheses}
Precursor elemental-composition hypotheses are inferred from the observed precursor mass, ion/adduct context and permissible fragment evidence. Formula correctness and Top-\(K\) formula recall are measured separately from molecular graph recovery. Chemical validity filters enforce allowed atom inventory, charges, valence, connectivity and precursor/adduct mass consistency. A missing true formula is counted explicitly as a composition-stage failure. Molecular fingerprints are not used to generate, route, score or guide structural proposals in the active target-free pipeline; post-inference Morgan/Tanimoto values are external similarity metrics only.

\subsection*{Fragmentation World and observation-conditioned Verifier}
For a proposed precursor graph, the current World \(W\) constructs a finite set of connected, structurally represented fragment and ion hypotheses, including the source-validated up-to-three-cut candidate support, hydrogen adjustments and permitted charge/proton carriers. It assigns fragment prior weights using the molecular graph and available acquisition context, not the measured target MS/MS fragment-peak list during forward prediction. A proposed fragment structure is a conditional model hypothesis, not a directly observed gas-phase intermediate or a proved electron-flow mechanism.

Verifier \(V\) compares those structural fragment hypotheses with measured product-ion masses and intensities to produce observation-conditioned evidence for the proposed precursor. Evidence is aggregated at the precursor-candidate level using recorded weights and candidate support. The distinction between common fragment masses and candidate-specific structural evidence is explicit. Fixed-support W/V tests, same-formula structural-decoy tests and precursor-compatible peer/shuffled-spectrum controls assess whether the experimental spectrum contributes discrimination beyond structural popularity or candidate priors. \methodtodo{Source-freeze the release W/V checkpoints, exact support representation, scoring coefficients and fragment-to-candidate aggregation.}

\subsection*{Generator, MMA and chemically constrained construction}
Generator \(G\) follows the current PR93/PR95 Peak-Ladder and Canonical Hybrid graph-construction implementation. Given measured MS/MS and admissible precursor/formula hypotheses, it uses chemically legal atom, ring and MMA motif starts/attachments with canonical trajectory ordering, atom-normalized beam scoring and motif-reserved expansion. MMA consists of source-linked reusable fragment/motif records derived from training structures and supported W-simulated structure–spectrum pairs. Graph construction is governed by chemical validity, not by a hidden target graph at inference time. The current optional Macro-Core procedure retrieves a non-evaluation structural homologue, removes one or more heavy-atom components and lets \(G\) complete the remaining graph; this is explicitly labelled homology-assisted completion and is not described as pure de novo generation.

Fixed-pool terminal \(W/V\) reranking changes only the ordering of already generated molecular candidates. Whenever enabled in a selected inference variant, intermediate \(W/V\) evidence influencing future \(G\) actions is separately identified as online guidance. The benchmark reports raw reach before ranking, Exact@1/10 after ranking, generated candidate count, invalid proposals and elapsed inference time. Differences between empty-graph, motif-seeded and homology-assisted modes are not pooled into a single undeclared metric.

\subsection*{Agent-directed recursive self-improvement and chemical experience}
Existing WGV learning tools supply the interventions used by Scientific Improver: forward Sleep simulation from non-evaluation molecular structures, real-spectrum Wake pseudo-labeling under source-specific evidence gates, motif/ring vocabulary expansion, Canonical Hybrid structure-supervised training of \(G\), and candidate-derived hard-isomer training of \(V\). PR95's historical Gen~1--10 progression records a sequence of human-directed or scripted model/algorithm configurations, not ten independently completed autonomous scientific-agent experiments.

For controlled RSI, let the generation state be \(\mathcal S_t=(\mathcal K_t,G_t,V_t;W)\), where \(\mathcal K_t\) is the MMA/chemical knowledge state and \(W\) is fixed in the initial evaluation. From a newly evaluated TRAIN FIT cohort, we obtain a diagnostic packet \(D_t\) distinguishing true-structure proposal misses, candidates present but incorrectly ranked, and where supported, the chemical evidence deficits associated with these cases. Given \(D_t\) and a persistent history \(\mathcal M_t\) of previous hypotheses, actions and measured outcomes, the Scientific Improver selects
\[
a_t=\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}(D_t,\mathcal M_t),\qquad
a_t\in\{\mathrm{EXPAND\_KNOWLEDGE},\,\mathrm{IMPROVE\_GENERATOR},\,\mathrm{IMPROVE\_VERIFIER},\,\mathrm{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}\}.
\]
The action is accompanied by a falsifiable diagnosis, supporting training-only case identifiers, an expected improvement metric and a predicted direction. The language model selects only an available, typed intervention; model execution and chemical validity remain with the existing WGV tools. The \(\mathrm{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}\) action requests further TRAIN analysis without claiming a trained candidate model.

Each accepted action may update \(\mathcal K_t\), \(G_t\) or \(V_t\), using the same frozen source-defined evaluation protocol before and after the intervention. The episode stores the input model lineage, selected action, training output, measured changes in raw candidate reach and final Exact@1, reflection and next-generation state. A completed episode's reflection can condition the following scientific decision through \(\mathcal M_{t+1}\); this is memory-mediated agent adaptation, not parameter fine-tuning of the underlying LLM. Genuine recursive evaluation requires recomputing the TRAIN FIT observation using the promoted state before the next action; replay of an unchanged diagnosis packet is not evidence of evolving scientific decision-making. Source-bound real dark-spectral evaluations assess whether any resulting gains manifest as additional defensible molecular structural information.

Matched evaluation compares autonomous agent choice with unchanged WGV, an identical fixed intervention and scripted failure routing; agent-memory ablation separates benefit from persistent experience. Valid comparisons use common initial checkpoints, equivalent candidate-generation settings, the same independent evaluation molecules, and measured training/GPU cost or an explicit unmatched-cost qualification. Updates are promoted only after completed evaluations. In the current PR96 implementation, the Scientific Improver and episode controller are executable code, but real multi-generation Taiji outcomes have not yet been established and therefore remain \methodtodo{prospectively measured multi-generation Agentic RSI results, the complete source and checkpoint ledger, ablation of agent memory and actual GPU-hour receipts}.

\subsection*{Structural resolution, calibration and selective interpretation}
Structure confidence is represented at several levels: putative unique connectivity, bounded alternative connectivity set, supported common core/scaffold, elemental composition or structural class, and unresolved/out-of-support. Thresholds for assigning these categories are selected on an independent known-identity calibration set, then frozen before dark-spectral interpretation. Empirical coverage refers to whether the benchmark true structure is retained by each proposed structural statement; confidence scores without truth containment are not treated as calibrated identification probabilities. Candidate-set size, formula ambiguity, unmodelled ion chemistry and detector/instrument limitations are recorded for every failed or downgraded inference. A calibration model may prefer a bounded isomer set to an unjustified single-molecule prediction.

\subsection*{Dark-spectral entity construction and independent recurrence}
Source-verified scans are associated with precursor ion/adduct and experimental spectrum information. Compatible collision energies and repeated injections may inform the same structural entity, but are retained as distinct observations with their original acquisition IDs. Spectral communities are derived from measured precursor/fragment matching with a fixed, source-audited recurrence criterion. A community present in multiple studies does not automatically have one unique molecular precursor or one unique chemical family. Whole-study controls, source overlap, technical-repeat removal and study-appropriate nulls are used to assess recurrence independently from model-assigned chemical structure.

\subsection*{Structure-supported chemical families and reference relationships}
A supported candidate family requires evidence for a shared graph-level chemical relationship. Family membership is represented by identified candidate molecular graphs, bounded alternative sets or a common supported core as dictated by spectral evidence. Graph-edit or substructure relations must specify atom/bond differences and molecular standardization. Comparisons to known metabolite structures use frozen chemical databases, and matched null models account for molecular formula, size, scaffold and the generator's own source prior. Structural differences are not equated with enzyme-catalysed reactions or confirmed biosynthetic pathways. Source and literature recognition checks distinguish known chemistry, unrecognized family-level relationships, candidate unreported structures and insufficient evidence.

\subsection*{Independent spectral evidence and optional orthogonal validation}
Prioritized molecular-family examples are selected using frozen evidence and recurrence criteria before inspection of later validation outcomes. Independent public MS/MS, compatible multienergy spectra and truly measured MSn ancestry are evaluated using complete precursor/adduct and study provenance. Candidate-specific fragment predictions are compared with measured ions and plausible alternative molecular graphs. Authentic standards, retention/coelution and other orthogonal information contribute stronger confirmation only when corresponding experimental records exist. Failed, ambiguous or unavailable validations remain in the prespecified outcome set. The study does not require disease association, phenotype modelling or functional inference to establish chemically meaningful structural hypotheses.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis and comparative experiments}
Primary structural performance is Exact@1/10 and candidate-pool reach for connectivity groups, accompanied by formula accuracy, valid candidate fraction, top-\(K\) structural similarity and computational cost. Molecular-group bootstrap confidence intervals account for multiple spectra representing the same reference structure. Known-formula, macro-core-assisted and database-fixed-pool tasks are presented separately from target-free generation. The critical controlled comparisons hold the experimental query, initial candidates and resource budgets fixed while changing MMA information, \(W/V\) ranking or new learning experiences. For source-scale dark-matter results, units are distinct spectral entities or structurally supported families with original spectra and independent studies traceable separately. Unavailable sources and unresolved chemical identities remain in denominators.
