\begin{orbitabstract}
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Untargeted metabolomics reveals extensive chemical diversity, yet most tandem mass spectra lack confident molecular assignments, leaving their structural relationships largely inaccessible. Here we introduce ORBIT-MS, a framework for elucidating reference-unmatched spectra through reusable chemical knowledge and spectrum-specific fragmentation evidence. A motif knowledge resource and mass-matched substructure alignment guide chemically constrained molecular graph construction, while structure-conditioned fragmentation and spectral verification distinguish competing connectivities. Information-guided learning integrates paired observations, unpaired molecular structures, unlabeled spectra and generated hypotheses; an evidence-guided scientific agent selects targeted updates from observed failures. Applied to \pending{r1-01} reference-unmatched spectral entities, ORBIT-MS recovers \pending{abstract-structural-assignments} molecular connectivities or bounded isomer assignments and reveals \pending{family-relationship-count} structure-supported molecular-family relationships. Across successive learning updates, \pending{r3-12} additional molecular structures become recoverable, and \pending{independent-corroborated-anchors} representative hypotheses receive independent spectral or chemical corroboration. These results connect metabolomic dark matter to interpretable, testable molecular chemistry.
\end{orbitabstract}

\noindent{\footnotesize\color{orbitmuted}\textit{Draft notation: \(x\) marks pending measurements or chemical identifiers; sentences containing \(x\) describe planned outcomes.}}\par

Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) reveals the small-molecule composition of biological and environmental samples, yet many measurements in untargeted metabolomics lack confident structural assignments.\cite{daSilva2015,Alseekh2021} Shared spectral libraries and public repositories have exposed a broad landscape of chemistry awaiting interpretation.\cite{Wang2016GNPS} Recovering a molecular connectivity, a bounded set of isomers or a shared structural core would reveal relationships obscured by missing annotations. The challenge is to determine which structural information each spectrum supports and how that information can aid the interpretation of other molecules.

Computational methods constrain molecular identity through formulas, fingerprints and chemical classes,\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Goldman2023} while fragmentation models connect proposed structures to expected spectra.\cite{Wang2021CFMID} Self-supervised learning extracts molecular representations from unannotated measurements.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams} Generative models propose structures beyond reference databases, and recent search methods iteratively refine hypotheses for individual spectra.\cite{Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX,Manjrekar2026FOAM,Acikalin2026GEMS} These advances motivate a further question: can inference failures guide changes in chemical knowledge and learned models that improve subsequent predictions across molecules?

Two distinct failures limit structure elucidation: the correct molecular graph may be absent from the generated pool, or it may be present but misranked among isomers sharing fragment masses. These are proposal and discrimination failures, not interchangeable error rates. Furthermore, a chemical prior can improve ranking without adding spectrum-specific evidence. The deeper question is whether observed fragments constrain candidate-specific substructures that can later be reused to recover another molecule, beyond simply generating more candidates or favouring familiar chemistry.\cite{Stravs2022,Schymanski2014} This requires tracing experimental peaks, alternative molecular graphs and molecular identities through learning and inference.

Here we introduce ORBIT-MS (Fig.~\ref{fig:orbit_method}), a framework for recursive chemical learning from tandem mass spectra. A spectrum-conditioned generator constructs molecular graphs from atoms, rings and reusable motifs linked to fragment-ion hypotheses by mass-matched substructure alignment. A fragmentation model predicts fragment structures from each proposed precursor without observing the measured target fragment peaks; a verifier compares those predictions with the experimental spectrum. Separating construction from spectral discrimination allows the system to distinguish failures to generate the correct structure from failures to rank available candidates.

Recursive learning offers a test of this transfer hypothesis. The Scientific Improver chooses a bounded update to the chemical knowledge resource, generator or verifier in response to diagnosed errors, and retains its measured outcome before selecting the next action. Two effects must be distinguished: the molecular model gains capabilities through training, while the decision policy adapts through experience. We evaluate both by following independent molecular identities through successive updates and comparing the agent with fixed or scripted interventions at matched starting states and budgets.

We evaluate ORBIT-MS through five connected analyses. A source-linked landscape of reference-unmatched spectra establishes the structural information available for elucidation. Controlled benchmarks quantify the contributions of motifs and fragmentation evidence, distinguishing inference with predicted formulas from inference with supplied formulas. Successive learning generations test the effects of targeted updates and retained decision experience. Molecular-graph comparisons then describe relationships among supported hypotheses, and independent spectral and chemical evidence assesses representative assignments. The framework links each learning decision to its consequences for molecular recovery and chemical interpretation.

\subsection*{Structurally resolving the reference-unmatched metabolome}

We established a source-audited experimental population for structural elucidation from the UC, IBD200 and VEO-IBD collections (Fig.~\ref{fig:dark_landscape}a). Of 348 expected acquisition files, 272 were verified and 76 were unavailable. The verified acquisitions yielded 835,953 QC-eligible biological MS/MS queries: 13,884 received an unambiguous accepted reference identity, 14,289 an ambiguous accepted identity, 613,331 lacked an accepted reference match and 194,449 were unassessable under the frozen matching protocol. These counts describe spectral queries, with independent chemical entities defined in the subsequent analysis. The broader six-repository inventory preserves additional source coverage separately from the verified acquisition and annotation totals of this three-study population.

Reference-unmatched spectra formed 2,255 communities recurring in at least two study collections, including 98 seen in all three (Fig.~\ref{fig:dark_landscape}b). The degree-preserving conditional topology comparison returned an empirical upper-tail probability of 0.91. This recurrence establishes reproducible measured signals, not enriched chemical families. The structural test instead begins with the source scan, precursor/adduct and fragment peaks: do the same graph-level relationships survive independent acquisitions after removing redundant files and alternative ion forms? These communities provide candidates for that test.

\subsection*{Fragment evidence enables molecular construction and discrimination}

We tested whether fragment-aligned motifs provide transferable constraints on molecular construction rather than merely additional chemical seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}a). Generator \(G\) builds valid candidate graphs through atom, ring and motif operations; World \(W\) predicts structure-linked fragment hypotheses, and Verifier \(V\) compares them with measured peaks. Candidate reach measures whether the target graph is generated, whereas Exact@1/10 measures whether it is ranked correctly. Comparing both endpoints on the same molecular cases separates new structural capability from improved candidate ordering.

Mass-matched substructure alignment (MMA) improved graph construction in a strict same-checkpoint comparison on 200 held-out calibration molecules with the reference formula provided (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}b). Disabling MMA yielded 1.50\% Exact@1, 4.50\% Exact@10 and 6.50\% candidate reach. Enabling MMA raised these values to 6.00\%, 14.50\% and 19.00\%, respectively, while teacher-forced top-action accuracy increased from 69.1\% to 76.0\%. Combining atom-normalized search with motif-reserved expansion in the Canonical Hybrid generator further achieved 12.50\% Exact@1, 21.50\% Exact@10 and 22.50\% reach in the reported 200-molecule calibration progression. These measurements quantify connectivity recovery conditional on a supplied molecular formula.

Fragment-aware actions also shortened feasible molecular-construction paths. In a ground-truth-conditioned analysis, canonical atom order and multi-atom ring/motif operations reduced the mean trajectory from 32.4 to approximately 24.8 decisions. Motif actions were applicable to 87\% of the analysed molecules and attached a mean of 4.5 heavy atoms per occurrence. This experiment measures the efficiency of the structural action representation under a known target trajectory and shows how multi-atom operations shorten construction paths.

Holding the candidate pool fixed isolated the contribution of spectral verification (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}c). On the held-out calibration evaluation, \(G\)-only ranking achieved 7.00\% Exact@1 at 16.00\% reach; adding \(W/V\) evidence increased Exact@1 to 8.50\% at the same reach. MMA-seeded starts expanded reach to 19.00\%, while \(G\)-only Exact@1 decreased to 5.50\%; reranking these same candidates with \(W/V\) raised Exact@1 to 10.00\%. The additional proposals therefore increased the demands on discrimination, and spectrum-dependent verification recovered correct rankings within the expanded candidate pool. Within difficult same-formula and same-scaffold strata, fixed-pool \(W/V\) ranking improved Exact@1 by \(\pending{r2-01}\) and \(\pending{r2-02}\) percentage points, respectively, relative to \(G\)-only scores.

Fragment matches have value only when they distinguish competing precursors. In the existing near-isomer challenge, World fragments achieved 81.20\% correct pairwise discrimination; their naive union with MMA motifs reduced this to 80.17\%. Thus adding mass-compatible motifs can dilute structure-specific evidence. We will hold a candidate-isomer pool fixed, exchange the matched spectrum with a precursor-compatible peer spectrum and identify which observed ions change candidate preference. In \(\pending{r2-03}\) source-verified cases, actual competing graphs and diagnostic measured fragments will show where evidence localizes a connectivity difference (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}d). This distinguishes genuine spectral information from a prior preference for common structures.

In a full-population development snapshot covering all 17,556 MassSpecGym TEST spectra and 2,998 unique molecules,\cite{MassSpecGym2024} partial-core-assisted generation achieved 1.75\% single-spectrum Exact@1. Neutral-mass consensus achieved 4.84\% Exact@1, with 19.88\% candidate reach. In a separate analysis grouping spectra by known benchmark molecule identity, multienergy inference achieved 7.00\% Exact@1, 10.91\% Exact@10 and 23.65\% reach (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}e). These evaluations quantify partial-core completion under single-spectrum, neutral-mass and identity-assisted aggregation settings.

On the same full TEST population, frozen atom/ring/motif generation with predicted formulas and without supplied partial cores achieved \(\pending{r2-04}\%\) Exact@1, \(\pending{r2-05}\%\) Exact@10 and \(\pending{r2-06}\%\) raw candidate reach, with molecule-group bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals of \([\pending{r2-07},\pending{r2-08}]\%\), \([\pending{r2-09},\pending{r2-10}]\%\) and \([\pending{r2-11},\pending{r2-12}]\%\), respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_evidence}e). Reference-formula recall within the proposed formula hypotheses was \(\pending{r2-13}\%\); inference required \(\pending{r2-14}\) seconds per query and \(\pending{r2-15}\) GPU-hours for the complete population. Calibrated bounded predictions retained the true connectivity in \(\pending{r2-16}\%\) of cases at \(\pending{r2-17}\%\) coverage. External partial-core evaluations yielded 16.73\% Exact@1, 31.98\% Exact@10 and 41.82\% reach on 813 NPLIB1/CANOPUS spectra, and 4.64\%, 11.39\% and 16.46\%, respectively, on 237 CASMI 2022 spectra. Formula-hypothesis recall was 85.12\% and 90.30\%, respectively. In the separate homology-assisted, known-formula Macro-Core task, completion from a supplied partial core achieved 49.00\% Exact@1 and 90.00\% reach on 200 calibration molecules. Together, these experiments quantify the distinct contributions of fragment-informed construction, fixed-pool discrimination and conditional structural support.

\subsection*{Agent-guided recursive self-improvement expands the recoverable chemical space}

The key test of recursive chemical learning is whether evidence from one failed structure elucidation improves recovery of a different molecule. Missing candidate graphs motivate motif coverage or generator changes; reachable but misranked isomers motivate verifier updates. ORBIT-MS supplies the Scientific Improver with fresh TRAIN FIT candidate pools, chemical errors and fragment evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}a). The agent chooses one available intervention---expand knowledge, update \(G\), update \(V\) or collect evidence---and predicts which independent recovery endpoint should improve. This makes recursive learning a falsifiable chemical-transfer experiment rather than an account of successive model versions.

The historical development sequence established the chemical resources available to these interventions. World-simulated spectra of non-evaluation structures supplied structure--spectrum supervision and reusable fragment/motif records; generator rollouts supplied construction failures, alternative connectivities and hard isomer examples. The MMA resource grew from approximately 4.23 million TRAIN-derived entries to 12.98 million after HMDB-based simulation, followed by a multi-source version containing approximately 31.11 million entries. These human-directed or scripted development configurations addressed long construction trajectories, knowledge coverage, complex ring closure and candidate discrimination. These resources supply the intervention repertoire for controlled autonomous evaluation.

Each autonomous episode executes the chosen intervention and re-evaluates the same independent molecular cases, recording newly reached, newly rank-one and forgotten identities. To demonstrate transfer, an acquired motif must be traced from its source spectrum or training trajectory to an executed graph action on a different target and that target's changed recovery. Reconstruction of the original source molecule alone is not evidence of new capability. The evaluated model and its failures then produce a fresh FIT diagnosis, while the prior decision and measured outcome enter episode memory. World \(W\) and the agent language model remain fixed, separating chemical-model improvement from adaptation of scientific decisions.

Across \pending{rsi-generations} autonomous generations, ORBIT-MS recovered \(\pending{r3-01}\) previously unresolved unique molecular connectivities at least once, representing \(\pending{r3-02}\%\) of the \(\pending{r3-03}\) known-structure molecule groups unresolved by the initial system (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}b). Counting each connectivity at its first recovery, \(\pending{r3-04}\) gains arose from newly reaching the reference graph and \(\pending{r3-05}\) from correctly ranking an already reachable structure. Candidate reach increased from \(\pending{r3-06}\%\) to \(\pending{r3-07}\%\), and Exact@1 from \(\pending{r3-08}\%\) to \(\pending{r3-09}\%\). At the final generation, \(\pending{r3-10}\) newly recovered connectivities remained correct and \(\pending{r3-11}\) initially correct connectivities were lost, giving a net increase of \(\pending{r3-12}\) correctly recovered molecule groups. Gains spanned \(\pending{r3-13}\) structural classes, with class-specific net changes ranging from \(\pending{r3-14}\) to \(\pending{r3-15}\) groups and losses retained in the corresponding denominators.

Recursive updates increased the supported resolution of \(\pending{r3-16}\) experimental reference-unmatched entities (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}b). Among \(\pending{r3-17}\) independently defined entities evaluated across generations, \(\pending{r3-18}\) progressed from formula/class assignment to a shared scaffold, \(\pending{r3-19}\) from scaffold to bounded isomer alternatives, and \(\pending{r3-20}\) from bounded alternatives to putative unique 2D connectivity. Frozen calibration and independent supporting spectral evidence upheld \pending{dark-supported-upgrades} of these upgrades (\(\pending{r3-21}\%\)), while \(\pending{r3-22}\) entities retained or returned to a lower-resolution assignment. These transitions measure changes in defensible molecular information for source-linked observations.

Matched-budget trajectories separated adaptive intervention choice from the effects of repeated training (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive_space}e). Starting from the same checkpoints, the agent-guided path retained \(\pending{r3-23}\) newly recovered unique structures at the final generation, compared with \(\pending{r3-24}\) for the strongest fixed update schedule and \(\pending{r3-25}\) for scripted failure routing; the frozen system recovered \(\pending{r3-26}\) structures in total, compared with \(\pending{r3-27}\) after autonomous updating. The paired difference in retained newly recovered molecule groups at the final generation (agent minus fixed) was \pending{rsi-gain-over-fixed}, with a molecule-group 95\% confidence interval of \([\pending{r3-28},\pending{r3-29}]\). At measured costs of \(\pending{r3-30}\), \(\pending{r3-31}\) and \(\pending{r3-32}\) GPU-hours, the autonomous, fixed and scripted paths recovered \(\pending{r3-33}\), \(\pending{r3-34}\) and \(\pending{r3-35}\) additional structures per GPU-hour, respectively. On independent transfer structures, the corresponding gains were \(\pending{r3-36}\), \(\pending{r3-37}\) and \(\pending{r3-38}\) molecule groups. Removing episode memory reduced additional recovery from \(\pending{r3-39}\) to \(\pending{r3-40}\) groups under matched decision states, linking retained experimental outcomes to subsequent intervention quality.

The existing learning streams made complementary contributions: matched Sleep, Wake and Hindsight updates recovered \(\pending{r3-41}\), \(\pending{r3-42}\) and \(\pending{r3-43}\) additional molecule groups, respectively, and their combination recovered \(\pending{r3-44}\). \subsection*{Previously unresolved spectra reveal molecular-family relationships}

The proposed \(\pending{r4-01}\) molecular families test whether reusable fragment evidence supports chemical relationships across independently measured precursors (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}a). Family nodes represent candidate connectivities, bounded isomer sets or common cores according to the evidence. Edges require explicit atom/bond differences and supporting measured peaks, rather than nearby spectral embeddings or mass shifts alone. A family relation becomes especially informative when the same structural difference is supported in a different study or chemical class, rather than being inherited from the generator's favoured scaffold.

\section*{Discussion}

ORBIT-MS connects molecular structure generation, experimentally evaluated fragment evidence and recursive chemical learning. Its central premise is that unresolved predictions can reveal what a structure-elucidation system needs to learn next. A missing candidate, an incorrectly ranked isomer and an inadequate chemical motif resource motivate different interventions. Linking these diagnoses to measured updates provides a route to extending the range of molecular structures accessible from tandem spectra.

The component experiments establish the complementarity of fragment-informed construction and spectral verification. Reusable motifs improve candidate reach, while World/Verifier evidence improves selection within fixed candidate pools. The near-isomer comparison also shows that accumulating fragment matches alone can reduce discrimination. These observations motivate a Scientific Improver that chooses learning operations according to the current bottleneck. Recursive learning combines changes in reusable chemical knowledge and model capability with changes in subsequent scientific decisions through accumulated experience. The underlying language model can remain fixed while both processes adapt.

The chemical output is a structured interpretation of the reference-unmatched spectral record: explicit resolution levels, relationships among supported molecular graphs and representative hypotheses assessed with independent measurements. A shared scaffold or bounded isomer set can guide further identification even when a unique connectivity remains unresolved. Source-linked molecular relationships provide a resource for subsequent metabolomics and biological studies; interpreting their biological roles requires additional experiments. The relevant measure of progress is therefore newly recoverable chemical information and independent support, together with the structures and distinctions that remain unresolved.

Several limitations guide further development. Precursor formulas can be ambiguous, finite candidate searches can miss the correct graph, and shared fragmentation can leave isomers indistinguishable. Public spectra may also lack acquisition metadata or suitable independent references. Retaining bounded assignments and unresolved cases makes these limitations explicit. Additional collision energies, multistage mass spectrometry, ion mobility and authentic standards can be targeted to the specific structural alternatives that need discrimination.

Recursive chemical learning connects these measurement and modeling needs in a continuing cycle: unresolved spectra motivate molecular hypotheses, their failures identify useful learning experiences, and the updated system revisits chemical space. Its value rests on molecular recovery, interpretable chemical relationships and independent corroboration across successive learning cycles.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary units}
ORBIT-MS is assessed as a tandem-spectrum structure-elucidation system and a tool for studying chemically unresolved public MS/MS. Experimental spectral queries, unique precursor connectivity groups, distinct spectral entities and molecular-family relationships are separate counting units. Molecular predictions use available measured precursor and fragment information; a predicted structure is not an experimental molecular identity. The main outcomes are structure-level candidate recovery on known-identity evaluation sets, calibrated structural-resolution assignment on reference-unmatched spectra, supported chemical-family relations and independent experimental corroboration. \methodtodo{Freeze a complete figure-to-source table, final model checkpoint, public data snapshot, spectral entity definition and confirmatory outcome thresholds.}

\subsection*{Experimental spectra, source accounting and reference annotation}
Each query contains measured precursor \(m/z\), product-ion \(m/z\) and intensity, observed ion/adduct information when available and acquisition context supported by its original source. The three-study source audit uses UC, IBD200 and VEO-IBD collections and records expected files, verified and unavailable acquisitions, eligible biological MS/MS and mutually exclusive accepted, ambiguous, unmatched and unassessable reference statuses. Acquisition aliases, conversions, repeated spectra, collision energies, technical repeats and cross-repository redepositions are tracked without treating all records as independent molecules or biological samples. A separate ongoing pan-repository inventory spans GNPS/MassIVE, MetaboLights, Metabolomics Workbench, NORMAN/DSFP, MetaboBank and MB-POST; its public index counts are not pooled with verified physical MS2 counts.

Reference matching is re-executable from source-linked experimental scans, precursor/product-ion peak arrays and dated GNPS/MassBank/MoNA library snapshots using recorded mass tolerances, adduct rules, peak matching and acceptance thresholds. A reference-unmatched status means that no candidate passed this particular reference protocol, not that the structure is absent from all chemical databases. The source identifiers and annotation dispositions remain attached to all inferred structures. \methodtodo{Freeze release-wide spectrum/reference matching source data, complete missing-file dispositions and the exact number of deduplicated representative dark-spectral entities.}

\subsection*{Molecular identities and benchmark partitions}
Connectivity identity is evaluated using the first 14 characters of a canonical InChIKey. This identifies 2D graph connectivity under the chosen standardization and does not demonstrate stereochemical identity. Known-structure benchmarks retain fixed molecule-group partitions and the complete declared eligible denominator; confidence intervals resample molecular groups rather than treating repeated spectra as independent structures. Historical MassSpecGym TRAIN-derived development partitions contain 175,548 fitting spectra, 9,782 calibration spectra and 8,401 repeatedly inspected search-development spectra out of 193,731 eligible TRAIN spectra. These results remain distinguished from official MassSpecGym TEST evaluation. Target-free inference does not read target molecular formulas, target structures or target fingerprints; analyses providing a reference formula, supplied candidate set or partial Macro-Core are reported as separate conditional tasks. The full-cohort partial-core results use the 18 November 2026 multi-regime development summary with 17,556 spectra and 2,998 molecule groups, consolidating generation-specific candidate records. Single-spectrum prediction, consensus grouped by measured neutral mass, and multienergy aggregation by known benchmark molecule identity are reported separately. These aggregation conditions are retained in the figure source data and are distinct from atom/ring/motif generation with predicted formulas and without supplied partial cores.

\subsection*{Chemical novelty, benchmark grouping and prior knowledge access}
Chemical novelty is defined relative to the actual molecular structures accessible in paired training, structure-only pretraining, MMA indexing and inference, rather than only the absence of the query spectrum from a reference library. In addition to the official molecule-disjoint MassSpecGym benchmark, the planned analyses distinguish spectrum-random (S0), connectivity-identity-disjoint (S1), scaffold-disjoint (S2), official MCES-disjoint (S3), pretraining-aware structural novelty (S4), and external/study/temporal transfer (S5) evaluations. Scaffold distance and MCES distance are different criteria, not a presumed monotonic sequence of difficulty. Exact 2D connectivity is standardized with the first 14 canonical InChIKey characters; constitutional identity is not equated with stereochemical identity.

The source-access conditions are tracked separately: K0 contains only paired training structures and evidence; K1 excludes test identities and prespecified close structural neighbours from external pretraining and retrieval resources; K2 excludes exact test identities while allowing structurally related external chemistry; and K3 adds recursively acquired experiences to a declared K1 or K2 base. K3 is an update condition, not a new novelty metric. The manifest records the source snapshot, deduplication standard, permitted test-time formula and partial-core information, and the set of all test identities queried against each chemical library. The existing official TEST results remain valid as originally labelled experiments; experiments requiring a new training split or a filtered external corpus remain \methodtodo{prospective S0--S5/K0--K3 matched evaluations with fixed model and library manifests}.

\subsection*{Direct precursor-composition hypotheses}
Precursor elemental-composition hypotheses are inferred from the observed precursor mass, ion/adduct context and permissible fragment evidence. Formula correctness and Top-\(K\) formula recall are measured separately from molecular graph recovery. Chemical validity filters enforce allowed atom inventory, charges, valence, connectivity and precursor/adduct mass consistency. A missing true formula is counted explicitly as a composition-stage failure. Molecular fingerprints are not used to generate, route, score or guide structural proposals in the active target-free pipeline; post-inference Morgan/Tanimoto values are external similarity metrics only.

\subsection*{Fragmentation World and observation-conditioned Verifier}
For a proposed precursor graph, the current World \(W\) constructs a finite set of connected, structurally represented fragment and ion hypotheses, including the source-validated up-to-three-cut candidate support, hydrogen adjustments and permitted charge/proton carriers. It assigns fragment prior weights using the molecular graph and available acquisition context, not the measured target MS/MS fragment-peak list during forward prediction. A proposed fragment structure is a conditional model hypothesis, not a directly observed gas-phase intermediate or a proved electron-flow mechanism.

Verifier \(V\) compares those structural fragment hypotheses with measured product-ion masses and intensities to produce observation-conditioned evidence for the proposed precursor. Evidence is aggregated at the precursor-candidate level using recorded weights and candidate support. The distinction between common fragment masses and candidate-specific structural evidence is explicit. Fixed-support W/V tests, same-formula structural-decoy tests and precursor-compatible peer/shuffled-spectrum controls assess whether the experimental spectrum contributes discrimination beyond structural popularity or candidate priors. \methodtodo{Source-freeze the release W/V checkpoints, exact support representation, scoring coefficients and fragment-to-candidate aggregation.}

\subsection*{Generator, MMA and chemically constrained construction}
Generator \(G\) follows the current PR93/PR95 Peak-Ladder and Canonical Hybrid graph-construction implementation. Given measured MS/MS and admissible precursor/formula hypotheses, it uses chemically legal atom, ring and MMA motif starts/attachments with canonical trajectory ordering, atom-normalized beam scoring and motif-reserved expansion. MMA consists of source-linked reusable fragment/motif records derived from training structures and supported W-simulated structure–spectrum pairs. Graph construction is governed by chemical validity, not by a hidden target graph at inference time. The current optional Macro-Core procedure retrieves a non-evaluation structural homologue, removes one or more heavy-atom components and lets \(G\) complete the remaining graph; this is explicitly labelled homology-assisted completion and is not described as pure de novo generation.

Fixed-pool terminal \(W/V\) reranking changes only the ordering of already generated molecular candidates. Whenever enabled in a selected inference variant, intermediate \(W/V\) evidence influencing future \(G\) actions is separately identified as online guidance. The benchmark reports raw reach before ranking, Exact@1/10 after ranking, generated candidate count, invalid proposals and elapsed inference time. Differences between empty-graph, motif-seeded and homology-assisted modes are not pooled into a single undeclared metric.

\subsection*{Mass-matched substructure alignment and molecular construction experience}
Given measured precursor and product-ion \(m/z\), \(G\) forms admissible composition hypotheses consistent with the selected ion/adduct and experimental tolerance. MMA accesses reusable source-linked fragment and motif graph records through mass/formula-compatible hypotheses, then aligns those substructures with the evolving candidate graph and available spectrum-conditioned construction context. This converts an ion signal into a potential chemically executable operation only after enforcing atom inventory, allowed charges, valence, ring closure, attachment compatibility and remaining-precursor composition. Different peaks can be compatible with shared fragments or competing structural motifs; mass matching alone is not taken to prove a particular substructure.

The Peak-Ladder and Canonical Hybrid construction policy can initiate graph hypotheses from atoms, rings or mass-matched motifs and extend them through legal edits. Canonical ordering, atom-normalized beam ranking and motif-reserved expansions control differences in action granularity. For a completed candidate, structure-conditioned \(W\) supplies fragmentation hypotheses and measured-spectrum \(V\) evaluates their consistency. Intermediate W/V guidance, where used, affects subsequent generation and is distinguished from terminal fixed-pool reranking. MMA provenance links source spectra, experimental or simulated fragment hypotheses, motif matching and executed graph actions to the independent target's candidate reach; the appearance of a motif in a lookup table alone does not count as transferred recovery. \methodtodo{Freeze the exact release MMA matching tolerances, motif-scoring implementation, structural attachment masks and online versus terminal W/V aggregation coefficients.}

\subsection*{Sleep, Wake and Hindsight chemical experience}
Sleep begins from non-evaluation molecular graphs, generates structure-derived fragmentation observations with a frozen World and contributes source-linked synthetic structure--spectrum supervision and fragment/motif records. Wake begins from real but unlabeled experimental spectra; pseudo-label candidates enter an update only after prespecified formula, chemical validity and fragment-support evidence gates, with original acquisition and spectrum identifiers retained. Hindsight reuses chemically legal candidate alternatives, graph-construction failures and counterfactual proposals to produce corrective generator/verifier experiences, optionally paired with W-simulated evidence. A generated label is an optimization target with a recorded source and confidence, not an independently established experimental identity.

The experimental unit for studying learning is the independent target molecular connectivity, not the number of new motif-library entries. To distinguish the contributions of these learning sources, the planned controlled comparison holds \(W\), starting \(G/V\) checkpoints, allowable external chemistry and evaluated targets fixed while enabling Sleep, Wake, Hindsight and their combination. Acquisition by spectral entropy, structural scarcity or failure state is compared with random experience selection under explicit experience and computational budgets. All existing historical updates remain in the manuscript; \methodtodo{matched-budget learning-stream and selection-policy ablations with per-structure gains/losses are pending}.

\subsection*{Agent-directed recursive self-improvement and chemical experience}
Existing WGV learning tools supply the interventions used by Scientific Improver: forward Sleep simulation from non-evaluation molecular structures, real-spectrum Wake pseudo-labeling under source-specific evidence gates, motif/ring vocabulary expansion, Canonical Hybrid structure-supervised training of \(G\), and candidate-derived hard-isomer training of \(V\). PR95's historical Gen~1--10 progression records a sequence of human-directed or scripted model/algorithm configurations, not ten independently completed autonomous scientific-agent experiments.

For controlled RSI, let the generation state be \(\mathcal S_t=(\mathcal K_t,G_t,V_t;W)\), where \(\mathcal K_t\) is the MMA/chemical knowledge state and \(W\) is fixed in the initial evaluation. From a newly evaluated TRAIN FIT cohort, we obtain a diagnostic packet \(D_t\) distinguishing true-structure proposal misses, candidates present but incorrectly ranked, and where supported, the chemical evidence deficits associated with these cases. Given \(D_t\) and a persistent history \(\mathcal M_t\) of previous hypotheses, actions and measured outcomes, the Scientific Improver selects
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_t &= \pi_{\mathrm{agent}}(D_t,\mathcal M_t),\\
a_t &\in\{\mathrm{EXPAND\_KNOWLEDGE},\,\mathrm{IMPROVE\_GENERATOR},\\
    &\qquad \mathrm{IMPROVE\_VERIFIER},\,\mathrm{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The action is accompanied by a falsifiable diagnosis, supporting training-only case identifiers, an expected improvement metric and a predicted direction. The language model selects only an available, typed intervention; model execution and chemical validity remain with the existing WGV tools. The \(\mathrm{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}\) action requests further TRAIN analysis without claiming a trained candidate model.

Each accepted action may update \(\mathcal K_t\), \(G_t\) or \(V_t\), using the same frozen source-defined evaluation protocol before and after the intervention. The episode stores the input model lineage, selected action, training output, measured changes in raw candidate reach and final Exact@1, reflection and next-generation state. A completed episode's reflection can condition the following scientific decision through \(\mathcal M_{t+1}\); this is memory-mediated agent adaptation, not parameter fine-tuning of the underlying LLM. Genuine recursive evaluation requires recomputing the TRAIN FIT observation using the promoted state before the next action; replay of an unchanged diagnosis packet is not evidence of evolving scientific decision-making. Source-bound real dark-spectral evaluations assess whether any resulting gains manifest as additional defensible molecular structural information.

Matched evaluation compares autonomous agent choice with unchanged WGV, an identical fixed intervention and scripted failure routing; agent-memory ablation separates benefit from persistent experience. Valid comparisons use common initial checkpoints, equivalent candidate-generation settings, the same independent evaluation molecules, and measured training/GPU cost or an explicit unmatched-cost qualification. Updates are promoted only after completed evaluations. In the current PR96 implementation, the Scientific Improver and episode controller are executable code, but real multi-generation Taiji outcomes have not yet been established and therefore remain \methodtodo{prospectively measured multi-generation Agentic RSI results, the complete source and checkpoint ledger, ablation of agent memory and actual GPU-hour receipts}.

\subsection*{Scientific Improver episode execution, promotion and experience memory}
The Scientific Improver implementation provides a bounded selection layer over existing WGV interventions rather than arbitrary source-code generation. Each real episode recomputes a TRAIN FIT diagnostic cohort using the current promoted model state, separates unreached structures from candidate-present misrankings, and supplies the LLM with source-linked candidate evidence, the permitted action set, a resource budget and a bounded history of previous decisions. The action is one of \texttt{EXPAND\_KNOWLEDGE}, \texttt{IMPROVE\_GENERATOR}, \texttt{IMPROVE\_VERIFIER} or \texttt{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}. It includes a falsifiable chemical diagnosis, supporting FIT case identifiers, a target metric and an expected direction of change.

A typed controller dispatches the selected existing training or evidence-gathering stage. Evaluation and candidate-promotion rules are deterministic, and the decision agent does not access official TEST labels for policy selection. An episode ledger stores model/knowledge checkpoint identities, split manifests, intervention specification, measurable candidate reach and ranking changes, resource receipts and a reflection used to condition a later choice. A \texttt{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE} action is recorded as information acquisition rather than a fictitious parameter update or zero-cost performance improvement. Genuine autonomous RSI requires sequential fresh FIT observations and actual promoted WGV states; manually engineered historical generations or unexecuted LLM decisions are analysed separately. The code currently provides the controller and receipts, whereas \methodtodo{completed multi-generation matched Agent/Fixed/Scripted/No-memory trials, validated per-molecule recoveries and actual GPU-hours} remain dedicated experiments.

\subsection*{Structural resolution, calibration and selective interpretation}
Structure confidence is represented at several levels: putative unique connectivity, bounded alternative connectivity set, supported common core/scaffold, elemental composition or structural class, and unresolved/out-of-support. Thresholds for assigning these categories are selected on an independent known-identity calibration set, then frozen before dark-spectral interpretation. Empirical coverage refers to whether the benchmark true structure is retained by each proposed structural statement; confidence scores without truth containment are not treated as calibrated identification probabilities. Candidate-set size, formula ambiguity, unmodelled ion chemistry and detector/instrument limitations are recorded for every failed or downgraded inference. A calibration model may prefer a bounded isomer set to an unjustified single-molecule prediction.

\subsection*{Dark-spectral entity construction and independent recurrence}
Source-verified scans are associated with precursor ion/adduct and experimental spectrum information. Compatible collision energies and repeated injections may inform the same structural entity, but are retained as distinct observations with their original acquisition IDs. Spectral communities are derived from measured precursor/fragment matching with a fixed, source-audited recurrence criterion. A community present in multiple studies does not automatically have one unique molecular precursor or one unique chemical family. Whole-study controls, source overlap, technical-repeat removal and study-appropriate nulls are used to assess recurrence independently from model-assigned chemical structure.

\subsection*{Structure-supported chemical families and reference relationships}
A supported candidate family requires evidence for a shared graph-level chemical relationship. Family membership is represented by identified candidate molecular graphs, bounded alternative sets or a common supported core as dictated by spectral evidence. Graph-edit or substructure relations must specify atom/bond differences and molecular standardization. Comparisons to known metabolite structures use frozen chemical databases, and matched null models account for molecular formula, size, scaffold and the generator's own source prior. Structural differences are not equated with enzyme-catalysed reactions or confirmed biosynthetic pathways. Source and literature recognition checks distinguish known chemistry, unrecognized family-level relationships, candidate unreported structures and insufficient evidence.

\subsection*{Independent spectral evidence and optional orthogonal validation}
Prioritized molecular-family examples are evaluated from original source scans and retained alternative structures. Independent public MS/MS, compatible collision-energy series and genuinely measured MSn ancestry are matched by precursor/adduct and acquisition provenance; a repeated file is not an independent observation. Candidate-specific fragment predictions are compared with measured ions and plausible alternative molecular graphs. Authentic standards, retention/coelution and other orthogonal information contribute stronger confirmation only when corresponding experimental records exist. Failed, ambiguous or unavailable validations remain in the prespecified outcome set. The study does not require disease association, phenotype modelling or functional inference to establish chemically meaningful structural hypotheses.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis and comparative experiments}
Primary structural performance is Exact@1/10 and candidate-pool reach for connectivity groups, accompanied by formula accuracy, valid candidate fraction, top-\(K\) structural similarity and computational cost. Molecular-group bootstrap confidence intervals account for multiple spectra representing the same reference structure. Known-formula, macro-core-assisted and database-fixed-pool tasks are presented separately from target-free generation. The critical controlled comparisons hold the experimental query, initial candidates and resource budgets fixed while changing MMA information, \(W/V\) ranking or new learning experiences. For source-scale dark-matter results, units are distinct spectral entities or structurally supported families with original spectra and independent studies traceable separately. Unavailable sources and unresolved chemical identities remain in denominators.

\subsection*{Reporting of benchmark protocols and independent chemical recovery}
For each benchmark, the release will state the exact cohort and count of spectra, unique standardized molecular connectivities, source-specific acquisition grouping, adduct and formula availability, candidate-set origin and size, model checkpoints, library snapshot and any inter-spectrum or multi-energy aggregation. Candidate-pool Reach is the fraction of queries for which the correct reference connectivity appears anywhere in the generated set; Exact@10 and Exact@1 refer to the corresponding ranked subsets. The three quantities must be computed on the same per-case population when compared as nested outcomes. In particular, database-assisted full-horizon retrieval, zero-overlap partial-core completion, reference-formula-assisted CAL experiments, and homology-provided macro-core experiments are different inference regimes. The source audit records unmatched and unassessable real spectra without imputing structure correctness from model consensus alone.

Where generations are compared, the primary endpoint is the number of independent previously unreached or incorrectly ranked molecule groups newly recovered, together with losses among previously correct groups. Performance is reported both at spectral-query and connectivity-group levels, with molecular-group resampling for uncertainty and explicit evaluation/inference budgets. Structural-resolution improvements in the reference-unmatched metabolome are assessed only through source-linked evidence tiers and independent observations when available; increased rank score or candidate count alone is not treated as an elucidated novel molecule.
