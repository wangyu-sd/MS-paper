\noindent\textbf{
Untargeted metabolomics detects a far broader repertoire of tandem mass spectra than can be assigned confidently to molecular structures, leaving much of the chemistry represented by these signals unresolved. Recurring spectral patterns provide clues, but neither similarity between spectra nor a plausible generated structure establishes a molecular family. Here we introduce ORBIT-MS, which links spectrum-conditioned molecular graph generation to structure-conditioned fragment prediction and verification against measured MS/MS. By evaluating the fragments supported by competing molecular structures, ORBIT-MS connects spectral interpretation to de novo structure elucidation without assuming that every observation admits a unique structural explanation. Applied to \result{source-audited public MS/MS resource and annotation denominator}, the model yields \result{structurally supported, independently recurrent molecular families} and \result{their chemical relationships to characterized metabolites}. We assess \result{held-out structure-elucidation accuracy and calibrated structural resolution}, examine \result{reproducible biological distributions and perturbation responses}, and test selected assignments using \result{independent chemical validation including failed and ambiguous outcomes}. These analyses connect molecular structure elucidation to the systematic study of chemical relationships that conventional spectral annotations leave unresolved.
}

\vspace{1em}

Untargeted mass spectrometry has transformed the measurement of small molecules across organisms, environments and human disease, while much of the experimental tandem-spectral record still lacks an accepted molecular structure assignment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024,daSilva2015} Large reference libraries, molecular networking, elemental-composition analysis, fingerprint prediction and de novo structure generation have expanded chemical annotation.\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Goldman2023} However, the number of unmatched spectra is not a count of unknown compounds: a single molecule can yield multiple adducts, collision-energy spectra, related acquisition files and reproducible spectral patterns. Equally, similarity among unmatched spectra does not establish identical molecular connectivity or a structurally meaningful family.

We instead ask whether previously unrecognized \emph{chemical relationships} can be recovered at scale from these measurements. Establishing such relationships requires more than generating plausible candidate molecules. The precursor formula can remain ambiguous and closely related molecular graphs can produce overlapping fragment masses, so structural assignments must retain alternative connectivities when the experimental evidence does not distinguish them. The experimental spectrum is an incomplete observation of gas-phase fragmentation; neither predicted fragment-mass support nor an absent peak proves a unique fragmentation mechanism.

To turn a plausible structural proposal into a more discriminating chemical test, we developed ORBIT-MS. Its spectrum-conditioned Generator proposes precursor molecular graphs. For each graph, a spectrum-blind World predicts structurally represented fragments and their masses, whereas a Verifier evaluates how the observed MS/MS spectrum supports those predictions. Keeping these operations separate distinguishes fragmentation possibilities implied by a molecular structure from evidence supplied by the measurement itself. Fragment-level evidence can therefore be compared across alternative connectivities and, when incorporated during generation, inform subsequent graph modifications rather than merely reorder completed candidates. Because different structures can account for many of the same ions, fragment agreement is not treated as proof of a unique gas-phase pathway or of an unambiguous precursor identity.

We apply this framework to \result{source-audited public MS/MS collection} to examine the chemical relationships represented by spectra without accepted library annotations. We recover \result{number and structural resolution of independently supported molecular families} and place them in the context of characterized metabolite chemistry, testing whether those relationships recur across independent studies rather than arise from spectral similarity alone. Their distributions across biological sample contexts and controlled perturbations provide \result{replicated biological associations and their uncertainty}, while \result{independent chemical confirmation, failures and surviving structural alternatives} defines the limits of the proposed structures. The resulting unit of discovery is not an individual unmatched spectrum but a molecular relationship that can be supported, reproduced and tested independently.

\section*{Previously unrecognized molecular families in public tandem mass spectra}

We began by establishing the observable experimental universe and the reference-matching status of its spectra, before considering any molecular identities (Fig.~\ref{fig:discovered_families}a). The source inventory distinguishes publicly indexed studies and files, verified acquisition runs, raw MS2 scans, QC-eligible experimental spectra and those assessable against frozen reference libraries. Of 835,953 eligible biological MS2 queries, 13,884 received an unambiguous reference identity, 14,289 had an ambiguous accepted identity, 613,331 had no accepted hit under the frozen protocol, and 194,449 were not assessable. These quantities describe \emph{measured spectra} in this restricted, availability-limited corpus, not distinct compounds or the global public metabolome. \methodtodo{Promote only the finally audited source-bound PR94 scan and reference-match receipts into manuscript Figure 1a and source data.}

\section*{Chemical relationships between recovered families and characterized metabolites}

The existence of recurrent structure-supported families raises a distinct chemical question: are their members related to characterized metabolites by interpretable molecular changes, or do they occupy more remote chemical regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships})? The primary structural relationship graph will place \result{supported recovered-family structures and established reference metabolites} in a common 2D chemical space, with edges grounded in actual atom and bond differences (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}a). Neither nearest-neighbour positions in a spectral embedding nor differences in precursor mass alone constitute a verified structural relationship.

We will measure \result{family-level shortest valid structural distances to frozen characterized-metabolite references and the matched-null distributions}, including an explicit insufficient-resolution population (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}b). This comparison must control for the tendency of seed-bound, database-assisted or known-chemistry-trained generators to propose molecules close to their initial structure priors. Among families whose graphs are adequately supported, we will quantify \result{recurring atom-level structural difference types across independent families, classes and studies} and their uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:chemical_relationships}c,d). \section*{Structural evidence, discrimination and uncertainty in ORBIT-MS}

The molecular-family claims depend on how well the spectrum can discriminate structural alternatives and on whether the retained candidate set contains the true precursor. We therefore assess ORBIT-MS on known-structure data as a separate reliability question, rather than relying on isolated high-scoring examples (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_reliability}). The model integrates a spectrum-conditioned graph Generator \(G\), a structure-conditioned spectrum-blind fragment World \(W\), and a Verifier \(V\) that reweights the same structured fragment objects using measured MS/MS evidence. Its conditional W/V evidence is not a complete, experimentally established gas-phase reaction mechanism; its ability to improve \emph{de novo} structure generation rather than terminal ordering requires matched-budget causal controls.

On 8,401 repeatedly used MassSpecGym TRAIN-derived development spectra, the formula model recovers the true elemental composition in 87.87\% of queries at Top1. The selected conditional W achieves 72.19\% measured-intensity-weighted fragment-mass recall \emph{when given the correct precursor structure}; a learned V achieves 62.79\% matched Top100 weighted fragment recall on supplied W supports. These numbers measure separate conditional tasks and are not integrated structure-elucidation accuracy or official TEST results. Source-bound historical fixed-pool retrieval, graph-generation and chemistry-stratified failures remain detailed in Supplementary Tables~S2--S12.

The principal full-cohort accuracy assessment will quantify \result{candidate-space contraction and native truth containment} while separately reporting proposal misses, candidate-pruning errors and final ranking errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_reliability}a,f). The complete official de novo evaluation is \result{all 17,556 MassSpecGym TEST queries, target-blind Exact@1/10, Raw Reach@K, failure and group-level uncertainty}. A submitted TEST evaluation is not itself a scored result, and the repeatedly consulted training-fold development metrics must not be promoted as held-out system performance.

A successful structural inference method must reject difficult false alternatives for spectrum-specific reasons. The proposed tests include \result{same-formula, same-scaffold and positional-isomer truth-containment distributions} and paired matched-spectrum, peer-spectrum and appropriately shuffled-spectrum controls on \emph{identical} candidate sets and computational budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_reliability}b,c). Comparisons among G-only, identical-pool terminal W/V ranking and online W/V-guided generation are separately required to determine whether fragment evidence contributes to new graph proposals rather than simply reordering known candidates.

The structural evidence analysis will determine \result{how candidate-specific fragments, common peaks, formula and observed multistage ancestry affect false-candidate exclusion} without treating unexplained peaks as chemically impossible (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_reliability}d). Independently calibrated outputs must give \result{known-truth containment at each reported structural-resolution level, the population fraction assigned to that level, and selective risk--coverage intervals} (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_reliability}e). Finally, exact-population failures will be stratified by \result{size, ring complexity, adduct and heteroatom subgroup}, with no exclusion of invalid or difficult molecules (Fig.~\ref{fig:structure_reliability}g).

\section*{Discussion}

The proposed scientific contribution of ORBIT-MS is not a candidate ranking score in isolation. It is the possibility of using experimental fragmentation evidence to define meaningful, bounded precursor-structure relationships at scale, and then asking whether those relationships reveal chemistry and biology that disconnected spectral features obscure. The distinction between molecular prediction and chemically supported family discovery is critical: a plausible generated structure, a reference-library non-match and a recurrent spectral community are different forms of evidence.

Current component-development results support a useful decomposition of composition, conditional fragment hypotheses, structure proposals and evidence-conditioned ranking, but independent official accuracy and calibration remain necessary for strong structure-elucidation claims. The available three-study public analysis documents substantial reference-matching gaps and reproducible spectral similarity without demonstrating a nonrandom three-study topology enrichment under its stated null or identifying new molecules. The family-level landscape, structural novelty, biological effects and authentic-standard outcomes remain \result{evidence-dependent paper results still to be completed and independently validated}. Negative or inconclusive evidence reduces, rather than inflates, the strength of any chemical discovery statement.

The Nature-level argument rests on an integrated experimental chain: full-denominator calibrated inference; structure-supported previously unrecognized relationships; independently repeated observations; controls against model and acquisition biases; biological context; and orthogonal chemistry. The article's accuracy claim and the prominence of its discovery figures depend on whether that chain is ultimately substantiated, not on the complexity of the W/V/G architecture.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and separation of evidence}
ORBIT-MS is evaluated as a molecular structure-inference system with three distinct conditional objectives: molecular candidate generation from measured MS/MS, forward fragment-hypothesis prediction given a proposed precursor structure, and spectrum-conditioned verification of those fragment hypotheses. We distinguish paired structural prediction, candidate-ranking improvements within a fixed pool and genuinely novel structural proposals. Empirical dark-spectral recurrence and biological associations form separate downstream analyses. In particular, a predicted fragment assignment is not an experimentally measured fragment structure, and a spectral-similarity community is not automatically a molecular family. \methodtodo{Freeze confirmatory primary endpoints, all training and inference checkpoints, explicit evaluation budgets, and the promotion criteria for the released W/V/G configuration.}

\subsection*{Experimental MS/MS, molecular identity and split protection}
A query is defined by experimentally measured precursor \(m/z\), observed tandem fragment \(m/z\) and intensity, measured adduct/polarity when available, and acquisition variables supported by the dataset. Benchmark structural identity is evaluated at the first 14 characters of canonical InChIKey, so identical 2D connectivity is distinguished from stereochemical resolution. Spectral input features may include observed peak mass differences but may not include target precursor molecular fingerprints or hidden reference structures.

The audited MassSpecGym TRAIN-derived development corpus contains 193,731 eligible MS2 records partitioned by precursor connectivity into 175,548 parameter-fitting spectra, 9,782 promotion/calibration spectra and 8,401 repeatedly consulted search-development spectra. These partitions are pairwise group-disjoint. Historical results on the 8,401 development cases are descriptive model-development evidence, not a held-out MassSpecGym performance claim. \methodtodo{Freeze the final complete TEST submission, 17,556-case scoring receipt and train/pretrain/reference analogue-leakage checks; report candidate-pool scope for each comparator.}

\subsection*{Precursor-composition inference and graph eligibility}
The primary de novo inference path must not receive a target molecular formula, molecular structure or learned molecular-fingerprint reconstruction of the target as an input. ORBIT-MS uses measured precursor mass, ion context, fragment peaks and permitted acquisition metadata to propose elemental-composition hypotheses. The formula posterior and initial molecular graph are computed without access to the test ground truth. An oracle-formula variant, when used, is reported as a separate conditional upper-bound task and not mixed with standard de novo results. Chemical validity requires transparent checks on atom inventory, formal charge, valence, graph connectivity and precursor/adduct compatibility. Any chemistry or source unsupported by the inference grammar is recorded rather than silently removed from the evaluation denominator.

\subsection*{Spectrum-blind World over structured fragment candidates}
The source-validated baseline World \(W\) receives a proposed precursor graph and acquisition context, not the observed target fragment peaks. Its structural fragment support is constructed from connected heavy-atom subgraphs of that precursor accessible through up to three bond cuts, with explicit hydrogen adjustment and proton/sodium carrier hypotheses. \(W\) assigns candidate weights/probabilities and supplies source-linked fragment objects, their calculated masses and structural provenance. Real MS/MS may supervise training, but cannot enter the World during a target-blind forward prediction. The resulting ion hypotheses do not certify mechanistic electron-flow trajectories, unobserved products or exact collision-induced dissociation reaction paths.

An experimental extension considers additional structurally distinct rearrangement candidates as one competing candidate source for the same World distribution, not as a second independent score appended after the World. Such candidates require auditable chemical structures, ions, charged states and provenance, especially where precursor atom correspondence is uncertain. \methodtodo{Freeze the actual final World support, the corrected joint-candidate training result, selection budget, checkpoint and any demonstrated incremental contribution from new mechanistic candidates before including extension-specific claims.}

\subsection*{Observation-conditioned fragment Verifier}
The Verifier \(V\) receives the actual measured MS/MS alongside the frozen structured fragment candidates and prior weights supplied by \(W\) for a particular proposed precursor. It matches fragment masses and spectral evidence, producing observation-conditioned fragment support that can be pooled into molecular-candidate evidence. Fragment posterior weights are hypotheses conditional on \(W\)'s finite candidate support, not experimentally known fragment-to-atom labels. Unexplained observed peaks and W-support limitations remain measurable uncertainties. V cannot infer the correct precursor connectivity from a ground-truth molecular graph at de novo inference time; known precursor graphs enter only specifically labelled conditional component experiments.

Frozen candidate/support controls distinguish V improvements from expansion of the World fragment universe. Matched-spectrum, mass/adduct-compatible different-molecule peer-spectrum and shuffled-spectrum interventions are required to test whether the observed spectrum supplies discriminative information. \methodtodo{Source-freeze the final V posterior and molecule-level evidence aggregation used with the selected W and G, including error handling when atom mapping is unknown.}

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned molecular Generator}
The Generator \(G\) constructs candidate precursor molecular graphs from the measured spectrum and permissible predicted formula/precursor constraints. Recent implemented generator variants use legal molecular graph modifications (including constrained double-edge swaps) rather than assuming an oracle target structure is present among training-derived seed molecules. Architecture variants include a spectrum-conditioned graph denoiser and a separately learned or estimated corruption-time/stop signal; the final version and checkpoint lineage must be specified, not inferred from historical intermediate training plans.

For any conditional fragment evidence transferred from W/V into G, its structural origin and semantics are explicit. Fragment retention masks, atom-pair observability, or unexplained intensity can provide soft evidence; they are not automatically labels instructing the generator to delete a particular bond. Graph constraints are enforced separately from learned ranking. The correct structure is revealed only after target-blind generation to calculate Exact@K and candidate-pool reach. \methodtodo{Freeze the current supported G architecture (including the final W/V evidence-call schedule, graph initialization, time/stop model, number of generated candidates and failed trajectories) from the final code-release receipts.}

\subsection*{Online evidence, terminal ranking and causal ablations}
For every completed candidate precursor, terminal W/V provides structure-conditioned fragment support and a spectrum-conditioned consistency score for ranking. This does not create additional molecular graphs and cannot repair a target absent from the candidate pool. Online guidance uses evidence from intermediate candidate graphs to affect legal future G transitions when the selected implementation supports it. The two channels are evaluated separately.

The primary causal comparison fixes query spectra, admissible chemical formulas, candidate budgets and evaluation protocol: G without W/V; G with the same previously generated candidate IDs reordered by terminal W/V; and G with online W/V plus terminal verification. Report original and final target-containing candidate-pool reach, ranked accuracy, lost/discarded targets, hard-isomer performance, W/V call counts, wall-clock time and coverage by chemical subgroup. \methodtodo{Freeze coefficients, score normalization and matched-compute definitions from the complete release, and verify the zero-guidance baseline reproduces its fixed parent generator.}

\subsection*{Benchmark metrics and statistical evaluation}
For de novo structure elucidation we report Exact@1/10, Raw Reach@K, formula correctness, valid/unique candidate rate and chemical/structural difficulty strata on the benchmark-defined full test population. Retrieval is a distinct setting with a fixed experimentally permitted pool and whole-query Hit@K. Conditional forward fragmentation is evaluated only when the precursor graph is supplied; observed intensity-weighted fragment-mass coverage and prediction precision do not equal structure-identification accuracy. Spectrum simulation uses the official spectral metrics, including sparse cosine and Jensen--Shannon similarity, with acquisition and candidate support matched where applicable.

Every unsuccessful spectrum or failed generation contributes to the declared denominator, which is stated separately from independent molecule-group counts. Molecular groups, not individual repeated acquisitions of the same reference molecule, define clustered uncertainty for structural inference. No number from TRAIN-derived adaptive SEARCH_DEV is substituted for official TEST. Comparator-specific allowances for known formula, database access or molecular fingerprints are reported explicitly to avoid false like-for-like SOTA claims.

\subsection*{Structural resolution, ambiguity and calibration}
A point estimate is not equivalent to a confirmed 2D identity. We distinguish bounded unique putative structure, isomer-set membership, common structural core, chemical class/formula-only evidence and unresolved/insufficient-support outputs. Proposed structural containment and abstention thresholds require an independent calibration set and frozen operating point before application to unknown public MS/MS. Risk--coverage analyses report ground-truth retention in predicted sets and unsupported cases, not just confidence on successful candidates. \methodtodo{Provide model-independent candidate-set truth containment, calibration holdout, risk--coverage operating point and source-bound intervals before any dark-molecular-family claim.}

\subsection*{Public experimental spectra and reference-library assessment}
The downstream public-resource analysis is deliberately independent of training or invoking W/V/G. It distinguishes primary-source accessions, logical file listings, verified acquisitions, MS2 scans, QC-eligible biological spectra, reference-assessable spectra and individually resolved structural hypotheses. A source-manifested analysis of UC, IBD200 and VEO-IBD scans preserves sample/study provenance and all unavailable acquisitions. Pan-repository inventory extends the discovery scope to GNPS/MassIVE, MetaboLights, Metabolomics Workbench, NORMAN/DSFP, DDBJ MetaboBank and MB-POST, with exact source snapshots and reconciliation of repeated deposits, converted files and unsupported accessions.

A reference match is defined relative to an explicitly frozen set of real GNPS, MassBank, MoNA or other permitted experimental reference spectra and a stated mass/peak matching protocol. Accepted, ambiguous, unmatched and unassessable are disjoint observable statuses; absence from one reference snapshot is not molecular novelty. Previously published public-index totals are cited as published external context rather than converted into newly computed counts. \methodtodo{Final per-repository inclusion, matching and inaccessible-source audit for publication-wide Figure 3 and its data-release provenance.}

\subsection*{Study-independent spectral recurrence}
Observed spectral communities are built using declared precursor/adduct compatibility, measured peak-mass matching, reproducible similarity scores and a community-membership rule with controls against single-linkage chaining. Every accepted observation is linked to an accession, original study, raw acquisition and documented sample wherever available. Technical repeats, multiple collision energies, redepositions and shared samples do not qualify as independent study recurrence. Full-population cross-study edge enumeration, whole-study holdout and blank/QC controls are recorded with their denominators and missingness.

A conditional network-degree-preserving null addresses a specific concentration-of-edges/topology hypothesis; its p value does not test chemical uniqueness or independently prove that spectra arise from the same molecule. The original three-cohort PR94 study is a limited public-source universe and cannot be promoted to a global pan-repository assay count. \methodtodo{Freeze the publication eligibility of PR94's available-source results, global catalog overlap accounting and analysis-unit label in all displayed panels.}

\subsection*{Chemical relationships and recognition status}
Only candidate molecules with sufficient supported graph connectivity enter graph-edit-based family definitions. Known-metabolite reference snapshots, chemical identity normalization and structural-distance measures are frozen before biological analysis. Graph change types are molecular structural differences, not proof of biochemical transformation. Formula-, scaffold-, mass- and proposal-prior-matched nulls control the known-metabolite proximity caused by candidate retrieval or seed construction. Novelty is audited at three levels: unmatched reference spectrum, previously unrecognized family relationship and previously unreported molecular structure, with explicit unresolved category and dated source evidence. \methodtodo{Freeze known chemistry versions, valid graph-edge criteria and family membership/provenance files before constructing Figure 4.}

\subsection*{Biological association and perturbation analysis}
Biological sample/study provenance is audited before assigning sample/tissue/disease labels. Family definitions are frozen independently of their phenotype or sample context, and within-study effects are estimated before cross-study synthesis. Germ-free/gnotobiotic, antibiotic, defined-diet or other intervention datasets require verified study design, known-metabolite positive controls, technical nuisance adjustment, permutation controls, multiplicity correction and independent cohort replication. To test additional value, family-level and anonymous-feature-level analyses use the identical biological samples, covariates and preregistered primary endpoint. Association and perturbation dependence are not sufficient to establish microbial synthesis or enzymatic pathway assignments. \methodtodo{Cohort list, contrast manifest, replicate IDs, positive-control gate and source-bound full-population Figure 5 outcome.}
