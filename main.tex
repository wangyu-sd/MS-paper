\title[ORBIT-MS: Recursive chemical learning]{ORBIT-MS: Recursive chemical learning for molecular structure elucidation from tandem mass spectra}

% Replace draft metadata with verified author names, affiliations and emails.
\author[1]{\fnm{Authors} \sur{to be confirmed}}
\affil[1]{\orgname{Affiliations to be confirmed}}

\abstract{Untargeted tandem mass spectrometry detects a vast repertoire of molecular signals, yet most experimental spectra remain without a confident structural assignment. This metabolomic dark matter is difficult to elucidate because chemically distinct molecules can explain overlapping fragments, while fixed inference models cannot learn from the structural hypotheses they repeatedly fail to generate or distinguish. Here we introduce ORBIT-MS, an evidence-guided, agent-directed recursive self-improvement (RSI) system that couples a spectrum-conditioned molecular Generator, a structure-conditioned fragmentation World and an experimental-spectrum Verifier. A Scientific Improver Agent diagnoses failed inferences, selects targeted chemical-knowledge, generator or verifier updates, and retains the observed consequences as experience for subsequent decisions. In a source-audited three-study resource, 613,331 of 835,953 eligible biological MS/MS queries lacked an accepted reference-spectrum match. Existing known-formula calibration experiments show that fragment knowledge substantially improves molecular candidate reach and that W/V evidence improves ranking within the same candidate pool. We investigate \result{the population distribution of supported structural resolution among reference-unmatched spectra}, \result{new molecular structures and resolution tiers recovered across completed autonomous RSI generations}, and \result{chemically supported relationships connecting unresolved spectra to characterized molecular families}. Independent spectral evidence and, where available, orthogonal chemistry are used to assess the resulting assignments. ORBIT-MS aims to progressively extend the interpretable chemical space of the metabolomic dark matter rather than to assign an unsupported unique structure to every observation.}

\maketitle

\vspace{1em}

Untargeted metabolomics provides a broad view of the small-molecule signals present in biological and environmental samples, but structural interpretation remains a central bottleneck.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024,Alexandrov2026Perspective} Large experimental MS/MS archives contain measurements that are not assigned confidently by existing spectral libraries, and the number of measured spectra greatly exceeds the number of independently characterized molecular structures. Nevertheless, unannotated spectra are not synonymous with undiscovered molecules: different ion species, acquisition conditions and repeated experiments can produce many records for one precursor. Understanding the metabolomic dark matter therefore requires both source-aware measurement accounting and a defensible way to associate fragment evidence with molecular structures.

Established annotation approaches exploit exact-mass constraints, molecular formulas, reference spectra, predicted fingerprints, molecular-network relationships and computational fragmentation.\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Goldman2023,MassSpecGym2024} Spectral foundation models and large-scale experimental data improve representation learning, while molecular generative approaches increase the diversity of plausible structures that can be proposed.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams,Acikalin2026GEMS,Wang2026MetGenX} Yet molecular inference is inherently underdetermined. Distinct connectivities can have identical elemental compositions and overlapping fragment ions; neither a high spectral similarity score nor a plausible generated SMILES alone establishes a particular structure. Conversely, reducing the set of possible structures without removing the true molecular graph requires distinguishing genuinely structure-specific information from fragment evidence shared by many candidates.

We address this difficulty through evidence-guided molecular structure elucidation and agent-directed recursive learning. In ORBIT-MS, a spectrum-conditioned Generator \(G\) proposes candidate molecular graphs, including constructions that reuse fragment and motif knowledge. A structure-conditioned fragmentation World \(W\), which does not observe the measured target fragment peaks during forward inference, supplies fragment hypotheses from each proposed precursor. A Verifier \(V\) evaluates their agreement with measured MS/MS and helps distinguish competing molecular candidates. Crucially, the resulting failures have different chemical meanings: the generator may never reach the true graph, an adequate candidate may be misranked, or the present knowledge resource may lack transferable structural evidence. We use these distinctions to motivate an agentic recursive self-improvement (RSI) loop in which a Scientific Improver proposes an intervention, existing chemical training tools update the shared system and its outcome becomes evidence for the next decision. The agent does not replace the chemical models, and its language-model parameters need not be retrained: recursive improvement occurs in the WGV system and in the experience available to subsequent agent decisions.

We investigate this proposition at three levels. First, we construct an experimentally grounded universe of reference-unmatched spectra and quantify the structural resolution that can be supported. Second, using known-identity benchmarks and source-linked spectral examples, we measure how fragment-informed generation and discrimination determine which molecular graphs are recoverable, and ask whether agent-guided recursive updates unlock additional structures previously beyond the system's reach. Third, we organize supported hypotheses into chemically interpretable families and test them with independent spectral or orthogonal evidence. The paper's outcome is therefore the expansion and independent support of recoverable molecular knowledge, rather than a generic agent-performance leaderboard or an unsubstantiated biological pathway assignment.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Molecular structure recovery across chemical familiarity regimes}

We first evaluate the ability to recover molecular connectivity while keeping distinct modes of prior access separate (Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization}a). The PR95 full-cohort archive contains 17,556 MassSpecGym official TEST spectra representing 2,998 connectivity groups, alongside complete 813-spectrum NPLIB1/CANOPUS and 237-spectrum CASMI 2022 external evaluations. The database-assisted full-horizon setting reports 31.57\% Exact@1, 56.70\% Exact@10 and 64.77\% candidate-pool reach. In a separate test-identity-excluded, partial-core completion setting, the Gen15--16 cumulative archive reports 7.48\% Exact@1, 13.58\% Exact@10 and approximately 23.3\% reach. These reflect different candidate supply and aggregation protocols. They are complementary tasks, rather than two interchangeable measurements of unrestricted de novo generation.

On the NPLIB1/CANOPUS test set, the archived partial-core system reports 16.73\% Exact@1 and 31.98\% Exact@10. The corresponding CASMI 2022 results are 4.64\% and 11.39\%. The latest per-case machine-readable MassSpecGym summary also records single-spectrum, observable-cluster consensus and multi-energy outcomes as separately defined inference modes. \result{Produce one frozen-run, same-protocol main comparison with source-pinned candidate budgets, precursor-formula access, per-molecule confidence intervals and matched external baselines.} The remaining gap between generated-candidate reach and final exact identification motivates an explicit decomposition of formula misses, graph-proposal failures and ranking errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization}b).

A source-specific chemical knowledge census found 12,061 of 17,556 test spectra associated with structures already present in the enumerated public molecular databases; another 5,482 were placed in a predefined near-neighbour structural-edit tier, with 13 in a more remote tier. This audit describes proximity to the chosen chemical reference collection, not experimentally established biosynthesis or successful model recovery. \result{Quantify recovery versus paired-training MCES distance, scaffold novelty and distance to the complete accessible pretraining library, with molecular-class-matched uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization}c).}

To disentangle structure novelty from usable chemical priors, we define spectrum-random (S0), identity-disjoint (S1), scaffold-disjoint (S2), official MCES-disjoint (S3), pretraining-aware novelty (S4) and external/study/temporal (S5) evaluations. Chemical prior access is considered separately: paired-only knowledge (K0), near-neighbour-filtered external knowledge (K1), exact-identity-excluded knowledge (K2) and recursive experience added to either filtered prior (K3). Existing S3 results and their post-hoc S4 distance stratification can be evaluated without retraining the historical Gen series; changed training partitions or filtered pretraining corpora require controlled retraining of selected representative versions (Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization}d). \result{Measure how chemical familiarity and added evidence jointly affect independent molecular recovery.}

\subsection*{Mass-matched substructure alignment grounds transferable chemical knowledge}

We next ask how fragment evidence becomes usable structural knowledge rather than merely an auxiliary retrieval score (Fig.~\ref{fig:mma}). Given observed product-ion masses and possible fragment formulas, MMA retrieves mass-consistent motifs, learns correspondences between observed peaks and motif candidates, aligns motifs to a partial molecular graph and provides chemically legal atom, ring and motif actions for \(G\). The generated candidate is passed to \(W\) for structure-derived fragmentation hypotheses; \(V\) compares the hypotheses against the experimental spectrum. \(W\) does not directly observe the target experimental fragment list during forward simulation.

A same-checkpoint 200-molecule, known-formula calibration comparison measured 1.50\% Exact@1, 4.50\% Exact@10 and 6.50\% reach without MMA. Enabling MMA increased these to 6.00\%, 14.50\% and 19.00\%. Teacher-action accuracy increased from 69.1\% to 76.0\%. Canonical Hybrid additionally uses atom-normalized candidate scoring and motif-reserved expansion. A historical feasibility analysis shortened typical molecule-construction paths by replacing many atom-wise decisions with ring and multi-atom motif operations; this is an action-representation result, not proof of unknown-spectrum identification (Fig.~\ref{fig:mma}b).

Generation coverage and structure discrimination behave differently. In a fixed 200-case calibration protocol, an MMA-feature \(G\) candidate pool yielded 16.00\% reach and 7.00\% Exact@1; ranking the same pool with \(W/V\) increased Exact@1 to 8.50\% without changing reach. For the same MMA-seeded candidate set, \(G\)-only and \(G+W/V\) ranking achieved 5.50\% and 10.00\% Exact@1, respectively, at 19.00\% reach. Thus more plausible candidates may create additional isomer competition, while measured fragment evidence can improve ranking within a fixed set (Fig.~\ref{fig:mma}d).

\result{Under matched knowledge source, model and budget, isolate mass matching, motif retrieval, neural peak--motif alignment and motif--graph attachment; trace newly learned motifs to actions that recover independent molecules.} The central mechanistic question is whether source-local fragments become reusable, evidence-grounded chemical building blocks beyond the structures from which they were extracted.

\subsection*{Recursive chemical learning progressively expands recoverable molecular space}

The ORBIT-MS development sequence provides a record of successive improvements in chemical knowledge and structure inference (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive}). The reported MMA resources expanded from 4.23 million TRAIN-derived entries to 12.98 million entries with HMDB-based simulated experience and approximately 31.11 million entries with multi-source chemical experience. These are alignment records, not unique newly solved molecules. The system also accumulated generator, verifier and synthesis-related improvements, including partial-core completion, hard-isomer ranking, neutral-loss evidence and cross-energy candidate consensus.

On the historical cumulative partial-core evaluation, reported Exact@1 progressed from 0\% for the initial empty-start model to 0.52\% after early MMA development, 1.65\% after partial-core construction, 3.58\% after subsequent multi-source learning and 7.48\% in the Gen15--16 archive. Reach in the same retrospective series rose to approximately 23.3\%. These checkpoints and candidate archives represent genuine development milestones; their joint changes in library size, inference policy, candidate pooling and ranker cannot by themselves isolate the cause of each increment. \result{Recompute matched per-molecule recovered, newly reached and forgotten structure sets using raw historical outputs, preserving each generation's model and aggregation protocol (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive}b).}

Three existing learning streams offer different forms of experience. Sleep uses external unpaired molecular structures and \(W\)-simulated spectra. Wake generates and evidence-filters hypotheses for real unannotated experimental spectra. Hindsight reuses generated alternatives, failed construction trajectories and simulated counterfactual spectra. The scientific question is whether their complementarity and selection quality yield more transferable knowledge than simply increasing the number of synthetic pairs. \result{Compare Sleep-only, Wake-only, Hindsight-only and combined learning with matched effective experience, initial checkpoints and compute; compare random, entropy-based and structural-scarcity-based selection on independent recovery (Fig.~\ref{fig:recursive}d).}

We will also use source-linked examples to connect an initially unreached target, a specific new motif or corrective experience, actual subsequent graph actions and recovery of an independent molecule. Such cases test knowledge transfer, whereas aggregate synthetic-corpus growth alone does not establish it.

\subsection*{Adaptive scientific decisions target evolving elucidation failures}

Recursive training raises a decision problem: different generations can be limited by missing structural coverage, correct candidates lost during generation, or correct candidates ranked below chemically plausible isomers. A fixed expansion policy may become less useful as these failure modes shift. The Scientific Improver in PR96 formalizes four bounded interventions: \texttt{EXPAND\_KNOWLEDGE}, \texttt{IMPROVE\_GENERATOR}, \texttt{IMPROVE\_VERIFIER} and \texttt{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}. It receives a fresh TRAIN FIT diagnostic packet, proposes a testable action and records the measured result and reflection before the next decision (Fig.~\ref{fig:agent}a).

The existing project contains concrete language-model decision receipts, including recommendations to expand natural-product/Wake evidence and to focus on difficult isomer ranking. It also retains a historical Gen sequence with training and inference receipts. These assets demonstrate executable scientific choices and the technical components of the feedback loop. An LLM recommendation or historically improved model is not, however, a measured advantage over another intervention policy.

\result{Report at least three completed sequential autonomous agent-selected updates beginning from one frozen WGV state, with a newly recomputed TRAIN diagnosis at each generation. Compare net newly recovered independent molecules, losses and GPU-hours against a fixed action schedule and a scripted failure-routing controller at matched budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:agent}b,c).} A no-memory arm isolates the role of retained episode feedback. Analysis of how the dominant failure mode and selected action change from one generation to the next provides the key test of adaptive scientific decision-making (Fig.~\ref{fig:agent}d,e). The historical manual/scripted Gen archive is preserved separately and is not relabelled as a completed autonomous evaluation.

\subsection*{Evidence-supported elucidation of the dark metabolome}

We finally assess whether recursively acquired chemical knowledge can support structural hypotheses for real spectra that lack accepted reference-library matches (Fig.~\ref{fig:dark}). In three source-audited public study collections, 348 acquisition files were expected, 272 were verified and 76 unavailable. Among 835,953 eligible biological MS/MS queries, 613,331 had no accepted reference-spectrum match under the specified protocol. An additional 2,255 recurring spectral communities were observed across at least two collections, of which 98 appeared in all three. These figures characterize measured queries and recurring spectral signals, not independently identified unknown molecules.

A preliminary PR95 analysis of 1,000 GeMS spectra assigned 7, 47, 240 and 706 queries to progressively less structurally resolved evidence categories. Accordingly, 294 spectra passed at least the reported local structure/core-evidence thresholds. These tiers remain model-derived hypotheses pending independent calibration and external corroboration; evidence-supported shared cores and bounded isomer sets are distinct from authenticated unique connectivity. The associated mass-shift and candidate-network findings are useful leads for family-level interpretation, but mass differences alone cannot establish enzyme reactions or metabolic pathways.

\result{Apply frozen, independently calibrated structure-resolution criteria to deduplicated dark-spectral entities; report tier transitions and losses before and after recursive learning (Fig.~\ref{fig:dark}b,c).} \result{Select two or three real chemistry-supported families with observed peak-to-fragment evidence and verifiable molecular graph relationships, and evaluate independent experimental MS/MS, multi-energy or MSn observations and, where feasible, authentic standards (Fig.~\ref{fig:dark}d,e).} The intended output is additional defensible structural information and testable chemical hypotheses, while leaving unresolved or ambiguous spectra explicitly unresolved.

\section*{Discussion}

ORBIT-MS treats molecular structure elucidation as an inference-and-learning problem in which experimental fragment evidence is translated into reusable graph-construction knowledge. Its present experiments establish that MMA can improve known-formula structural reach and that candidate-conditioned fragmentation evidence can improve fixed-pool ranking. The recorded Gen progression shows that increasing structural knowledge and refining generation/ranking strategies can extend practical recovery, while motivating controlled studies to separate these contributions.

The broader objective is an adaptive system that identifies its current chemical bottleneck, gathers appropriate new experience and changes its subsequent decisions based on observed gains and failures. Establishing that advantage requires matched autonomous-versus-fixed trajectories on independent molecular structures, as opposed to counting LLM calls or retrospective manual upgrades. Chemical novelty should likewise be measured relative to the actual paired and external knowledge accessible to the system, rather than defined solely through a single split.

MS/MS alone often cannot uniquely identify constitutional isomers, and still less their stereochemistry. Source-linked structural hypotheses, bounded ambiguity and independent corroboration are therefore essential outputs. The present dark-metabolomics analyses provide real observations for evaluating this principle, while the planned calibration and chemical validation determine which additional structural claims can ultimately be made.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary units}
ORBIT-MS is assessed as a tandem-spectrum structure-elucidation system and a tool for studying chemically unresolved public MS/MS. Experimental spectral queries, unique precursor connectivity groups, distinct spectral entities and molecular-family relationships are separate counting units. Molecular predictions use available measured precursor and fragment information; a predicted structure is not an experimental molecular identity. The main outcomes are structure-level candidate recovery on known-identity evaluation sets, calibrated structural-resolution assignment on reference-unmatched spectra, supported chemical-family relations and independent experimental corroboration. \methodtodo{Freeze a complete figure-to-source table, final model checkpoint, public data snapshot, spectral entity definition and confirmatory outcome thresholds.}

\subsection*{Experimental spectra, source accounting and reference annotation}
Each query contains measured precursor \(m/z\), product-ion \(m/z\) and intensity, observed ion/adduct information when available and acquisition context supported by its original source. The three-study source audit uses UC, IBD200 and VEO-IBD collections and records expected files, verified and unavailable acquisitions, eligible biological MS/MS and mutually exclusive accepted, ambiguous, unmatched and unassessable reference statuses. Acquisition aliases, conversions, repeated spectra, collision energies, technical repeats and cross-repository redepositions are tracked without treating all records as independent molecules or biological samples. A separate ongoing pan-repository inventory spans GNPS/MassIVE, MetaboLights, Metabolomics Workbench, NORMAN/DSFP, MetaboBank and MB-POST; its public index counts are not pooled with verified physical MS2 counts.

Reference matching is defined by the date-stamped GNPS/MassBank/MoNA or other source-verified experimental spectral snapshot, precursor tolerance, fragment matching rule, adduct restrictions and score thresholds. A reference-unmatched status means that no candidate passed this particular reference protocol, not that the structure is absent from all chemical databases. The source identifiers and annotation dispositions remain attached to all inferred structures. \methodtodo{Freeze release-wide spectrum/reference matching source data, complete missing-file dispositions and the exact number of deduplicated representative dark-spectral entities.}

\subsection*{Molecular identities and benchmark partitions}
Connectivity identity is evaluated using the first 14 characters of a canonical InChIKey. This identifies 2D graph connectivity under the chosen standardization and does not demonstrate stereochemical identity. Known-structure benchmarks retain fixed molecule-group partitions and the complete declared eligible denominator; confidence intervals resample molecular groups rather than treating repeated spectra as independent structures. Historical MassSpecGym TRAIN-derived development partitions contain 175,548 fitting spectra, 9,782 calibration spectra and 8,401 repeatedly inspected search-development spectra out of 193,731 eligible TRAIN spectra. These results remain distinguished from official MassSpecGym TEST evaluation. Target-free inference does not read target molecular formulas, target structures or target fingerprints; analyses providing a reference formula, supplied candidate set or partial Macro-Core are reported as separate conditional tasks.

\subsection*{Direct precursor-composition hypotheses}
Precursor elemental-composition hypotheses are inferred from the observed precursor mass, ion/adduct context and permissible fragment evidence. Formula correctness and Top-\(K\) formula recall are measured separately from molecular graph recovery. Chemical validity filters enforce allowed atom inventory, charges, valence, connectivity and precursor/adduct mass consistency. A missing true formula is counted explicitly as a composition-stage failure. Molecular fingerprints are not used to generate, route, score or guide structural proposals in the active target-free pipeline; post-inference Morgan/Tanimoto values are external similarity metrics only.

\subsection*{Fragmentation World and observation-conditioned Verifier}
For a proposed precursor graph, the current World \(W\) constructs a finite set of connected, structurally represented fragment and ion hypotheses, including the source-validated up-to-three-cut candidate support, hydrogen adjustments and permitted charge/proton carriers. It assigns fragment prior weights using the molecular graph and available acquisition context, not the measured target MS/MS fragment-peak list during forward prediction. A proposed fragment structure is a conditional model hypothesis, not a directly observed gas-phase intermediate or a proved electron-flow mechanism.

Verifier \(V\) compares those structural fragment hypotheses with measured product-ion masses and intensities to produce observation-conditioned evidence for the proposed precursor. Evidence is aggregated at the precursor-candidate level using recorded weights and candidate support. The distinction between common fragment masses and candidate-specific structural evidence is explicit. Fixed-support W/V tests, same-formula structural-decoy tests and precursor-compatible peer/shuffled-spectrum controls assess whether the experimental spectrum contributes discrimination beyond structural popularity or candidate priors. \methodtodo{Source-freeze the release W/V checkpoints, exact support representation, scoring coefficients and fragment-to-candidate aggregation.}

\subsection*{Generator, MMA and chemically constrained construction}
Generator \(G\) follows the current PR93/PR95 Peak-Ladder and Canonical Hybrid graph-construction implementation. Given measured MS/MS and admissible precursor/formula hypotheses, it uses chemically legal atom, ring and MMA motif starts/attachments with canonical trajectory ordering, atom-normalized beam scoring and motif-reserved expansion. MMA consists of source-linked reusable fragment/motif records derived from training structures and supported W-simulated structure–spectrum pairs. Graph construction is governed by chemical validity, not by a hidden target graph at inference time. The current optional Macro-Core procedure retrieves a non-evaluation structural homologue, removes one or more heavy-atom components and lets \(G\) complete the remaining graph; this is explicitly labelled homology-assisted completion and is not described as pure de novo generation.

Fixed-pool terminal \(W/V\) reranking changes only the ordering of already generated molecular candidates. Whenever enabled in a selected inference variant, intermediate \(W/V\) evidence influencing future \(G\) actions is separately identified as online guidance. The benchmark reports raw reach before ranking, Exact@1/10 after ranking, generated candidate count, invalid proposals and elapsed inference time. Differences between empty-graph, motif-seeded and homology-assisted modes are not pooled into a single undeclared metric.

\subsection*{Agent-directed recursive self-improvement and chemical experience}
Existing WGV learning tools supply the interventions used by Scientific Improver: forward Sleep simulation from non-evaluation molecular structures, real-spectrum Wake pseudo-labeling under source-specific evidence gates, motif/ring vocabulary expansion, Canonical Hybrid structure-supervised training of \(G\), and candidate-derived hard-isomer training of \(V\). PR95's historical Gen~1--10 progression records a sequence of human-directed or scripted model/algorithm configurations, not ten independently completed autonomous scientific-agent experiments.

For controlled RSI, let the generation state be \(\mathcal S_t=(\mathcal K_t,G_t,V_t;W)\), where \(\mathcal K_t\) is the MMA/chemical knowledge state and \(W\) is fixed in the initial evaluation. From a newly evaluated TRAIN FIT cohort, we obtain a diagnostic packet \(D_t\) distinguishing true-structure proposal misses, candidates present but incorrectly ranked, and where supported, the chemical evidence deficits associated with these cases. Given \(D_t\) and a persistent history \(\mathcal M_t\) of previous hypotheses, actions and measured outcomes, the Scientific Improver selects
\[
a_t=\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}(D_t,\mathcal M_t),\qquad
a_t\in\{\mathrm{EXPAND\_KNOWLEDGE},\,\mathrm{IMPROVE\_GENERATOR},\,\mathrm{IMPROVE\_VERIFIER},\,\mathrm{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}\}.
\]
The action is accompanied by a falsifiable diagnosis, supporting training-only case identifiers, an expected improvement metric and a predicted direction. The language model selects only an available, typed intervention; model execution and chemical validity remain with the existing WGV tools. The \(\mathrm{COLLECT\_EVIDENCE}\) action requests further TRAIN analysis without claiming a trained candidate model.

Each accepted action may update \(\mathcal K_t\), \(G_t\) or \(V_t\), using the same frozen source-defined evaluation protocol before and after the intervention. The episode stores the input model lineage, selected action, training output, measured changes in raw candidate reach and final Exact@1, reflection and next-generation state. A completed episode's reflection can condition the following scientific decision through \(\mathcal M_{t+1}\); this is memory-mediated agent adaptation, not parameter fine-tuning of the underlying LLM. Genuine recursive evaluation requires recomputing the TRAIN FIT observation using the promoted state before the next action; replay of an unchanged diagnosis packet is not evidence of evolving scientific decision-making. Source-bound real dark-spectral evaluations assess whether any resulting gains manifest as additional defensible molecular structural information.

Matched evaluation compares autonomous agent choice with unchanged WGV, an identical fixed intervention and scripted failure routing; agent-memory ablation separates benefit from persistent experience. Valid comparisons use common initial checkpoints, equivalent candidate-generation settings, the same independent evaluation molecules, and measured training/GPU cost or an explicit unmatched-cost qualification. Updates are promoted only after completed evaluations. In the current PR96 implementation, the Scientific Improver and episode controller are executable code, but real multi-generation Taiji outcomes have not yet been established and therefore remain \methodtodo{prospectively measured multi-generation Agentic RSI results, the complete source and checkpoint ledger, ablation of agent memory and actual GPU-hour receipts}.

\subsection*{Structural resolution, calibration and selective interpretation}
Structure confidence is represented at several levels: putative unique connectivity, bounded alternative connectivity set, supported common core/scaffold, elemental composition or structural class, and unresolved/out-of-support. Thresholds for assigning these categories are selected on an independent known-identity calibration set, then frozen before dark-spectral interpretation. Empirical coverage refers to whether the benchmark true structure is retained by each proposed structural statement; confidence scores without truth containment are not treated as calibrated identification probabilities. Candidate-set size, formula ambiguity, unmodelled ion chemistry and detector/instrument limitations are recorded for every failed or downgraded inference. A calibration model may prefer a bounded isomer set to an unjustified single-molecule prediction.

\subsection*{Dark-spectral entity construction and independent recurrence}
Source-verified scans are associated with precursor ion/adduct and experimental spectrum information. Compatible collision energies and repeated injections may inform the same structural entity, but are retained as distinct observations with their original acquisition IDs. Spectral communities are derived from measured precursor/fragment matching with a fixed, source-audited recurrence criterion. A community present in multiple studies does not automatically have one unique molecular precursor or one unique chemical family. Whole-study controls, source overlap, technical-repeat removal and study-appropriate nulls are used to assess recurrence independently from model-assigned chemical structure.

\subsection*{Structure-supported chemical families and reference relationships}
A supported candidate family requires evidence for a shared graph-level chemical relationship. Family membership is represented by identified candidate molecular graphs, bounded alternative sets or a common supported core as dictated by spectral evidence. Graph-edit or substructure relations must specify atom/bond differences and molecular standardization. Comparisons to known metabolite structures use frozen chemical databases, and matched null models account for molecular formula, size, scaffold and the generator's own source prior. Structural differences are not equated with enzyme-catalysed reactions or confirmed biosynthetic pathways. Source and literature recognition checks distinguish known chemistry, unrecognized family-level relationships, candidate unreported structures and insufficient evidence.

\subsection*{Independent spectral evidence and optional orthogonal validation}
Prioritized molecular-family examples are selected using frozen evidence and recurrence criteria before inspection of later validation outcomes. Independent public MS/MS, compatible multienergy spectra and truly measured MSn ancestry are evaluated using complete precursor/adduct and study provenance. Candidate-specific fragment predictions are compared with measured ions and plausible alternative molecular graphs. Authentic standards, retention/coelution and other orthogonal information contribute stronger confirmation only when corresponding experimental records exist. Failed, ambiguous or unavailable validations remain in the prespecified outcome set. The study does not require disease association, phenotype modelling or functional inference to establish chemically meaningful structural hypotheses.

\subsection*{Chemical novelty, prior access and evaluation partitions}
We distinguish repeated-spectrum, identity-disjoint, scaffold-disjoint, official MCES-disjoint, pretraining-aware novelty and independent external/study/temporal evaluations (S0--S5). The standard MassSpecGym MCES split is retained without alteration. For each target graph, novelty is separately characterized relative to paired training structures and each complete molecular knowledge source accessible during pretraining, MMA retrieval or inference. The nearest-neighbour definitions, fingerprint specification, MCES implementation, scaffold standardization and effective sample counts are fixed before comparing learning methods. S0 includes potentially repeated molecular identities across spectra and is not used to support unseen-molecule generalization. Scaffold identity and MCES novelty describe different chemical relationships and are not assumed to be nested hardness levels.

Prior-access regimes include paired-only knowledge (K0); external structure-only knowledge excluding each test identity and a prespecified neighbourhood (K1); external knowledge excluding only exact test connectivity identities (K2); and recursive experience acquisition applied to a recorded K1 or K2 source state (K3). K3 is a training mechanism and not a separate structural distance threshold. Molecular formula access (observed/ground-truth formula, predicted formula, or no supplied formula), partial-core availability, external candidate libraries and admissible cross-energy aggregation are independently declared. A stratified analysis of an already trained checkpoint does not count as retraining under a newly defined partition. \methodtodo{Freeze S0--S5 and K0--K3 manifests, pretraining snapshots, near-neighbour cutoffs and matched cohort composition before final experiments.}

\subsection*{Experience-source and acquisition ablations}
The Sleep dataset pairs external unpaired molecular graphs with spectra simulated by frozen \(W\), retaining generator version, simulated observation conditions and source provenance. Wake uses experimental unlabeled spectra and selects candidate labels only through predefined evidence gates. Hindsight stores real \(G\) trajectories, failed alternatives, permitted counterfactual graphs and their generated spectra. The same test identities and evaluation-specific library records are excluded from all update streams. Random, spectral entropy, formula/ring scarcity and joint information-guided acquisition are compared at equivalent effective experience volumes and measured training cost; structural diversity, source frequency and model-inference budget are recorded. Changes in independent true-structure reach and final Exact@1 are the primary endpoints, rather than self-generated peak likelihood or sample count alone. \methodtodo{Complete source-stratified and selection-policy experiments on fixed initial WGV states.}

\subsection*{Statistical analysis and comparative experiments}
Primary structural performance is Exact@1/10 and candidate-pool reach for connectivity groups, accompanied by formula accuracy, valid candidate fraction, top-\(K\) structural similarity and computational cost. Molecular-group bootstrap confidence intervals account for multiple spectra representing the same reference structure. Known-formula, macro-core-assisted and database-fixed-pool tasks are presented separately from target-free generation. The critical controlled comparisons hold the experimental query, initial candidates and resource budgets fixed while changing MMA information, \(W/V\) ranking or new learning experiences. For source-scale dark-matter results, units are distinct spectral entities or structurally supported families with original spectra and independent studies traceable separately. Unavailable sources and unresolved chemical identities remain in denominators.
