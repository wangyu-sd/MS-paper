\noindent\textbf{
Tandem mass spectrometry can detect thousands of small molecules in complex samples, yet most spectra remain structurally unresolved because many chemically plausible molecules can explain the same sparse fragmentation measurements.\cite{daSilva2015,Alseekh2021,MassSpecGym2024} Recent methods have markedly improved formula inference, molecular retrieval, spectrum prediction and de novo structure generation,\cite{Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Hoffmann2022,Goldman2023,Xing2023,Stravs2022,Young2024,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} but increasingly realistic molecular hypotheses create a neglected second problem: the fixed spectral evidence used to rank them loses discriminative power as the alternatives become more similar. These results establish adaptive, candidate-specific fragmentation evidence as a distinct computational object for molecular structure elucidation.

\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is a central technology for characterizing small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Modern LC--MS/MS experiments acquire large numbers of fragmentation spectra from complex samples, but translating those spectra into molecular structures remains a major bottleneck. Spectral-library matching provides high-confidence annotation when an authentic reference has already been measured, yet reference libraries cover only a small fraction of the chemical diversity encountered in untargeted experiments.\cite{daSilva2015,Wang2016GNPS} Consequently, much of computational metabolomics has focused on extracting progressively richer structural information directly from tandem spectra.

This effort has produced several complementary advances. SIRIUS and CSI:FingerID combine fragmentation trees and learned fingerprints for database search;\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019} CANOPUS extends annotation to chemical classes;\cite{Duhrkop2021} COSMIC and BUDDY improve confidence-aware structure and formula annotation beyond exact spectral-library matching;\cite{Hoffmann2022,Xing2023} and MIST incorporates chemical formulae and neutral-loss relationships into neural spectrum representations.\cite{Goldman2023} In the forward direction, CFM-ID, MassFormer and FIORA predict tandem spectra or fragmentation events from molecular structure.\cite{Wang2021CFMID,Young2024,Nowatzky2025} De novo methods including MSNovelist, DiffMS and structure-informed generation increasingly produce chemically plausible molecular candidates from spectra.\cite{Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} DreaMS further demonstrates that millions of unlabelled spectra can support transferable molecular representations.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams}

These developments shift the computational difficulty. For an early, implausible decoy, many observed peaks may separate it from the correct structure. For a late same-formula isomer that shares the scaffold and most local substructures, those peaks may be common to both molecules and cease to be informative. A high-quality spectrum simulation therefore does not necessarily identify the correct structure; a chemically plausible fragment assignment does not necessarily distinguish it from competitors; and a visually convincing mechanistic explanation can exceed the evidentiary authority of the underlying computation. We therefore distinguish three objects: \emph{simulation}, which asks whether a candidate can reproduce a spectrum; \emph{explanation}, which asks whether peaks can be connected to chemically plausible fragmentation paths; and \emph{discriminative evidence}, which asks whether those paths carry information that separates one candidate from its close alternatives.

We developed ORBIT-MS to model the third object directly. METEOR provides spectrum-conditioned molecular hypotheses, whereas pLSE constructs auditable fragmentation evidence for a spectrum--candidate pair. Candidate evidence is evaluated relative to the competing structures and is permitted to adapt when new candidate populations expose systematic failures. The ORBIT runtime makes this adaptation bounded and replayable, but the agentic machinery is not the scientific claim: the central question is whether adaptive, candidate-specific chemical evidence improves molecular resolution beyond fixed scoring, chemical prior and opponent-specific optimization.

\section*{Candidate-specific fragmentation evidence resolves close molecular alternatives}

The evidence bottleneck cannot be solved by rewarding every theoretically reachable fragment. A candidate with a large fragment lattice has more opportunities to match observed peaks by chance, and a mass-compatible fragment can arise from chemically different or weakly supported pathways. We therefore defined fragmentation evidence at two levels: candidate support for an observed peak and the specificity of that support relative to the current competing structures.

For candidate \(M_j\) and observed peak \(p\), let \(\mathcal{T}(M_j,p)\) denote the set of atom-traceable trajectories generated by the frozen reaction basis that terminate in an ion compatible with \(p\) under the preregistered mass tolerance. We define peak support as
\[
s_{jp}=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(M_j,p)} Q(\tau,p),
\]
where \(Q\) is a non-ground-truth-dependent evidence-quality function over mass accuracy, reaction authority, atom/formula/charge conservation, explicit hydrogen or charge transfer where represented, and trajectory provenance. For candidate set \(\mathcal{C}\), the candidate-specific value of that peak is
\[
d_{jp}=s_{jp}-\max_{k\neq j}s_{kp}.
\]
The deployed candidate score aggregates positive candidate-specific evidence while controlling for accidental matching opportunity,
\[
D(M_j\mid S,\mathcal{C})=
\sum_{p\in S} w_p\,\phi(d_{jp})-\lambda\Omega(M_j),
\]
where \(\phi(x)=\max(0,x)\), \(w_p\) is the frozen peak weight and \(\Omega(M_j)\) measures candidate opportunity. \methodtodo{Replace this target definition with the exact frozen pLSE aggregation and report all coefficients or parameter-free terms.} Because the reaction basis is incomplete, missing support is not treated as strong negative evidence unless fragment-generation recall is independently shown to justify such a claim.

The trajectories underlying \(s_{jp}\) are represented as auditable state transitions:
\[

\rightarrow
\text{reaction centre}
\rightarrow
\text{elementary event}
\rightarrow
\text{product/co-product}
\rightarrow
\text{new microstate}.
\]
Each accepted transition records atom/component lineage, broken and formed bonds, charge-carrier state, formal-charge changes, hydrogen transfer where applicable, theoretical mass and source provenance. A mass coincidence or descriptive mechanism string without the corresponding trace does not receive reaction-level evidence authority.

\section*{Experimental spectra reshape molecular evidence beyond chemical prior}

A chemically plausible fragmentation explanation does not establish that the experimental spectrum contributed to the molecular decision. This is particularly important for generative molecular models, which can produce plausible structural narratives from molecular priors alone. We therefore separated two matched supervision views. In \emph{Chemical-World}, a molecular structure is expanded into a proposal graph of fragmentation possibilities under the current chemistry backend, without assigning experimental peak presence, intensity or empirical reaction probability. In \emph{Spectral-Reality}, the same proposal graph is paired with the measured MS/MS spectrum and linked to observed peaks under a deterministic mass gate. The two views therefore differ in experimental observation while sharing the same structural prior.

We quantified the contribution of the experimental spectrum with three matched conditions: the correct recipient spectrum, no spectrum, and a shuffled spectrum drawn from a distinct molecule group matched for adduct, neutral mass and \result{peak-richness/acquisition constraints}. Candidate pools were frozen across conditions. We defined spectral lift as the correct-spectrum improvement relative to the no-spectrum condition and identity lift as the improvement relative to the shuffled-spectrum condition.

For METEOR, the correct spectrum improved \result{primary same-formula rank/likelihood endpoint} by \result{spectral lift} over chemical background and by \result{identity lift} over the shuffled control. For pLSE, candidate-specific evidence decreased by \result{effect} when the recipient spectrum was replaced with a matched shuffled spectrum. These effects remained after molecule-group aggregation and were strongest in candidate sets that shared formula and scaffold, demonstrating that the useful signal was tied to the identity of the experimental observation rather than to structural plausibility alone.

The control also exposed a failure mode that conventional explanation metrics miss. \result{fraction} of chemically plausible fragment statements were either shared by the closest alternatives, unsupported at the available trace authority, or preserved under shuffled spectra. Such statements can be chemically sensible without carrying discriminative experimental information. ORBIT-MS therefore treats chemical plausibility as a proposal prior and experimental candidate specificity as the evidentiary quantity used for structure resolution.

\section*{Discussion}

Our results identify a distinct limitation in tandem-mass-spectrometry structure elucidation. The dominant computational framing has been to improve the inverse mapping from spectrum to formula, fingerprint, database candidate or generated molecular structure.\cite{Duhrkop2019,Hoffmann2022,Goldman2023,Xing2023,Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} Those advances remain essential, but increasingly realistic molecular hypotheses create a second-order problem: the evidence that separated earlier candidates can become non-specific among later same-formula alternatives. The practical bottleneck can therefore move from molecular generation to candidate discrimination.

This distinction separates simulation, explanation and discrimination. Forward models can reproduce spectra with increasing accuracy,\cite{Wang2021CFMID,Young2024,Nowatzky2025} but spectral agreement does not identify a unique causal fragmentation path. Conversely, an atom-plausible fragment assignment can be shared by every hard candidate in the set. ORBIT-MS therefore asks a different question: which experimentally observed fragments provide support that is specific to one candidate relative to its close competitors? This candidate-relative definition prevents global peak coverage from being mistaken for structural evidence and provides a natural way to compare mechanistic claims at the resolution supported by their trace.

The framework is deliberately conservative about incomplete chemistry. Current deterministic reaction bases cannot enumerate every process that contributes to an experimental CID spectrum. Absence of a generated fragment is therefore not automatically negative evidence against a candidate. Instead, ORBIT-MS emphasizes positive, high-authority candidate-specific evidence and exposes unsupported or unresolved regions separately. This asymmetry is scientifically useful: a small number of selective fragments can justify structural contraction even when much of the spectrum remains mechanistically unexplained, while the incomplete reaction basis is prevented from manufacturing false contradictions.

A related consequence is that non-resolution becomes a legitimate output. Tandem spectra can be insufficient to identify a unique two-dimensional structure and are generally even less informative about stereochemistry.\cite{Schymanski2014,Hoffmann2022,MassSpecGym2024} By separating generator recall from conditional discrimination and returning calibrated candidate sets, ORBIT-MS makes the reported resolution commensurate with the information present in the experiment. The mouse deployment illustrates the practical importance of this distinction: previously unlabelled spectra are converted into testable structural hypotheses and bounded candidate sets without lowering the evidentiary standard for calling a metabolite identified.

More broadly, these results suggest that inverse scientific prediction may become evidence-limited when hypothesis generators improve faster than the models used to adjudicate their outputs. ORBIT-MS addresses this regime by adapting the evidence interpretation to the alternatives that must actually be distinguished. A stronger formulation in which the system also chooses new experiments---for example, the precursor, fragment or collision condition expected to maximally reduce residual structural ambiguity---would turn adaptive evidence interpretation into sequential hypothesis--experiment co-design. Establishing that prospective loop is an important direction for future work rather than a claim of the present study.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary separation}

The study is organized around three frozen objects at each evaluation point: a candidate-generation model, a candidate-specific evidence model and the experimental spectrum. The hypothesis model maps a spectrum and precursor constraints to a candidate set. The evidence model maps the same spectrum together with a candidate set to candidate-specific fragmentation evidence and ranking. The experimental spectrum is never described as ``evolving'' unless a new measurement is physically acquired.

All population-level analyses use molecule groups, rather than individual spectra, as the primary independent unit whenever repeated spectra correspond to the same molecular structure. Development, calibration and test partitions are molecule-group disjoint. Candidate pools used for causal comparisons are frozen before evaluation, and official test data are not used to authorize model or evidence-program changes.

A chemical mechanism is considered auditable only to the level represented by the deterministic trace. A mass match without an atom-mapped event is not labelled as a verified reaction. A chemistry-backend proposal is treated as possible under that backend, not as an empirical reaction probability. Unsupported peaks are not candidate contradictions unless the relevant fragment-generation recall has independently passed a preregistered reliability criterion.

METEOR is the spectrum-conditioned molecular hypothesis model. The final implementation uses \result{final backbone, model size and checkpoint lineage}, with a chemistry-specific vocabulary, continuous token-mass state and peak-level spectrum conditioning. Experimental peaks are represented using absolute \(m/z\), precursor-relative \(m/z\), intensity and acquisition metadata where observed. \methodtodo{Freeze final direct-prefix/cross-attention interface and checkpoint identities.}

Training is staged: structure-only chemical adaptation; Chemical-World supervision using structure-derived fragment-reaction proposal graphs; Spectral-Reality fine-tuning using experimental spectra; and evidence-guided post-training only after the active pLSE snapshot passes the frozen reward-fidelity criteria. Generated non-ground-truth molecules may act as hard negatives or policy actions but do not become teacher-forced positive labels solely because they receive high similarity or pLSE scores.

\subsection*{Chemical-World and Spectral-Reality controls}

Chemical-World and Spectral-Reality share the same fragment-reaction graph schema but differ in experimental authority. Chemical-World contains structure-derived fragmentation possibilities only. Spectral-Reality retains the same proposal graph and links it to observed peaks through a deterministic mass gate. A peak can remain compatible with multiple fragments; the training record therefore does not force a unique fragment identity when the spectrum does not support one.

Spectrum dependence is evaluated with correct-spectrum, no-spectrum and shuffled-spectrum conditions on identical candidate pools. Shuffled donors must come from different molecule groups and match the recipient for adduct, neutral mass within \result{window}, and \methodtodo{final acquisition/peak-richness matching}. Spectral and identity lift are computed after equal-weight molecule-group aggregation.

Structured reasoning-specific controls use the parent/pilot \(\times\) normal/no-reasoning/shuffled-reasoning six-arm design. This analysis is supporting evidence and is reported in Extended Data rather than used as the principal chemical claim.

\subsection*{Reciprocal adaptation and factorial endpoint evaluation}

A reciprocal ORBIT run begins from immutable hypothesis and evidence snapshots. Each round produces a deterministic observation, selects one target, authorizes at most one bounded intervention and settles the change through paired evaluation. Evidence updates must improve the live candidate-frontier endpoint while preserving frozen anchors, non-target checks and a success bank. Hypothesis updates require a held-out molecular endpoint and correct-versus-shuffled spectrum dependence. Training loss, reward and pLSE score alone cannot authorize a METEOR promotion.

The principal causal comparison is performed after final snapshots freeze. We evaluate \((H_0,E_0)\), \((H_T,E_0)\), \((H_0,E_T)\) and \((H_T,E_T)\) on identical held-out cases and calculate the preregistered interaction effect. \result{Add fixed-side and ungated-alternation controls and replicate runs in the final experiment.} The complete cross-generation matrix is computed post hoc from frozen snapshots and is not used to tune intermediate rounds.

\subsection*{External-generator transfer}

External candidate sets are produced by \result{final peer-reviewed candidate generators/retrieval methods} without pLSE-guided sampling or reranking. All external candidates undergo the same standardization, deduplication and precursor-constraint checks. Initial and adapted pLSE snapshots are compared using paired molecule-group inference, including strict same-formula and near-isomer strata.

\subsection*{Risk-controlled structural resolution}

Candidate generation and evidence-based discrimination are reported separately. For candidate set \(\mathcal C_K\), generator recall is the fraction of molecule groups for which the ground truth is present in \(\mathcal C_K\). Conditional discrimination is evaluated only among sets containing the ground truth. End-to-end unique resolution combines both terms.

A calibration split disjoint from final testing is used to construct structural prediction sets \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\alpha}(S)\) under \methodtodo{final split-conformal or other frozen risk-control method}. We report empirical set coverage, candidate-set size, unique-resolution rate, conditional accuracy and false-resolution rate. A unique structure is emitted only when the calibrated set contains one candidate. Stereochemical resolution is not claimed from MS/MS alone unless independently supported.

\subsection*{Statistics}

Unless otherwise stated, the molecule group is the independent unit. Repeated spectra are collapsed within group before population-level inference. Primary effect sizes are reported with \result{95\% or 99\% confidence intervals}. Paired comparisons use \result{group bootstrap and exact/Monte Carlo sign-flip tests}, with family-wise error controlled across preregistered confirmatory endpoints by \result{final correction}. Minimum independent-group support and primary endpoints are frozen before final testing.

Acquisition strata such as instrument family and collision energy are analysed only when values are observed and source-verifiable. Default priors or missing metadata do not silently create measured strata.
