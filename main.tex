\noindent\textbf{
Tandem mass spectra are incomplete observations of the gas-phase chemistry that generates them, yet molecular structure elucidation is usually treated as a direct mapping from spectrum to structure. Here we introduce ORBIT-MS, which instead treats each molecular prediction as a hypothesis that can be tested by executable fragmentation. A spectrum-conditioned Generator proposes molecular structures, a Verifier proposes coherent electron-redistribution events that explain observed peaks, and a spectrum-blind World evaluates the same events before a deterministic bond--electron executor materializes their charged products. Across molecule-disjoint development data, this design separates molecular proposal quality from forward-fragmentation quality and from evidence-based reranking, yielding \result{final no-formula de novo result}, \result{final spectrum-simulation and retrieval result} and \result{matched WGV reranking result}. We then calibrate the resulting evidence into bounded structural statements and apply them to \result{dark spectrum count} previously unannotated spectra, identifying \result{recurrent entity/family count} recurrent molecular entities or families with \result{headline structural/biological result}. ORBIT-MS therefore turns spectrum-to-structure prediction into a testable chemical inference problem and provides a route from uncertain tandem spectra to auditable structural and biological analysis.
}

\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is a principal technology for surveying small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Yet most tandem mass spectra generated by untargeted studies still lack a confident structural assignment.\cite{daSilva2015,Alseekh2021} Spectral libraries, molecular networking, formula and fingerprint prediction, learned spectrum models and de novo generators have all improved the hypotheses that can be proposed for a spectrum.\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Wang2021CFMID,Goldman2023,Bohde2025,Qiang2026DeepMet} Most of these approaches nevertheless evaluate a spectrum through an answer---a similarity score, fingerprint, ranked candidate or generated molecule---rather than through the gas-phase events that could have produced the observation.

This distinction matters because a tandem mass spectrum is only a partial observation of fragmentation chemistry. Peak presence does not identify a unique reaction path, and peak absence does not by itself imply chemical impossibility. A model can therefore produce a plausible structure without stating which executable fragmentation evidence supports it, while a forward spectrum model can fit common peaks without discriminating among closely related structural alternatives. The missing object is an explicit, testable connection between a molecular hypothesis and the gas-phase products that hypothesis can generate.

We address this gap by representing fragmentation in a bond--electron state space and separating molecular proposal from forward chemical evidence. ORBIT-MS combines a spectrum-conditioned molecular Generator \(G\), a spectrum-conditioned Verifier \(V\), a spectrum-blind fragmentation World \(W\) and a deterministic chemical executor. \(G\) proposes molecular hypotheses; \(V\) proposes coherent electron-redistribution events that explain a selected observed peak; \(W\) scores the same event without seeing the fragment spectrum; and the executor determines the explicit charged products and their masses. Candidate ranking can therefore combine inverse-generation confidence with forward evidence while keeping learned preference distinct from chemical executability.

We first evaluate these components through the standard MassSpecGym task families---de novo generation, molecular retrieval and spectrum simulation---and explicitly separate proposal recall from World-based reranking. We then calibrate executable evidence into the strongest structural statement supported by each spectrum rather than forcing every case to a unique identity. Applied to repository-scale dark spectra, this framework is designed to convert anonymous observations into recurrent structural entities or bounded molecular families that can be compared across datasets and related to biological perturbations. The paper therefore proceeds from a testable fragmentation-world hypothesis, through benchmarked structural evidence, to the chemical and biological organization of the dark metabolome.

\section*{Executable fragmentation provides a forward test of molecular hypotheses}

ORBIT-MS was designed to make forward chemistry an explicit test of a molecular prediction. The Generator \(G\) proposes structures from the observed spectrum, whereas the spectrum-blind World \(W\) receives only a candidate molecule and acquisition context. For a candidate and selected observed peak, the Verifier \(V\) proposes a coherent electron event; \(W\) scores that same trace without access to the measured fragment peaks; and the deterministic executor materializes the event into explicit charged products before any evidence is assigned \figureonetodo. This separation allows forward fragmentation to be evaluated independently of molecular generation.

We first tested \(W\) as a spectrum simulator. On the complete 8,401-spectrum molecule-disjoint SEARCH\_DEV development set, the current spectrum-blind World achieves a mean official sparse cosine similarity of 0.09935 and Jensen--Shannon similarity of 0.09199 at the frozen sampling budget. These values define the present forward-model baseline rather than a solved simulation task. The corresponding official molecular-retrieval evaluation, which ranks the mass-based candidate universe using World-generated fragment-mass fingerprints, is reported as \result{complete mass-pool Hit@1/5/20 and MCES@1}; the formula-filtered pool is retained as a secondary bonus setting. Together, simulation and retrieval test whether \(W\) captures experimentally useful fragmentation without allowing the observed spectrum to enter the forward policy.

\section*{Molecular connectivity, rather than formula availability, limits current de novo recovery}

We next evaluated the inverse problem under the standard no-ground-truth-formula setting. Across all 8,401 SEARCH\_DEV spectra, \(G\) recovers the correct connectivity at Exact@1 in 131 cases (1.559\%) and at Exact@10 in 370 cases (4.404\%); the correct structure appears anywhere among 100 independent molecular samples in 464 cases (5.523\%). These results use connectivity-level InChIKey-14 matching and retain failed trajectories in the denominator \figuretwotodo.

The low recall is not primarily explained by failure to propose the elemental composition. The correct formula is present in the mass/adduct-derived proposal lists for 8,140 of 8,401 cases (96.89\%), yet only 464 correct connectivities are generated. The gap widens with molecular size: among 2,110 targets containing at least 30 heavy atoms, the correct formula is available for 1,930 cases but the correct connectivity is sampled in only five. Thus, under the current model and generation budget, precise bond connectivity is the dominant inverse-model bottleneck, rather than formula availability or empty candidate lists.

\section*{World evidence separates reranking from molecular proposal recall}

A structure that is absent from the Generator pool cannot be rescued by a verifier, so we evaluate mechanistic evidence only on fixed candidate sets. For each candidate \(M\) and observed peak \(p\), \(V\) samples events from \(q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c)\); the executor determines the charged products; and \(W\) replays the identical trace to obtain \(p_W(a\mid M,c)\). Event evidence combines the spectrum-blind World probability, correction for the observation-conditioned proposal and agreement between executed product masses and the target peak, and is then aggregated across event samples and observed peaks.

The primary WGV comparison therefore freezes one Generator candidate pool and compares its original ordering with \(G+\lambda E(M,S)\) on exactly the same molecules. A zero-World weight is required to reproduce the frozen Generator ranking case by case. Under this matched protocol, WGV changes Exact@1/10 by \result{matched WGV Exact@1/10 result and paired gain/loss analysis}. This design separates two otherwise conflated questions: whether \(G\) can propose the correct structure at all, and whether executable fragmentation evidence can identify it once proposed.

Mechanistic controls are reported separately from the standard-task outcomes. They test whether \(V\) responds to the measured spectrum, whether \(W\) remains spectrum-blind and whether latent product supervision changes event selection, but they are not used as headline performance metrics. Model promotion is tied to the standard de novo, retrieval and spectrum-simulation tasks rather than to internal event-support statistics.

\section*{Discussion}

The central change in ORBIT-MS is to make a molecular prediction testable through an executable intermediate representation. Instead of asking only which structure best matches a spectrum, the framework separates molecular proposal from the gas-phase evidence that a candidate can generate. The Generator, Verifier and World are not required to be one neural model; they communicate through the same bond--electron state, coherent-event language and deterministic executor. This separation turns a predicted structure from an endpoint into a hypothesis about observable fragmentation.

Executable does not mean experimentally proven mechanism. The executor enforces electron bookkeeping and materializability, whereas the propensity and relevance of a fragmentation event remain learned. Multiple latent events may produce the same observed mass, and a spectrum-conditioned verifier has access to information unavailable to the free World. The \(p_W/q_V\) correction and explicit product comparison are therefore important conceptually: \(V\) proposes observation-compatible events, \(W\) asks whether those events are plausible without seeing the answer, and the executor determines what chemistry they actually produce.

The present development results also expose where the framework is still weak. No-formula candidate lists already contain the correct composition for most spectra, yet exact-connectivity proposal recall remains low, particularly for larger molecules. The current World produces only modest standard spectrum-simulation similarity, so forward fragmentation remains an independent bottleneck rather than a solved verifier. These limitations are useful because they are separable: improvements to \(G\), \(W\) and WGV reranking can be evaluated under different frozen contracts instead of being hidden inside one end-to-end score.

If these benchmark bottlenecks can be overcome, the same decomposition provides a natural way to represent structural uncertainty in the dark metabolome. A spectrum need not be forced to a unique rank-1 structure; it can support a formula, a bounded candidate set, a shared substructure or a unique molecular hypothesis according to the evidence available. Repository-scale analysis can then operate on recurrent structural entities or families while preserving unresolved cases and provenance. This is the intended bridge from the computational method to the chemical and biological questions addressed in the later sections.

Several limitations remain beyond model accuracy. A bond--electron executor is an approximation to supported gas-phase chemistry rather than a quantum-mechanical mechanism simulator. Executor-supported products used to train \(P2\) and \(W\) are latent hypotheses, not experimental fragment-structure labels. The observed spectrum is incomplete, so an unobserved generated product cannot generally be declared chemically false. Development results are reported on a molecule-group-disjoint partition of the official training fold; official validation and test data remain sealed until the model and evaluation protocol are frozen. Repository-scale entity construction, biological-source attribution and family-level associations introduce additional uncertainties that must be quantified separately.

ORBIT-MS therefore makes a specific empirical proposition: structure elucidation should improve when molecular hypotheses are required to survive an executable forward chemical test. The value of that proposition is determined by matched benchmark gains and by whether the resulting evidence supports reproducible chemical and biological conclusions, not by the complexity of the architecture itself.

\subsection*{Molecular and spectral representations}
ORBIT-MS separates the representation used for molecular construction from the representation used for gas-phase electron redistribution. An experimental spectrum is represented as a variable-length set of peaks \(S=\{(m_i,I_i)\}\) together with precursor \(m/z\), adduct, ionization polarity and available acquisition metadata. Spectrum encoders operate on peak nodes and pairwise relations derived from normalized \(m/z\), intensity, pairwise mass differences and, when available, ordered parent--child MS\(^{n}\) transitions. For spectrum-blind World inference, acquisition context contains only precursor and acquisition variables and never measured fragment-peak features.

Gas-phase fragmentation is represented by a \emph{bond--electron} (BE) state. A BE state stores atom identities and atomic numbers together with a symmetric integer matrix whose diagonal entries are non-bonding valence electrons localized on an atom and whose off-diagonal entries are electrons shared between atom pairs. A conventional single, double or triple bond therefore contains two, four or six shared electrons, while odd occupancies allow radical intermediates without introducing a separate graph-edit language. Charged precursor roots are compiled from the candidate structure and adduct and are retained only when they materialize to the observed precursor mass and polarity.

\subsection*{Exact bond--electron chemical executor}
Learned policies never directly declare a fragment structure. Instead, they propose electron-redistribution events that are applied by a deterministic chemical executor. An electron site is either an atom or an occupied bond, and an elementary arrow transfers one or two electrons from a source site to a target atom or bond. Local masks exclude moves that lack source electrons or immediately exceed supported shell or bond-electron capacities, but they do not encode a finite catalogue of named fragmentation reactions.

For a parent BE state \(x\) and a proposed coherent event \(a\), the executor applies all electron transfers to obtain a new BE state,
\[
x' = T_{\mathrm{exec}}(x,a).
\]
It then checks exact electron bookkeeping and BE symmetry and attempts to materialize the resulting state as explicit chemical products. Successful materialization returns charged and neutral components with product SMILES, elemental formula, formal charge, exact \(m/z\) and the corresponding parent-atom indices. Failed bookkeeping or materialization remains a failed event rather than being repaired by local search. Mechanistic class labels, where recorded, are audit metadata and do not define event legality.

\subsection*{Coherent electron-event parameterization}
The fragmentation policy generates one complete coherent electron event per Monte Carlo draw. An event is an autoregressive sequence of one- or two-electron arrows. For each arrow the policy selects an occupied source site, chooses the electron count, predicts whether the target is an atom or bond, selects the target atom and, for a bond target, its partner atom, and then predicts whether to continue the same coherent event or stop. The partially updated BE matrix is carried through the sequence, so electrons transferred into a site may be used as a later source within the same concerted event. A finite maximum number of arrows is used only as an engineering compute guard.

The event policy uses independent complete samples. It does not perform local top-\(k\) action selection, beam search, sequential Monte Carlo, effective-sample-size resampling, survivor duplication or fallback enumeration of reaction templates. The same event factorization is used by the spectrum-conditioned verifier and the spectrum-blind World; their conditioning information and learned parameters differ.

\subsection*{Peak-to-product posterior}
To provide a soft product-level hypothesis for a selected observed fragment peak \(p=(m_p,I_p)\), ORBIT-MS uses a peak-to-product posterior \(P2\). Given a candidate BE state, acquisition context and the measured spectrum, the posterior encodes the candidate and spectrum once and conditions a target-peak query on the candidate representation, the global spectrum representation, the embedding of the queried peak and numerical target-\(m/z\) features. It predicts a retention logit for every precursor atom and a charge distribution over \(\{-2,-1,+1,+2\}\). The retention probabilities define a soft precursor-atom set, and together with the charge distribution yield an expected product \(m/z\).

The product posterior is trained with weak composition and observed-charge supervision over the full training population and with stronger atom-set and charge supervision only when a training-set event is executed successfully and its product strictly supports the queried peak. These executor-supported products are treated as latent mechanistic hypotheses rather than experimentally observed fragment-structure labels. During verifier training the product posterior is frozen; its atom-retention probabilities, predicted fragment boundary, retention uncertainty and target-mass residual are converted into continuous intent features and injected into the event representation.

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned verifier}
The Verifier \(V\) is a peak-anchored posterior event policy,
\[
q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c),
\]
that proposes coherent electron events capable of explaining a selected observed peak. It combines a guided BE/spectrum encoding of the candidate with the product intent supplied by \(P2\). The measured spectrum affects the verifier through the spectrum encoder and target-peak query; it does not alter the executor or the local chemistry masks.

For each selected peak, \(V\) draws multiple complete events independently and executes each draw exactly once. A draw that cannot be materialized receives a negative reward. Materialized products receive a smooth reward determined by the distance between a same-polarity unit-charge product \(m/z\) and the target peak, with an additional intensity-weighted bonus for a strict mass match. Training uses a group-relative policy-gradient loss within the set of draws for the same target, increasing the probability of events with higher relative reward without treating any single latent mechanism as observed ground truth. Strictly supported products and their exact event traces are stored as training-set latent-event receipts for subsequent product-posterior refresh and World training.

\subsection*{Spectrum-blind gas-phase fragmentation World}
The free World \(W\) parameterizes
\[
p_W(a\mid M,c)
\]
with the same coherent-event factorization as \(V\) but without access to the measured fragment spectrum or a target peak. Its input is the candidate BE state and acquisition context only. During inference, independent World draws are executed by the same deterministic chemical executor to obtain charged products and a spectrum-blind distribution over fragment masses.

The World used in the reported experiments is trained by posterior-to-World trace replay. Exact-supported event traces discovered by \(V\) are grouped by training spectrum and target peak and replayed under \(W\); the loss is the negative log probability of the supported trace under the spectrum-blind policy, balanced across supported peaks. Training cases without a replayable supported trace do not contribute a trace-likelihood gradient. The reported trace-replay objective does not use importance weights. This separation makes \(V\) an observation-conditioned proposal mechanism and \(W\) a spectrum-blind forward model over the same executable event language.

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned molecular generator}
The Generator \(G\) constructs precursor structures directly from the measured spectrum. Its spectrum encoder represents the MS/MS spectrum as a peak-relation graph with peak-node features and pairwise mass/intensity relations. The current molecular construction state is encoded by a graph neural network over atom identity, formal charge, radical state, local degree/valence and current bonds. A separate molecular-goal representation records the remaining elemental inventory or formula hypothesis. Graph--spectrum cross-attention injects the spectral representation into the current molecular graph, followed by message passing conditioned on the construction state, molecular goal and pooled spectrum context.

The construction policy is factorized rather than enumerating the full graph-edit action space. It predicts the action kind, element, formal charge, radical state, attachment site or atom pair and bond order, with hard masks enforcing remaining formula inventory, charge-aware valence, connectivity and legal emission. The primary paired-supervision stage compiles each known precursor structure into legal graph-construction teacher actions and minimizes their negative log likelihood. Optional posterior-distillation and World-reward stages are evaluated separately and are not part of the canonical paired generator unless explicitly stated.

For the standard no-formula de novo task, formula hypotheses are inferred from precursor mass, adduct and TRAIN-derived formula priors using only benchmark-permitted inputs. Ground-truth formula is reserved for the secondary formula-provided setting. At inference time the Generator produces independent ancestral molecular samples; it does not use beam search, top-\(k\) graph expansion, particle filtering or resampling. Invalid or unfinished trajectories remain failures in the denominator.

\subsection*{Integrated WGV evidence and candidate ranking}
For a completed molecular candidate \(M\) and an observed target peak \(p\), \(V\) is used as an inference proposal over coherent events while \(W\) scores the same event traces without seeing the measured fragment spectrum. For each sampled event
\[
a_k\sim q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c),
\]
the exact executor returns explicit charged products. The World replays the identical trace to obtain \(\log p_W(a_k\mid M,c)\), and the observation term is computed from the distance between the closest same-polarity unit-charge product and the target peak. With a fixed Gaussian mass kernel and a finite evidence floor, the event-level log evidence is
\[
\ell_k =
\log p_W(a_k\mid M,c)
-\log q_V(a_k\mid M,S,p,c)
+\log p_{\mathrm{obs}}(p\mid M,a_k),
\]
with an additional precursor-root log weight when multiple charged precursor roots are evaluated.

Event samples for one peak are combined with a log-mean-exp estimator,
\[
E_p(M,S)=\operatorname{logmeanexp}_k \ell_k.
\]
Peak-level evidence is then aggregated using square-root intensity weights,
\[
E(M,S)=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{\sqrt{I_p}}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{I_j}}\,
E_p(M,S),
\]
where \(\mathcal P\) is the selected set of eligible observed fragment peaks. This produces a candidate-specific World evidence score while correcting for the fact that \(V\) was allowed to condition on the observation. For matched molecular candidate pools, integrated WGV ranking combines the frozen Generator score with the World evidence,
\[
\operatorname{Score}(M)=
\operatorname{GScore}(M)+\lambda E(M,S),
\]
and the zero-World setting is required to reproduce the frozen Generator ordering exactly.

\subsection*{Spectrum simulation, molecular retrieval and benchmark protocol}
The forward World is also evaluated independently of the inverse generator. For spectrum simulation, molecular structure and benchmark-permitted acquisition context are supplied to \(W\), independent coherent events are sampled, and their executed charged products are aggregated into a predicted fragment-mass distribution. The measured spectrum is used only by the frozen evaluation metrics. For molecular retrieval, each candidate in the official MassSpecGym candidate universe is passed through the same spectrum-blind World to construct a World-generated fragment-mass fingerprint, which is then matched to the observed spectrum. Fixed bond-cut, neutral-loss or rearrangement rules are retained only as baselines or auxiliary diagnostics and are not the canonical retrieval backend.

Adaptive development uses only a molecule-group-disjoint partition of the official MassSpecGym training fold. The development partition contains 175,548 training spectra from 20,430 molecule groups, 8,401 SEARCH\_DEV spectra from 1,135 molecule groups and 9,782 promotion-calibration spectra from 1,136 molecule groups; official validation and test folds remain sealed during adaptive development. The standard no-ground-truth-formula setting is primary wherever MassSpecGym defines one. De novo generation is evaluated with connectivity-level InChIKey-14 Exact@1/10 together with structural-similarity metrics and a fixed raw generation budget; retrieval uses the official mass-based candidate pool with Hit@1/5/20 and MCES, with the formula-filtered pool reported separately as a bonus setting; and spectrum simulation uses the official sparse cosine and Jensen--Shannon similarity implementations. Every evaluation spectrum and every failed generation remains in the denominator.
