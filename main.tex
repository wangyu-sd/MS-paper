\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is one of the principal technologies for discovering and characterizing small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Modern LC--MS/MS experiments can survey thousands of molecular features in a complex sample, but converting fragmentation spectra into chemical structures remains a central bottleneck. This problem persists even as metabolomics expands from single-cell measurements to population-scale studies: a recent Nature Perspective identified metabolite identification as a shared challenge across these regimes and as a constraint on future metabolic atlases and foundation models.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Spectral-library matching provides strong evidence when an authentic reference has already been measured, yet libraries cover only a small fraction of the chemical diversity encountered in untargeted experiments.\cite{daSilva2015,Wang2016GNPS} Most measured spectra therefore lie between partial structural information and resolved chemical identity.

Computational approaches have substantially improved the molecular hypotheses that can be brought to these spectra. Fragmentation trees, molecular fingerprints and formula-aware models enabled increasingly powerful retrieval and chemical-class inference;\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Hoffmann2022,Xing2023,Goldman2023} forward models learned to reproduce spectra and local fragmentation behaviour from molecular structure;\cite{Wang2021CFMID,Young2024,Nowatzky2025} and de novo generators increasingly construct complete, chemically plausible structures directly from spectra.\cite{Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} DeepMet recently showed that chemical language models can anticipate previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites and combine a learned molecular prior with predicted MS/MS to prioritize candidate structures in complex biological samples.\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet} At the same time, representation learning on millions of unlabelled spectra has expanded the scale at which chemical similarity can be organized.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams} These advances improve hypothesis generation, but they do not guarantee that the experiment contains evidence capable of distinguishing the resulting alternatives.

We therefore separate the structural support available from a molecular candidate from the experimental observations that make that support discriminative. For a molecule $M$ and executable fragmentation program $G$, we define a fragment-mass fingerprint $F_G(M)$ as the set of exact fragment masses supported by the program, together with provenance and authority when available. This representation deliberately avoids assuming that every supported mass is observed, intense or generated by a uniquely known gas-phase mechanism. A measured MS/MS spectrum samples this larger structure-derived support space under acquisition-dependent observability. The same observed mass may be supported by many candidates and therefore carry little identity information; another, possibly weak, peak can be decisive if its mass is supported only by one serious alternative.

This framing also changes how fragmentation knowledge should improve. A fixed $G$ is necessarily incomplete: it can miss informative masses, overgenerate broad low-specificity support or assign insufficient authority to an otherwise useful mass. ORBIT-MS therefore treats the executable FMF/evidence program as a mutable scientific object while keeping the definition of scientific validity outside the mutable search space. Hard candidate sets produce explicit counterexamples. An LLM mutation worker proposes a typed, exact program edit under a preassigned scientific objective and mutation surface; deterministic chemistry and evaluation rules test that edit; replay and ablation determine whether the measured gain is causally attributable to it; and only replay-supported lessons from mutation-eligible data can guide later mutation. Protected held-out, external and prospective evidence can support or refute a final lesson but cannot flow back into adaptive mutation. We use this framework to ask three linked questions: how much molecular identity is carried by FMF space, why that information becomes limiting among increasingly faithful alternatives, and whether verified program evolution can improve the evidence needed to map previously dark metabolomic features.

\section*{Agent-driven evolution improves the executable FMF evidence program}

A fixed FMF generator is incomplete by construction. Hard molecular alternatives exposed three recurrent classes of failure: \result{final missing-support, overgeneration/shared-support and authority/search taxonomy}. Missing-support failures contained experimentally informative masses supported by the ground-truth hypothesis but not reached by the active program; overgeneration failures arose when broad transformations created support shared by many alternatives; authority failures occurred when mass compatibility existed but the available trace could not justify a stronger mechanistic claim. We treated these failures as falsifying counterexamples for the executable FMF/evidence program rather than as labels for unconstrained model fitting.

ORBIT-MS implements scientific evolution as a typed program-revision loop. For each authorized mutation, the campaign freezes the parent program, target surface, failure packet and mutation operator before invoking an LLM worker. The worker returns an exact program edit together with a hypothesis, causal claim and predicted effects, but it cannot choose the scientific objective, alter the evaluator or promote its own proposal. Every mutation is bound to a falsification contract specifying the parent identity, failure evidence, typed surface, exact edit, predicted gain and regression, required evidence and compute budget.

The mutable program operates inside an immutable epistemic kernel. Atom, hydrogen, charge/electron and exact-mass conservation, atom lineage, the prohibition on spectrum-created chemistry edges, dataset/evaluator receipts, protected-data isolation, frozen METEOR pretraining and final promotion authority are not evolvable. Candidate programs are freshly evaluated on mutation-eligible data. An apparent gain receives reusable causal credit only when the exact edit reproduces its predicted improvement on the same dataset receipt and evaluator version without exceeding its compute budget; ablation must remove the gain before the edit is labelled causally localized. We therefore distinguish associated-only, replay-supported and ablation-supported mutations.

Replay-supported changes are compressed into \emph{ScientificLessons} that retain scope, failure signature, exact-edit provenance and predicted trade-offs. Only replay- or ablation-supported lessons derived exclusively from screen/discovery evidence can be supplied to future mutation workers. Held-out, external and prospective evidence can subsequently support or refute a lesson, but that protected evidence makes the lesson ineligible for adaptive reuse. Independent review is similarly separated from repair: a candidate producer cannot review its own candidate, the reviewer cannot perform the repair, and the repaired child inherits no evaluation evidence and must undergo fresh deterministic evaluation.

\section*{Discussion}

Our results recast tandem-mass-spectrometry metabolite elucidation around a structure-derived mass-support space. The field has made rapid progress in molecular hypothesis generation, from formula and fingerprint inference to retrieval, spectrum simulation and complete molecular generation.\cite{Duhrkop2019,Hoffmann2022,Goldman2023,Xing2023,Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX,Qiang2026DeepMet} FMFs address a complementary question: what fragment masses can a structure support, and which of those masses actually distinguish it from the alternatives relevant to the measured spectrum? Sparse FMF combinations can carry substantial molecular identity information, yet the value of any one mass is conditional on the candidate set and on whether the experiment observes it.

This distinction explains why stronger molecular generation need not make elucidation easier. Close same-formula candidates share much of the same fragment-mass support, causing global coverage and spectrum similarity to lose margin as the candidate frontier improves. The resulting evidence bottleneck is not solved by maximizing the size of the fragmentation lattice or explained intensity. Broad support can increase accidental matching; conversely, a weak but candidate-specific mass can carry disproportionate structural information. Atom-traceable CID trajectories therefore serve as a higher-authority grounding layer for selected evidence rather than as a requirement that every observed peak receive a uniquely known mechanism.

The executable nature of FMF generation creates an additional opportunity: fragmentation knowledge can be revised as falsifying cases accumulate. ORBIT-MS does not make the LLM a chemical authority. Instead, the LLM acts as a constrained scientific program-mutation operator inside an immutable epistemic boundary. Exact edits must survive fresh deterministic evaluation, fixed evaluator/data receipts, compute-budget checks and causal replay; only replay-supported mutation-eligible lessons can guide future edits. This design separates proposal from proof and converts self-improvement from unconstrained recursive coding into a falsifiable process for improving a scientific inference program.

At metabolomics scale, the same principle leads to a different kind of resource. A dark-metabolome map should not be a catalogue of forced predicted SMILES. It should preserve the resolution justified by each experiment: exact structure where discriminative evidence and orthogonal validation support it, bounded candidate sets where ambiguity remains, and molecular families where the spectrum cannot decide. Such a resource can still reveal recurrent structural programmes across tissues, conditions or phenotypes while making uncertainty explicit. \result{Insert final resource-scale and biological-discovery synthesis.}

More broadly, metabolite identification remains a shared challenge as metabolomics moves toward cellular and population-scale atlases and foundation models.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Increasing the number or plausibility of molecular hypotheses does not increase the information contained in a fixed spectrum. Reliable elucidation therefore requires both a scalable structural representation and a mechanism for improving the scientific program that interprets it without weakening evidentiary standards. FMF space provides the former; verified agent-driven scientific evolution provides the latter. Together they create a route from passive spectrum-to-structure prediction toward evidence-seeking, auditable and ultimately prospective metabolite discovery.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidentiary separation}

The study separates three authority-bearing objects at every evaluation point: molecular hypotheses, the executable FMF/evidence program and the experimental spectrum. Development, calibration and test partitions are molecule-group disjoint. Candidate pools used for causal comparison are frozen before evaluation, and protected held-out, external or prospective evidence is never used to generate adaptive program mutations.

A mass match, a reaction certificate and a molecular identification are distinct claims. Fragment-mass compatibility can support a candidate at the FMF layer; a reaction-level claim additionally requires a complete atom-traceable trajectory; a unique structural identification requires candidate-relative evidence and the frozen resolution policy. Unsupported adducts, unavailable reaction families and search truncation are reported separately from candidate contradiction.

\]
where $m_i$ is the exact fragment mass supported by the program, $a_i$ denotes the evidence/source authority available for that mass and $\rho_i$ retains provenance such as generating operator, depth and trace identity where available. Duplicate masses are collapsed according to \methodtodo{final binning/provenance retention rule}. The FMF is a structure-derived support space and does not imply that every mass is experimentally observable or that one unique gas-phase structure/mechanism is known.

The initial FMF layers are generated from \methodtodo{final ERSF/CID structural enumeration and reaction basis}. Ablations include depth-1 structural fragments, depth-limited fingerprints, neutral-loss inclusion/exclusion, full-lattice support and high-authority trajectory-certified subsets. The synthetic resolving-power experiment samples $K$ masses from a molecule's FMF and retrieves against a frozen molecular pool; this experiment measures intrinsic representational identity and is reported separately from measured-spectrum evaluation.

For a measured peak $p$ and candidate $M_j$, low-level FMF support $u_{jp}$ records whether an eligible FMF mass falls within the frozen tolerance. Candidate-relative specificity is computed against the same fixed candidate pool, conceptually as
\[

\subsection*{High-authority CID evidence}

The pLSE/CID engine represents fragmentation as a bounded reaction-state network. Ion states record molecular graph, analytical adduct, charge carrier, formal-charge state, proton/sodium and radical state where represented, and root-atom lineage. Elementary events record bond changes, atom/component lineage, formal-charge changes, hydrogen/hydride transfer where represented, product charge carrier, theoretical masses and provenance.

The current CID certificate uses direct complete trajectories as reaction-level evidence under the frozen 0.02-Da mass contract. A mass-reached peak receives positive reaction authority when at least one complete legal certifying trajectory is found. Exhaustive enumeration of unrelated alternative trajectories is not required for positive evidence. In contrast, absence of a certificate contributes negative evidence only when the relevant alternative trajectory search is complete; trajectory-budget truncation is reported as search truncation. Endpoint reachability remains diagnostic rather than reaction-level evidence, and unsupported adducts are \texttt{NOT\_EVALUATED} rather than scientific failures.

Shuffled donors come from different molecule groups and are matched for \result{final adduct, neutral-mass, acquisition and peak-richness constraints}. Spectral and identity lift are aggregated with equal molecule-group weighting.

\subsection*{Agent-driven scientific evolution of the FMF program}

The evolvable scientific target is an explicit algorithm/program genome rather than an unrestricted source tree. Mutable surfaces can include FMF/ERSF branching and depth policy, CID reaction expansion and state priority, pruning/dominance logic, provenance retention, whole-spectrum assignment, candidate evidence scoring, observation-model parameters and selected post-pretraining METEOR programs. Immutable surfaces include the chemistry legality kernel, evaluation contracts, protected-data firewall, pretrained METEOR lineage and Protocol-v2 promotion authority.

Each evolution round begins from a measured failure packet on mutation-eligible data. The campaign selects the scientific target, parent program(s), mutation mode and typed operator before calling the LLM mutation worker. The worker receives parent genomes, failure context, empirical mutation outcomes and eligible ScientificLessons and must return an exact machine-readable genome edit. It cannot select the evaluation objective, read protected evidence, execute arbitrary shell actions or self-report fitness.

Every proposed edit is represented by a ScientificMutation contract containing parent identifiers, typed surface, failure-evidence references, a scientific hypothesis, causal claim, exact edit, predicted gains and regressions, falsification test, required evidence and compute budget. Candidate programs are freshly evaluated under immutable dataset and evaluator receipts. Promotion requires the target scientific endpoint and all hard gates; training loss, optimized reward, model prose or mutation-worker confidence are never promotion evidence.

\subsection*{Causal replay, scientific memory and independent repair}

Accepted archive gains are initially treated as associations. Causal replay evaluates the parent and exact edited program under the same dataset receipt and evaluator version, with an optional ablated program that removes the edit. A predicted gain receives replay support only when all preregistered target metrics exceed their minimum gains and the edit remains within its compute budget. Causal localization additionally requires the ablation to remove the gain. Outcomes are recorded as \texttt{ASSOCIATED\_ONLY}, \texttt{REPLAY\_SUPPORTED} or \texttt{ABLATION\_SUPPORTED}.

Replay-supported outcomes can be compressed into ScientificLessons retaining the target, typed surface, statement, scope, failure signature, raw evidence references, exact-edit provenance and predicted trade-offs. A lesson is reusable for future mutation only when its causal status is replay- or ablation-supported and all source stages belong to mutation-eligible screen/discovery data. Evidence from held-out, external or prospective stages can support or refute a lesson but makes that protected evidence unavailable to adaptive reuse.

Independent review is bound to an immutable candidate/evaluation snapshot. The candidate producer cannot review its own proposal. If repair is requested, the reviewer cannot be the repair actor; the repair creates a new child candidate, inherits no previous evaluation and requires fresh deterministic evaluation. Protocol v2 remains the final promotion authority.

Primary comparisons include full scientific RSI, random/no-agent mutation, no-replay, no-reusable-memory and ungated or weakened-verifier controls. We report accepted/rejected mutation counts, replay-confirmation and ablation-localization rates, GT-decoy discrimination, certified coverage, FMF overgeneration, runtime/expanded-state cost and Pareto efficiency.

Generalization is evaluated only after the evolving program freezes. The final program is applied to disjoint molecule groups, independent candidate generators/retrieval sources and \datatodo{external dataset(s)} without adaptive feedback. Protected evaluation can support or refute the final scientific lesson but never enters mutation memory.

\subsection*{Risk-controlled structural resolution}

Candidate generation and evidence-based discrimination are reported separately. Generator recall is the fraction of molecule groups in which the ground truth is present in the candidate set. Conditional discrimination is evaluated only when the ground truth is present. End-to-end unique resolution combines both components.

A disjoint calibration split constructs structural prediction sets $\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\alpha}(S)$ using \methodtodo{final split-conformal or other risk-control method}. We report empirical set coverage, candidate-set size, unique-resolution rate, conditional accuracy and false-resolution rate. A unique two-dimensional structure is emitted only when the calibrated set contains one candidate. Stereochemical resolution is not claimed from MS/MS alone unless independently supported.
