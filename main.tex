\noindent\textbf{
Tandem mass spectra are incomplete observations of the gas-phase chemistry that generates them, yet molecular structure elucidation is usually treated as a direct mapping from spectrum to structure. Here we introduce ORBIT-MS, which instead treats each molecular prediction as a hypothesis that can be tested by executable fragmentation. A spectrum-conditioned Generator proposes molecular structures, a Verifier proposes coherent electron-redistribution events that explain observed peaks, and a spectrum-blind World evaluates the same events before a deterministic bond--electron executor materializes their charged products. Across molecule-disjoint development data, this design separates molecular proposal quality from forward-fragmentation quality and from evidence-based reranking, yielding \result{final no-formula de novo result}, \result{final spectrum-simulation and retrieval result} and \result{matched WGV reranking result}. We then calibrate the resulting evidence into bounded structural statements and apply them to \result{dark spectrum count} previously unannotated spectra, identifying \result{recurrent entity/family count} recurrent molecular entities or families with \result{headline structural/biological result}. ORBIT-MS therefore turns spectrum-to-structure prediction into a testable chemical inference problem and provides a route from uncertain tandem spectra to auditable structural and biological analysis.
}

\vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is a principal technology for surveying small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} Yet most tandem mass spectra generated by untargeted studies still lack a confident structural assignment.\cite{daSilva2015,Alseekh2021} Spectral libraries, molecular networking, formula and fingerprint prediction, learned spectrum models and de novo generators have all improved the hypotheses that can be proposed for a spectrum.\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Wang2021CFMID,Goldman2023,Bohde2025,Qiang2026DeepMet} Most of these approaches nevertheless evaluate a spectrum through an answer---a similarity score, fingerprint, ranked candidate or generated molecule---rather than through the gas-phase events that could have produced the observation.

This distinction matters because a tandem mass spectrum is only a partial observation of fragmentation chemistry. Peak presence does not identify a unique reaction path, and peak absence does not by itself imply chemical impossibility. A model can therefore produce a plausible structure without stating which executable fragmentation evidence supports it, while a forward spectrum model can fit common peaks without discriminating among closely related structural alternatives. The missing object is an explicit, testable connection between a molecular hypothesis and the gas-phase products that hypothesis can generate.

We address this gap by representing fragmentation in a bond--electron state space and separating molecular proposal from forward chemical evidence. ORBIT-MS combines a spectrum-conditioned molecular Generator \(G\), a spectrum-conditioned Verifier \(V\), a spectrum-blind fragmentation World \(W\) and a deterministic chemical executor. \(G\) proposes molecular hypotheses; \(V\) proposes coherent electron-redistribution events that explain a selected observed peak; \(W\) scores the same event without seeing the fragment spectrum; and the executor determines the explicit charged products and their masses. Candidate ranking can therefore combine inverse-generation confidence with forward evidence while keeping learned preference distinct from chemical executability.

We first evaluate these components through the standard MassSpecGym task families---de novo generation, molecular retrieval and spectrum simulation---and explicitly separate proposal recall from World-based reranking. We then calibrate executable evidence into the strongest structural statement supported by each spectrum rather than forcing every case to a unique identity. Applied to repository-scale dark spectra, this framework is designed to convert anonymous observations into recurrent structural entities or bounded molecular families that can be compared across datasets and related to biological perturbations. The paper therefore proceeds from a testable fragmentation-world hypothesis, through benchmarked structural evidence, to the chemical and biological organization of the dark metabolome.

\section*{Executable fragmentation provides a forward test of molecular hypotheses}

ORBIT-MS was designed to make forward chemistry an explicit test of a molecular prediction. The Generator \(G\) proposes structures from the observed spectrum, whereas the spectrum-blind World \(W\) receives only a candidate molecule and acquisition context. For a candidate and selected observed peak, the Verifier \(V\) proposes a coherent electron event; \(W\) scores that same trace without access to the measured fragment peaks; and the deterministic executor materializes the event into explicit charged products before any evidence is assigned \figureonetodo. This separation allows forward fragmentation to be evaluated independently of molecular generation.

We first tested \(W\) as a spectrum simulator. On the complete 8,401-spectrum molecule-disjoint SEARCH\_DEV development set, the current spectrum-blind World achieves a mean official sparse cosine similarity of 0.09935 and Jensen--Shannon similarity of 0.09199 at the frozen sampling budget. These values define the present forward-model baseline rather than a solved simulation task. The corresponding official molecular-retrieval evaluation, which ranks the mass-based candidate universe using World-generated fragment-mass fingerprints, is reported as \result{complete mass-pool Hit@1/5/20 and MCES@1}; the formula-filtered pool is retained as a secondary bonus setting. Together, simulation and retrieval test whether \(W\) captures experimentally useful fragmentation without allowing the observed spectrum to enter the forward policy.

\section*{Molecular connectivity, rather than formula availability, limits current de novo recovery}

We next evaluated the inverse problem under the standard no-ground-truth-formula setting. Across all 8,401 SEARCH\_DEV spectra, \(G\) recovers the correct connectivity at Exact@1 in 131 cases (1.559\%) and at Exact@10 in 370 cases (4.404\%); the correct structure appears anywhere among 100 independent molecular samples in 464 cases (5.523\%). These results use connectivity-level InChIKey-14 matching and retain failed trajectories in the denominator \figuretwotodo.

The low recall is not primarily explained by failure to propose the elemental composition. The correct formula is present in the mass/adduct-derived proposal lists for 8,140 of 8,401 cases (96.89\%), yet only 464 correct connectivities are generated. The gap widens with molecular size: among 2,110 targets containing at least 30 heavy atoms, the correct formula is available for 1,930 cases but the correct connectivity is sampled in only five. Thus, under the current model and generation budget, precise bond connectivity is the dominant inverse-model bottleneck, rather than formula availability or empty candidate lists.

\section*{World evidence separates reranking from molecular proposal recall}

A structure that is absent from the Generator pool cannot be rescued by a verifier, so we evaluate mechanistic evidence only on fixed candidate sets. For each candidate \(M\) and observed peak \(p\), \(V\) samples events from \(q_V(a\mid M,S,p,c)\); the executor determines the charged products; and \(W\) replays the identical trace to obtain \(p_W(a\mid M,c)\). Event evidence combines the spectrum-blind World probability, correction for the observation-conditioned proposal and agreement between executed product masses and the target peak, and is then aggregated across event samples and observed peaks.

The primary WGV comparison therefore freezes one Generator candidate pool and compares its original ordering with \(G+\lambda E(M,S)\) on exactly the same molecules. A zero-World weight is required to reproduce the frozen Generator ranking case by case. Under this matched protocol, WGV changes Exact@1/10 by \result{matched WGV Exact@1/10 result and paired gain/loss analysis}. This design separates two otherwise conflated questions: whether \(G\) can propose the correct structure at all, and whether executable fragmentation evidence can identify it once proposed.

Mechanistic controls are reported separately from the standard-task outcomes. They test whether \(V\) responds to the measured spectrum, whether \(W\) remains spectrum-blind and whether latent product supervision changes event selection, but they are not used as headline performance metrics. Model promotion is tied to the standard de novo, retrieval and spectrum-simulation tasks rather than to internal event-support statistics.

\section*{Discussion}

The central change in ORBIT-MS is to make a molecular prediction testable through an executable intermediate representation. Instead of asking only which structure best matches a spectrum, the framework separates molecular proposal from the gas-phase evidence that a candidate can generate. The Generator, Verifier and World are not required to be one neural model; they communicate through the same bond--electron state, coherent-event language and deterministic executor. This separation turns a predicted structure from an endpoint into a hypothesis about observable fragmentation.

Executable does not mean experimentally proven mechanism. The executor enforces electron bookkeeping and materializability, whereas the propensity and relevance of a fragmentation event remain learned. Multiple latent events may produce the same observed mass, and a spectrum-conditioned verifier has access to information unavailable to the free World. The \(p_W/q_V\) correction and explicit product comparison are therefore important conceptually: \(V\) proposes observation-compatible events, \(W\) asks whether those events are plausible without seeing the answer, and the executor determines what chemistry they actually produce.

The present development results also expose where the framework is still weak. No-formula candidate lists already contain the correct composition for most spectra, yet exact-connectivity proposal recall remains low, particularly for larger molecules. The current World produces only modest standard spectrum-simulation similarity, so forward fragmentation remains an independent bottleneck rather than a solved verifier. These limitations are useful because they are separable: improvements to \(G\), \(W\) and WGV reranking can be evaluated under different frozen contracts instead of being hidden inside one end-to-end score.

If these benchmark bottlenecks can be overcome, the same decomposition provides a natural way to represent structural uncertainty in the dark metabolome. A spectrum need not be forced to a unique rank-1 structure; it can support a formula, a bounded candidate set, a shared substructure or a unique molecular hypothesis according to the evidence available. Repository-scale analysis can then operate on recurrent structural entities or families while preserving unresolved cases and provenance. This is the intended bridge from the computational method to the chemical and biological questions addressed in the later sections.

Several limitations remain beyond model accuracy. A bond--electron executor is an approximation to supported gas-phase chemistry rather than a quantum-mechanical mechanism simulator. Executor-supported products used to train \(P2\) and \(W\) are latent hypotheses, not experimental fragment-structure labels. The observed spectrum is incomplete, so an unobserved generated product cannot generally be declared chemically false. Development results are reported on a molecule-group-disjoint partition of the official training fold; official validation and test data remain sealed until the model and evaluation protocol are frozen. Repository-scale entity construction, biological-source attribution and family-level associations introduce additional uncertainties that must be quantified separately.

ORBIT-MS therefore makes a specific empirical proposition: structure elucidation should improve when molecular hypotheses are required to survive an executable forward chemical test. The value of that proposition is determined by matched benchmark gains and by whether the resulting evidence supports reproducible chemical and biological conclusions, not by the complexity of the architecture itself.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and implementation authority}
ORBIT-MS separates three learning problems: estimating fragment-structure
priors from a known molecular graph without access to its measured product
spectrum (World, \(W\)); updating those priors with a measured MS/MS spectrum
(Verifier, \(V\)); and inferring precursor connectivity from the spectrum with
chemically constrained graph denoising (Generator, \(G\)).
The current implementation uses the evaluated PR86 three-cut CandidateWorld,
the evaluated PR87 VSetPosterior and the PR91 spectrum-conditioned CoCoGraph V2
generator. The three-cut fragment model is distinct from historical
bond--electron event policies retained in the repository: no electron-arrow
trajectory, peak-to-product event policy, or exact bond--electron event
executor is required by this canonical W/V-to-G path.
The CoCoGraph component adopts a constrained double-edge-swap (DES)
generation kernel and a separate time estimator
\cite{RuizBotella2026CoCoGraph}; the spectrum conditioning and
fragment-derived atom/pair evidence are ORBIT-MS-specific additions.

\subsection*{Experimental spectra, paired structures and held-out boundaries}
A measured query contains observed \((m/z,\text{intensity})\) pairs,
precursor \(m/z\), adduct and available collision-energy and instrument
metadata. MassSpecGym provides flat MS2 peak lists, not observed MS\(^{n}\)
fragmentation ancestry. We therefore preserve the measured peaks and
intensities, represent the precursor separately as metadata and set
observed parent--child transition channels to zero. Numerical peak-mass
differences, neutral-loss compatibility and inferred fragment subformulas
are model features, never substituted for measured MS\(^{n}\) edges.
No artificial high-intensity precursor peak is inserted.

The TRAIN-derived corpus comprises 175,548 experimental spectra from
20,430 molecule groups. PR91 V2 defines 18,425 groups for fitting and 2,005
TRAIN-internal groups for pre-refit G diagnostics; its final declared epoch
includes all 20,430 TRAIN groups. The development SEARCH\_DEV authority
contains 8,401 spectra from 1,135 molecule groups and is not the official
MassSpecGym TEST set. W and V were previously fitted on the full TRAIN
corpus, so the 2,005-group G diagnostic is \emph{not} an independent
out-of-sample test of W/V. Official benchmark validation and test data
remain sealed for model development.

\subsection*{Spectrum-blind structural fragmentation World}
For a candidate precursor \(M\) and acquisition context \(c\), the
PR86 World enumerates connected heavy-atom subsets obtainable by cutting
at most three bonds in the precursor graph. For each structural mask,
mass hypotheses include integer hydrogen offsets from \(-6\) to \(+6\)
and permitted charge-carrier channels, with explicit constraints imposed
by the adduct, total hydrogen inventory and precursor mass.
Thus a candidate fragment object
\(f=(A,\Delta H,\mathrm{carrier},m_f)\) records its precursor heavy-atom
mask \(A\), provisional hydrogen adjustment, carrier identity and calculated
mass. These are mass-compatible structural hypotheses, not independently
confirmed gas-phase fragment structures or executed electron pathways.

A three-layer, 128-dimensional graph message-passing encoder produces
atomic states from elemental, bond, valence and other local molecular
features. A candidate scorer pools the retained-atom representation,
its complement and a global graph/acquisition representation. It predicts
relative logits for structural-mask/ion-channel variants and a separate
unexplained-peak (noise) logit. The inference interface exports calculated
fragment masses, normalized candidate priors and precursor-atom masks
without reading the target MS/MS spectrum:
\[
p_W(f\mid M,c).
\]
During TRAIN fitting only, observed peak masses weakly supervise the
candidate scores through a listwise mass-match objective. Eligible
fragment variants within 0.005 Da of a measured peak compete to explain
its normalized intensity; peaks outside the enumerated support contribute
through the noise branch. No experimentally observed peak-to-fragment
atom mapping is assumed. The conditional World evaluation reports
fragment-intensity-weighted mass coverage separately from structural
identification; high coverage does not establish the correctness of a
fragment mechanism or de novo recovery.

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned fragment-set Verifier}
The PR87 Verifier receives a \emph{frozen} World candidate set for a
specified precursor graph and the actual measured spectrum \(S\).
Its atom graph encoder has two message-passing layers with 96-dimensional
hidden representations. Each candidate contains its atom mask, calculated
mass, hydrogen/carrier attributes and frozen World prior. Measured peaks
are embedded using relative \(m/z\), neutral loss relative to the
precursor, intensity and periodic mass features. Candidate-to-peak
attention combines global spectral context with candidate-local
evidence.

Let \(s_\theta(f,M,S)\) denote the learned compatibility logit.
The candidate-set posterior is
\[
q_V(f\mid M,S)
=\operatorname{softmax}_{f\in\mathcal F_W(M)}
\left[\log p_W(f\mid M,c)+s_\theta(f,M,S)\right].
\]
With a Gaussian mass kernel
\(K_{fj}=\exp[-(m_f-m_j)^2/(2\sigma^2)]\),
\(\sigma=0.0025\) Da, the spectrum evidence over subprecursor fragment
peaks is
\[
E_V(M,S)=\sum_j \bar I_j
\log\left(\epsilon+
\sum_{f\in\mathcal F_W(M)}q_V(f\mid M,S)K_{fj}\right),
\qquad \epsilon=0.001.
\]
Here \(\bar I_j\) are normalized eligible-peak intensity weights.
V is trained with whole-spectrum evidence, matched versus
different-molecule peer-spectrum contrast and structurally strict,
same-formula/same-acquisition-context true-versus-decoy contrast where
eligible. It does not use experimentally labelled fragment structures.
The resulting posterior is restricted to W's candidate set and is not
an externally calibrated posterior over all possible fragmentation
mechanisms or molecular structures.

\subsection*{Transferring fragmentation evidence to graph connectivity}
For each current candidate molecular graph, W emits fragments with
precursor-atom masks and V assigns spectrum-conditioned weights and
mass support. A validated heavy-atom isomorphism maps these W masks onto
the explicit-hydrogen CoCoGraph state; an unchecked atom-index match is
not used. The implemented PR91 V2 adapter aggregates three atom-level,
six symmetric atom-pair and two global channels. Pair channels describe
posterior- and mass-weighted co-membership of two atoms in a fragment,
their separation by a fragment boundary and ambiguity in fragment
membership. Global features include unexplained fragment-peak
intensity and normalized V posterior entropy.

These signals express \emph{fragment observability}, not an observed
bond-edit instruction. In particular, a structural cut required to
form a candidate fragment is not interpreted as evidence that the
corresponding precursor bond should be deleted during denoising.
Learned atom/pair/global projections acquire signed effects on G's
remove, add and clean-bond logits only from paired TRAIN clean-graph
supervision. An experimental peak with no compatible W candidate
contributes to unexplained-intensity diagnostics; it is not assigned
a fabricated atom or bond.

\subsection*{Spectrum-conditioned CoCoGraph V2 molecular generator}
The primary inverse model starts from target-free precursor data.
A separate TRAIN-fitted formula posterior produces up to eight neutral
formula hypotheses from the measured precursor/adduct and spectral
information. A formula-only constructor generates up to four chemically
valid initial molecular graphs per hypothesis without access to the
reference structure or a retrieved target candidate. CoCoGraph states
contain explicit hydrogen atoms, formal charges and bond multiplicities.
The pinned connected DES operator replaces two disjoint bond units
with one of the two cross-pairings and validates the resulting graph.
This move preserves the relevant atom inventory, charge and
incident bond-unit-degree constraints; consequently, certain target
connectivities cannot be reached from incompatible initializers.

The spectrum encoder uses measured-peak features and numerical
peak-pair relations from the original inverse-G relation Transformer,
augmented with continuous Gaussian--Fourier \(m/z\) and neutral-loss
features. Model-inferred peak-formula compatibility relations remain
separate from observed-ancestry channels. Current graph atoms attend
to the spectrum through graph--spectrum cross-attention, followed
by edge-aware GINE message passing. A symmetric, \(O(N^2)\)
atom-pair head predicts whether current bond units should be removed,
where connections should be added and the target clean bond order
(0, 1, 2 or 3). The model does not construct a dense \(N^4\)
quadruplet action tensor or enumerate complete candidate molecules for
every DES decision.

An independent, graph-only CoCoGraph time model estimates a
normalized corruption level for the current graph. Its prediction
is a learned denoising/trajectory-selection heuristic, not physical
fragmentation time and not a certified distance to the true molecule.
The pairwise heads select factors of a constrained DES step; the exact
graph-swap executor checks connectivity, charge/degree requirements
and RDKit validity before accepting a proposal.

\subsection*{Paired clean-graph supervision for G and the time model}
Known TRAIN structures generate valid CoCoGraph forward-noising
trajectories. For each molecule group, the V2 trainer retains its
clean graph and suitable shallow and deep corrupted states, and rotates
available real spectra for that group. The denoiser is supervised
against the \emph{complete clean adjacency} \(A_0\), not only the
last randomly sampled inverse swap. Removal/addition labels indicate
disagreements between current and clean bond units; a separate
four-class head predicts clean bond order. The time model learns
normalized corruption depth and \(t=0\) for clean graphs. The known
inverse DES receives a lower-weight auxiliary loss. Eligible
different-molecule, same-formula/adduct/acquisition-context spectra
supply a contrastive signal; absent peers are skipped rather than
synthesized.

The source implementation defines
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_G={}&\mathcal L_{\mathrm{remove}}
+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{add}}
+0.50\mathcal L_{\mathrm{clean}}
+0.50\mathcal L_{\mathrm{time}}\\
&+0.15\mathcal L_{\mathrm{inverse}}
+0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{spectral\ contrast}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Training uses four group-disjoint fitting epochs and one declared
full-TRAIN refit epoch. Frozen W/V features are introduced only
for a bounded subset of clean or shallow corrupted states in later
epochs, with conditional dropout so that the spectrum-only G path
remains trained. Matched and peer W/V posteriors are recomputed
independently on the same current graph; the matched posterior is
never reused for the peer spectrum. If one member of a spectral
contrast pair lacks valid W/V evidence, both contrast branches
omit W/V rather than inherit inconsistent evidence.

\subsection*{W/V-guided sampling and terminal molecular ranking}
At inference, each initial graph follows a bounded sequence of at
most 32 directly sampled legal DES attempts. The G denoiser always
observes the measured MS/MS. The independent time model evaluates
accepted states, and the two lowest predicted-noise snapshots and
terminal snapshot are retained per trajectory before connectivity
deduplication. Online W/V feedback is restricted to one selected
late trajectory per predicted formula, with spectrum-aware trajectory
allocation and structured W/V evidence on current source graphs.
At remaining-step checkpoints 5 and 2, the implemented sampler
directly proposes at most two DES child graphs and scores only
the actually generated children using frozen W/V. The next
state is sampled probabilistically from a bounded combination of
G action likelihood, predicted graph noise time and within-sibling
standardized V evidence. Neither exhaustive action enumeration nor
monotonic improvement in V score is assumed.

Terminal scoring is separate from online guidance. For a generated
candidate \(M\) supported by predicted formula \(F\), the source
terminal W/V ranker uses
\[
\mathrm{Score}_{WV}(M,S)
=E_V(M,S)+0.5\log p_{\mathrm{formula}}(F\mid S),
\]
whereas the no-W/V arm uses the declared formula/time ranking rule.
At most 100 unique connectivity candidates are returned. Generation
writes target-free candidate and provenance records before any reference
structure is read for post-hoc scoring. The primary comparisons are:
G without W/V, the \emph{identical generated pool} with terminal W/V
only, and online W/V plus terminal W/V. This separates gains from
ranking existing molecules from gains in generating otherwise absent
connectivities.

\subsection*{Planned confidence-gated self-distillation on unlabelled spectra}
\methodtodo{This subsection specifies the planned next-stage algorithm.
Implement and freeze the pseudo-label calibrator, unlabelled data manifest,
acceptance thresholds, number of rounds and independent evaluation gates
before retaining completed-tense claims in a submission version. No
multi-round G improvement has yet been established.}

The intended outer loop begins with the supervised G checkpoint \(G_0\)
and freezes W, V and the precursor-formula model. For each real
unlabelled experimental spectrum \(S_u\), \(G_t\) generates a
target-free, formula-consistent candidate set \(\mathcal C_t(S_u)\)
at a fixed compute budget. Frozen W/V assigns candidate-specific
fragment evidence. These scores are \emph{relative compatibility}
measures rather than ground-truth structural probabilities. A separately
established calibration and abstention rule will convert them into a
weighted pseudo-target distribution
\(\tilde q_t(M\mid S_u)\) over eligible candidates, with an explicit
rejection option when discrimination is insufficient. The same-formula
decoy difficulty, score margins, coverage and ambiguity must be checked
on reference-confirmed molecules independent of W/V fitting;
PR91's 2,005 TRAIN-internal G calibration groups alone cannot meet
that independence condition.

A retained pseudo-structure \(M\) supplies a provisional clean graph
\(A_0(M)\). Using the same legal forward DES corruption kernel as
supervised G, the intended learning signal is weighted clean-graph
denoising rather than direct regression to the W/V score:
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pseudo}}=
\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{accepted}}}w_u
\sum_{M\in\mathcal C_t(S_u)}
\tilde q_t(M\mid S_u)
\mathbb E_{X\sim q_{\mathrm{DES}}(\cdot\mid M)}
\left[\mathcal L_{\mathrm{denoise}}(G_{t+1};X,S_u,A_0(M))\right].
\]
This objective is \emph{proposed}, not an existing PR91 training
entry point. Subsequent fitting will retain the original
experimentally paired loss alongside pseudo-supervision, and
will update G only. Frozen W/V will not be retrained on the same
unconfirmed pseudo-structures in the initial loop.
Each proposed promotion \(G_t\rightarrow G_{t+1}\) must pass
a predeclared independent, non-pseudo-labelled structural benchmark
with molecule-level confidence intervals and calibrated uncertainty;
otherwise the preceding checkpoint is retained. Controls must compare
W/V-selected pseudo-targets to G-only selection, shuffled-spectrum
selection and no self-distillation under matched generation budgets.
This design cannot recover chemistry wholly absent from the G
candidate support, and repeated high W/V agreement does not
independently establish a novel chemical structure.

\subsection*{Benchmarks, conditional evidence and ablation controls}
The canonical PR91 V2 de novo protocol uses predicted, not
ground-truth, formulas; neither oracle formula nor molecular
fingerprints are passed to proposal, initialization, denoising or
candidate ranking. Post-hoc structural metrics may compute
connectivity-level 14-character InChIKey agreement and
structural similarity after candidate generation, never as
training teachers or model inputs. All 8,401 SEARCH\_DEV cases,
including invalid and empty outputs, belong in reported
denominators. Primary metrics are Exact@1, Exact@10,
raw generated Recall@100, ranked Reach@100, formula Top-\(k\)
containment, candidate diversity, invalid-step frequency,
run time and molecule-group bootstrap intervals.

W and V are also audited conditionally on independently supplied
precursor graphs. W mass coverage and V fragment posterior
retrieval are not substitutes for de novo structural recovery.
For W/V causality, compare spectrum-matched with shuffled/peer
observations and test same-formula structural decoys. For online
generation, freeze checkpoint, formula hypotheses, initial draws
and attempt budgets across no-W/V, terminal-only and online
feedback arms. The terminal-only arm must reuse precisely the
no-W/V generated graph set. Compare the raw candidate sets
separately from their final rankings, including chemistry/size
strata and failures. Any future self-distillation experiment
must additionally measure gain across rounds on independently
held-out molecules and record pseudo-target rejection rates,
confidence calibration and drift.
