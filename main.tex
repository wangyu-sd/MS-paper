\noindent\textbf{
Untargeted metabolomics detects far more molecules than it can identify, and the unidentified majority---the dark metabolome---is normally discarded because no method reports what a spectrum does and does not determine.\cite{daSilva2015,Bittremieux2023,Alexandrov2026Perspective} Here we resolve the dark metabolome by simulating the physical process that produces a tandem mass spectrum. An executable fragmentation model derives, for any candidate structure, the fragment masses it can support together with atom-balanced dissociation trajectories, so that a candidate can be eliminated by a peak it cannot produce rather than merely down-ranked by a similarity score. Because elimination is mechanistic rather than statistical, what survives defines the most specific structural statement the measurement supports, and its confidence is calibrated rather than assigned: predicted confidence matches observed accuracy on structures deposited in public libraries only after our system was frozen, an assessment in which the answers were generated by independent laboratories after the predictions were made \result{insert calibration error and blinded accuracy}. \vspace{1em}

Mass spectrometry is the principal technology for surveying small molecules in biology, medicine and the environment.\cite{Wang2016GNPS,Alseekh2021,Gentry2024} A single untargeted LC--MS/MS experiment resolves thousands of molecular features, yet only a small percentage are assigned a structure; the rest are recorded, counted and then set aside.\cite{daSilva2015,Alseekh2021} This is not merely an annotation backlog. Chemically anonymous features cannot be grouped into structural families, ordered into transformation series, or attributed to host, microbial or dietary metabolism, so the biology they encode is inaccessible in principle rather than merely unlabelled.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective}

The obstacle is usually described as insufficient accuracy, but the more consequential problem is the form of the answer. Every scalable annotation method returns a ranked candidate list with a similarity score.\cite{Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Wang2021CFMID,Goldman2023} Rankings do not compose across evidence, do not carry the provenance needed for audit, and---critically---do not distinguish a spectrum that determines a unique structure from one that cannot distinguish a hundred isomers. Practitioners are therefore left to verify candidates by manual expert inspection, which does not scale, and the community has explicitly called for tools that instead communicate the maximum knowledge derivable from the data together with the additional experiment needed to go further.\cite{Bittremieux2023} The confidence-level conventions that encode this idea are assigned by human judgement after the fact rather than computed from the measurement.\cite{Schymanski2014}

Recent work has greatly improved the molecular hypotheses available for a spectrum. Fragmentation trees, fingerprint predictors and formula-aware models transformed retrieval and compound-class inference;\cite{Duhrkop2015,Duhrkop2019,Duhrkop2021,Hoffmann2022,Xing2023,Goldman2023} forward models learned to reproduce fragmentation behaviour from structure;\cite{Wang2021CFMID,Young2024,Nowatzky2025} de novo generators construct complete structures directly from spectra;\cite{Stravs2022,Bohde2025,Wang2026MetGenX} chemical language models anticipate metabolites that have never been observed;\cite{Qiang2026DeepMet} and self-supervised representation learning has organized hundreds of millions of unannotated spectra by chemical similarity.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams} These advances enlarge and improve the candidate space. They do not establish which candidates the experiment can actually exclude, and under leakage-controlled evaluation the exact-structure accuracy of the strongest de novo systems remains low enough that a database of unique predicted structures would not be usable as a resource.\cite{MassSpecGym2024,Bohde2025}

We take a different route to a usable answer. Rather than scoring how well a candidate resembles a spectrum, we simulate the physical process that generated it. For a candidate structure, an executable fragmentation model (pLSE) enumerates dissociation events under explicit atom, charge and hydrogen-transfer accounting and returns the set of fragment masses the structure can support, each with the trajectory that produces it. A peak that no trajectory of a candidate can reach is evidence against that candidate, not a small penalty. Elimination of this kind composes across peaks and is machine-checkable, so the set of candidates surviving all observed peaks has a well-defined meaning. When reference candidates do not exist---the normal situation in the dark metabolome---a spectrum-conditioned hypothesis model (METEOR) supplies them through a reasoning trace whose intermediate claims are individually verifiable against the same fragmentation model.

Two consequences follow, and together they make a resource possible. First, the output is not a rank but the most specific true statement the surviving set admits: a unique structure when one candidate survives, a bounded isomer set or a shared substructure or chemical class otherwise. The structural resolution level is therefore computed from the evidence rather than assigned by convention.\cite{Schymanski2014} Second, because elimination rests on mechanism rather than on similarity to training data, its reliability can be calibrated and, crucially, calibrated against chemistry the system has never encountered. We exploit the fact that public spectral libraries grow: freezing the system against a historical library snapshot and evaluating it on spectra that were dark at that time, but whose structures were subsequently determined and deposited by independent laboratories, provides a blinded prospective test in which the answers did not exist when the predictions were made.

We use this system to ask what the dark metabolome is made of. We report the system and its calibration, the structural atlas obtained by applying it to mouse and repository-scale spectra, the size and chemical composition of the dark metabolome that the atlas reveals, the partition of dark structural families by biological origin, and the principal biological programme that becomes visible once anonymous features acquire structure.

\section*{Physical fragmentation modelling resolves spectra that spectral similarity cannot}

\section*{Structural confidence calibrated against prospective library growth}

A resource is only usable if the confidence attached to each entry means what it says. We defined, for each spectrum, a confidence that the reported structural statement contains the true structure, derived from the stringency of the eliminations that produced it and from the completeness of the fragmentation model over the relevant chemistry (Methods). Because mechanistic completeness is empirical rather than provable---no fragmentation model covers all rearrangement chemistry\cite{Goldman2023,Wang2021CFMID}---we treat soundness as a quantity to be controlled rather than assumed, and tune elimination stringency to a target rate of excluding the true structure.

Calibration on held-out library spectra was accurate but potentially optimistic, because held-out structures resemble training chemistry. We therefore constructed a blinded prospective assessment from the growth of public spectral libraries. The complete system, including the fragmentation model, the hypothesis model and the calibration mapping, was frozen against a \datatodo{library snapshot date} snapshot of GNPS, MassBank and MoNA. We then evaluated it on spectra that were unannotated under that snapshot but whose structures were independently determined and deposited by other laboratories before \datatodo{evaluation snapshot date}. \section*{A structural atlas of the mouse and repository-scale dark metabolome}

We applied the frozen system to \datatodo{mouse cohort description and spectrum count} and to \result{final count} unannotated tandem mass spectra mined from public repositories.\cite{Bushuiev2026Dreams} Spectra with an accepted exact library match under the frozen search protocol were removed, and the remainder---the dark subset---was processed without any access to sample, tissue or phenotype labels.

A central difficulty in describing the dark metabolome is that its feature count is not its molecule count. Adducts, in-source fragments, isotopologues, multiply charged forms and redundant acquisitions of the same molecule inflate feature tables by an unknown factor, and this ambiguity has prevented the field from stating how much unidentified chemistry actually exists.\cite{Alexandrov2026Perspective} Structural resolution removes the ambiguity: entries resolved to the same structure, isomer set or substructure can be collapsed on chemical rather than spectral grounds. \section*{Discussion}

The dark metabolome has been treated as a problem of insufficient accuracy, to be solved when structure prediction becomes good enough to name every molecule. Our results suggest a different and more immediately achievable framing. What blocks the use of unidentified spectra is not that predictions are imperfect but that their output form conveys no reliable information about its own limits, so a correct prediction and a hallucinated one are indistinguishable to the downstream analyst. Simulating the physical fragmentation process changes the output form: candidates are eliminated by peaks they cannot produce, what survives is a statement rather than a rank, and the reliability of that statement can be calibrated against chemistry the system has never seen. A resource built on statements of controlled reliability is usable at scale even though only \result{fraction} of its entries name a unique structure---the same trade that made large-scale predicted protein structures useful despite substantial low-confidence content.

Two aspects of the resulting atlas revise how the dark metabolome should be understood. Its size, once features are collapsed onto structures, is \result{insert relation} smaller than feature counts imply, which changes the scale of the identification problem the field faces. Its composition includes a substantial fraction of chemistry that is not a near neighbour of any characterized metabolite, and that fraction is systematically invisible to the analogue-propagation, reaction-network and learned-prior strategies that currently drive metabolite discovery,\cite{Bittremieux2023,Gentry2024,Qiang2026DeepMet} because those strategies locate new molecules by their similarity to known ones. Mechanistic elimination does not depend on that similarity, which is why it reaches this fraction at all.

Important limitations remain. Mechanistic completeness is empirical: our elimination stringency is calibrated to a measured rate of excluding true structures, and chemistry poorly covered by the fragmentation model is protected by lower reported confidence rather than by a guarantee. Prospective validation is limited to chemistry that independent laboratories happened to characterize during the freeze interval, which over-represents tractable compounds. Biological origin attribution rests on public perturbation data and establishes dependence, not biosynthesis. Structures reported above the reference-standard level are hypotheses of controlled reliability, not identifications, and we have kept that boundary explicit throughout.

The atlas is released with per-entry evidence: the surviving candidate set, the eliminating peaks, the atom-level trajectories supporting each retained assignment, the calibrated confidence, and, for unresolved entries, the measurement that would resolve them. That last field converts the dark metabolome from a static backlog into a prioritized experimental agenda, which is what the community asked for when it called for tools that report the maximum knowledge the data support and the experiment needed to go further.\cite{Bittremieux2023}
