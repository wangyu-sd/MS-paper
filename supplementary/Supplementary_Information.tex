\section{Scope and reading order}

This document distinguishes completed component-level mass-spectrometric experiments from prospective validation and dark-metabolome applications that remain planned. The currently evaluated fragmentation World predicts structure-conditioned fragment hypotheses, the Verifier conditions those hypotheses on observed tandem spectra, and the inverse molecular generator is evaluated separately by whether it proposes the correct connectivity. The completed benchmark tables below report their actual denominators, controls and limitations. Sections containing unexecuted experimental requirements are explicitly marked as such; they do not constitute completed discoveries or prospective chemical identifications.

\section{Spectrum-blind structural fragmentation World and conditional Verifier}

\subsection{Fragment hypotheses from a supplied precursor structure}
For an input precursor molecular graph $M$ and acquisition context $c$, the implemented World $W$ constructs connected precursor heavy-atom subsets obtainable by cutting at most three heavy-atom bonds. Each retained subset is combined with permissible integer hydrogen adjustments, ion-carrier hypotheses and mass constraints derived from the precursor composition and adduct. Candidate fragments thus retain a heavy-atom mapping to $M$ together with calculated mass and charge-carrier information. These candidates are \emph{structural and mass hypotheses}; a cut in the source graph is not an experimentally validated gas-phase cleavage mechanism or an executable sequence of electron transfers.

\subsection{World scoring without target-spectrum access}
The World encodes the precursor graph and acquisition metadata, ranks its structural fragment hypotheses and exports their masses, atom masks and normalized prior weights. Its forward inference cannot read the measured fragment peaks of the query being evaluated. During model fitting, experimental fragment masses and intensities provide \emph{weak} supervision through a mass-matching likelihood; they do not reveal the atom identities of experimental product ions. A separate unexplained-peak term prevents every observed signal from being forcibly assigned to the enumerated fragments. For the completed development evaluation, an extended three-cut component was mixed with the prior two-cut candidate distribution using weights selected on the distinct model-selection partition. Fragment support is measured with a 0.005-Da mass window and a bounded predicted-mass budget; this is not equivalent to official full-spectrum cosine similarity.

\subsection{Spectrum-conditioned fragment-set verification}
For a fixed candidate precursor graph, the Verifier $V$ receives the \emph{same frozen} World fragment set together with the experimental MS/MS spectrum. It computes spectrum-conditioned compatibility scores over World fragment objects using their precursor atom masks, masses, hydrogen/carrier attributes, prior probabilities and the observed peak distribution. Its posterior reweights fragment hypotheses and can rank evidence sets associated with competing molecular structures. Since no experimental fragment-to-atom mapping is provided, a mass-compatible predicted product cannot be taken as a known experimental fragment structure. Comparisons between the measured spectrum and unrelated measured spectra keep the candidate structures fixed, isolating whether verification truly responds to the query's fragment evidence.

\subsection{Scope and limitations of conditional evidence}
The measured tandem mass spectrum is only a partial observation of precursor fragmentation. An unobserved predicted fragment is not necessarily chemically impossible, and missing predicted support may reflect absent chemistry in the World, ion-state ambiguity, noise or incomplete measurements. Distinct subgraphs can produce equal or near-equal fragment masses. Consequently, a mass match establishes neither a specific fragmentation pathway nor unique precursor connectivity. The completed World/Verifier tables below evaluate \emph{conditional fragment-mass evidence given a known precursor structure}, not end-to-end unknown-precursor identification. Older bond--electron transfer agents, explicit electron-event executors and reaction-DAG planners remain separate historical developments and must not be silently substituted for this frozen three-cut World.

\section{Levels of structural evidence and remaining validation}

\subsection{What is measured and what remains conditional}
The current components provide a hierarchy of observations rather than a guarantee of molecular identification:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Precursor composition:} a molecular formula is compatible with the measured precursor mass, adduct and observed MS/MS evidence.
\item \textbf{Candidate validity:} a molecular graph satisfies the chosen valence, formula and connectivity constraints.
\item \textbf{Conditional fragment support:} given a proposed precursor graph, the spectrum-blind World predicts masses that coincide with observed fragment peaks.
\item \textbf{Candidate discrimination:} the measured spectrum preferentially supports one fixed molecular candidate over independently selected structural alternatives.
\item \textbf{Reference-based benchmark recovery:} when the true molecular structure is known to the evaluator, exact-connectivity recall and final rank are computed after inference.
\item \textbf{Independent calibrated statement or authentic-standard confirmation:} prospective confidence calibration, validated alternatives and orthogonal confirmation are required before a dark-spectrum prediction can be elevated to a reported identification.
\end{enumerate}

The first five items are distinct \emph{measurement types}, not interchangeable confidence levels. Fragment-mass support is not evidence of an executed reaction path. A correct formula does not imply exact connectivity, and a target structure present in a candidate list does not imply correct ranking. The final item remains a planned validation stage, not a completed outcome of the experiments tabulated below.

\subsection{Planned bounded structural statements and calibration}
An eventual annotation system may return a proposed unique connectivity, a bounded isomer set, a shared scaffold or chemical class, a molecular formula only, or an unresolved result. Such statements must be accompanied by independent uncertainty calibration; absent that calibration they remain structural \emph{hypotheses}. Community annotation confidence levels and standard-confirmed identifications must be assigned according to their respective experimental requirements, not inferred from World support alone.

\methodtodo{Before reporting calibrated dark-structure resolution, define a structure-disjoint confidence calibration cohort; show false-exclusion/containment versus retained resolution under fixed thresholds; validate the effect of candidate-pool support and World incompleteness; verify the correspondence to accepted metabolomics identification levels; report all failed confirmations. The present benchmark development-set results do not satisfy these requirements.}

\section{Forward fragment prediction, verification and inverse molecular inference}

\subsection{Conditional forward prediction}
Given a hypothesized molecular structure and acquisition context, the implemented World produces a probability-weighted set of fragment-mass and precursor-atom-mask hypotheses without access to the target fragment spectrum. This forward mapping tests whether an already proposed candidate is compatible with a measured spectrum, but its fragment masks are not experimentally established reaction intermediates.

\subsection{Observation-conditioned verification}
The Verifier uses observed peak masses and intensities to redistribute probability within the frozen World fragment set. Fragment-weighted evidence can discriminate two precursor hypotheses only after both molecular candidates have been supplied. When comparing measured with mismatched spectra, the molecular candidate identities and scoring budget are held fixed; separate structural-decoy tests evaluate whether the evidence favors the known precursor over an isomeric alternative. Fragment-set ranking remains a conditional measurement, not inverse generation.

\subsection{Inverse molecular proposals and topology revision}
For unknown precursor structures, the inverse model must produce complete, chemically valid candidate graphs from the measured spectrum. Earlier completed experiments used molecular fingerprint-related retrieval and local graph-editing priors; their results are retained only as historical benchmark diagnostics. The newer source implementation studies spectrum-conditioned constrained molecular graph denoising, with double-edge-swap transitions and optional structured World/Verifier evidence projected to graph features. Inference must use predicted molecular formulas, not ground-truth composition or the target molecular graph. The benchmark-ready endpoints are raw candidate connectivity reach, ranked Exact@1/10 and matched-versus-control behavior at a fixed proposal budget. No completed all-spectrum result for the newer integrated inverse generator is asserted here.

\subsection{Future bidirectional calibration and model adaptation}
A full inverse-to-World-to-spectrum validation requires the same generated molecular candidate to be scored by a separately frozen World/Verifier, with explicit controls for missing candidate support and false-confidence amplification. Confidence-gated self-distillation on unlabeled real MS/MS and any subsequent cross-generation improvement are planned experiments rather than results of the present benchmark tables.
\methodtodo{For the final integrated system, report the completed source-frozen inverse checkpoint, exact candidate-generation budget, independent model-overlap/leakage audit, matched versus substituted-spectrum negative controls and all-spectrum de novo evaluation. Separately validate confidence thresholds before reporting unobserved compounds as structurally resolved.}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mechanistic completeness is empirical.} Elimination stringency is calibrated to a measured false-exclusion rate. Chemistry poorly covered by the fragmentation model is protected by lower reported confidence, not by a guarantee.
\item \textbf{The prospective set is not a random sample.} It contains chemistry that independent laboratories chose to characterize during the freeze interval, which over-represents tractable and biologically topical compounds.
\item \textbf{Origin attribution establishes dependence.} No biosynthetic experiment was performed here.
\item \textbf{Detectability bias.} The atlas describes molecules that ionize and fragment under the acquisition conditions represented in the corpus. It is not a census of the metabolome.
\item \textbf{Structures above the reference-standard level are hypotheses.} They are reported with calibrated confidence and are not identifications.
\item \textbf{No new measurement was acquired.} Evidence-guided acquisition, in which the system selects the next experiment and that experiment is performed, is described as a future direction and is not demonstrated.
\end{enumerate}
