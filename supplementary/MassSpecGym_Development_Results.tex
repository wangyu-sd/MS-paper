\section{Molecular composition, fragmentation evidence and connectivity recovery}
\label{sec:msdev_results}

These experiments evaluate different components of spectrum-to-structure inference on 8,401 spectra representing 1,135 molecular connectivity groups. The data are a molecule-disjoint development subset of the \emph{official MassSpecGym training partition}, repeatedly used for model development, not a held-out validation or test set. Every query, including unsuccessful predictions, contributes to case-weighted results. Bootstrap intervals resample molecular groups because individual molecules can have several measured spectra. Exact structure recovery uses the first 14 InChIKey characters and does not imply stereochemical identification.

The forward fragmentation \emph{World} ($W$) predicts fragment masses when a precursor structure is \emph{provided}; the spectrum-conditioned \emph{Verifier} ($V$) reweights fragments generated from that same known structure. In contrast, the inverse \emph{Generator} ($G$) proposes precursor molecular graphs using the measured spectrum, without the true formula or structure. Retrieval ranks existing candidates in a fixed pool, while de novo generation must first produce candidate molecules. We therefore report molecular formula recall, fragment coverage, retrieval Hit@$k$, de novo Exact@$k$ and candidate-pool reach as separate endpoints.

A \emph{matched} spectrum is the experimental spectrum corresponding to the query structure; a \emph{peer} spectrum comes from a different molecular group and is substituted while the query's precursor context and candidate identities stay fixed. The fragment-verification experiment uses an additional \emph{shuffled-spectrum} negative control. Reported matched-minus-control gaps measure sensitivity to spectral evidence rather than independent structural identification. All outcome rates are expressed as percentages (\%); differences between rates and their confidence intervals are expressed in percentage points (pp). Dataset sizes, subgroup denominators, candidate budgets and computational costs remain absolute counts.
